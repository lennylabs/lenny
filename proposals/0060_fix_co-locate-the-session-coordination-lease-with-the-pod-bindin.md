# Proposal: Co-locate the session-coordination lease with the pod binding: acquire the lease at bind and re-adopt the live pod on handoff so the coordinating replica always serves the session

- **Status:** Implemented green (2026-07-26), independently verified on reached tiers 0/1/2/4/7a/8 (changed-line coverage 87.4%); no spec edit; ready for `--no-ff` integration, not merged. Approved (2026-07-25) by jaf sign-off with a direction to implement. Verified (2026-07-25) after 12 adversarial review rounds (19 findings fixed). All open decisions ratified on sign-off: D1 (acquire the lease at bind on the binding replica) and D2 (re-adopt eagerly at takeover) resolved as staged; D3 — the production-dead barrier fresh-dial is RETIRED (deleted, not gated); D4 — the off-holder REST forwarding (`messages`/`interrupt`/`upload-to-session` over `ForwardMessage`) stays OUT of scope, owned by C-49/T-JRN.15 (this proposal establishes the invariant that makes forward-to-coordinator correct, not the forwarding wiring); the resume-path `ErrHeld` interim is a fail-closed rejection (publish no competing binding, client retries) until C-49 wires the forward hop. Section 9 records the review rounds, and section 10 the resolved decisions. The challenge-round revisions already folded into the staged changes below are that the coordination lease is acquired synchronously at bind on the binding replica while the Sweeper renews it and adopts only lapsed orphans, that on crash takeover the Sweeper re-establishes and holds the serving binding (it re-adopts the still-running pod through a fence-first re-adopt that dials the pod and sends `CoordinatorFence` as the first RPC before any version handshake, because the crash-takeover pod is already in its §10.1 hold state and rejects every other inbound RPC, and it publishes the `BindResult` to the shared `podRegistry` only after the fence acknowledges) so the reacquired pod's coordinator channel stays live and the pod does not re-enter its §10.1 hold state, while the §4.7 `Attach` content stream stays lazily opened on the first request so an idle taken-over session does not consume the single-consumer slot, and that the barrier fresh-dial reconciliation and the two spec edits are moved to Non-goals.
- **Date:** 2026-07-25.
- **Scope:** A code-first build that co-locates the §10.1 per-session coordination lease with the in-memory pod binding so the replica holding the lease is always the replica that can serve the session over the live adapter connection. The at-bind `Acquire` (through the existing `leasestore.Acquire`, `pkg/gateway/storage/leasestore/leasestore.go:129`) runs on the binding replica at every bind-and-publish site, before the session row becomes observable to a peer Sweeper as an adoptable running-pod session and before the binding is published, so the replica that holds the binding is the lease holder from bind time. `registerBinding` (`pkg/gateway/sessionserver/start.go:2737`) is the funnel for the two-step `/start` bind (`:1105`) and the snapshotless resume-rebuild bind (`:3682`), where the row is still `ready` when `registerBinding` runs and the at-bind `Acquire` inside it precedes the running-commit. The single-call `/start` (`:861`) and the delegated-child materialize (`:1034`) commit the `running` row with `pod_assignment` through `store.Create` (`:846`) and `store.Update` (`:1005`) **before** `registerBinding`, so on those two paths the at-bind `Acquire` is hoisted ahead of that commit rather than left to `registerBinding`. The checkpoint-restore resume publishes the serving binding directly through `podRegistry.Put` (`:3745`) without calling `registerBinding`, so that fifth publish site routes through the same at-bind `Acquire` before its `Put`. Because `leasestore.Acquire` is idempotent for the same holder (`leasestore.go:100-104`), a later `registerBinding` on a path whose `Acquire` was already hoisted self-renews harmlessly. On `ErrHeld` (a live foreign holder, reachable on the resume paths) the bind publishes nothing, releases the pod, and returns the error so the caller fails the bind closed. The Sweeper's session-lease `Acquire` (`pkg/gateway/coordination/coordination/coordination.go:133`) then renews the lease for the sessions this replica binds or already holds the lease for, and adopts only a lapsed lease of a still-running-pod session (state `running` or `input_required` with a persisted pod assignment) it holds no binding for (the pre-`Acquire` `Get` returns `leasestore.ErrNotFound` at `:127-131`); it does not acquire the lease of a `created`, `finalizing`, `ready`, or `suspended` session, and a session whose lease a live foreign replica holds is skipped on `ErrHeld` (`coordination.go:134`). On the crash-takeover edge (a successful `Acquire` that changed the holder to this replica for an adoptable session it holds no binding for), it bumps `coordination_generation` and re-establishes the serving binding eagerly on the same replica: the Sweeper re-adopts the still-running pod through a fence-first re-adopt entry point that dials the pod from its persisted `SandboxName` and sends `CoordinatorFence` as the first RPC over that connection (generalizing the resume re-bind fence trigger, `cmd/lenny-gateway/stores.go:1371`, `pkg/gateway/sessionserver/start.go:3914`, to the crash-takeover trigger) before any §15.5 version handshake, because a crash-takeover pod is already in its §10.1 hold state and rejects every inbound RPC except `CoordinatorFence` (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:49`); the existing `Binder.reconnect` seam (`pkg/gateway/podlifecycle/podsession/binder.go:1098`) runs `NegotiateVersion` before any fence (`binder.go:1114`) and so cannot be reused unmodified on this path. Only on the fence acknowledgement does the Sweeper publish the `BindResult` to the shared `podRegistry` and hold the connection open as the session's binding. Publishing after the acknowledgement honors the §10.1 precondition that no operational RPC reaches the pod until the fence acknowledges (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:34`), because the executor opens its first `Attach` only over a published binding. Because the takeover replica holds the live gateway-to-pod gRPC channel from the re-adopt onward, the pod observes no coordinator-connection loss and never re-enters the §10.1 hold state that would self-terminate an idle session (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:47,50`). The §4.7 `Attach` content stream stays lazily opened on the first `Send` (`pkg/gateway/session/executor/pod.go:35-37,154`), so the held binding does not consume the single-consumer slot for an idle taken-over session. The executor's serving path is unchanged and serves the taken-over session from the published binding at `registry.Get` (`pkg/gateway/session/executor/pod.go:134`); a request landing in the narrow window before the takeover publishes hits the pre-existing fail-closed error and retries, bounded by the fence completion. C1 adds only a stream-eviction method to the executor, called on a dead-connection eviction so the cached Attach stream does not shadow a same-replica re-adopt; it does not change the serving path. No spec edit is required: §10.1 already states the coordinator handoff re-adopts the still-running pod without a checkpoint restore (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:180`) and §4.6.1 already defines the coordinating replica as the lease holder driving the pod over its held connection (`spec/04_system-components.md:489`). The off-holder REST forwarding for `messages`, `interrupt`, and `upload-to-session` over the §10.1 `ForwardMessage` transport is separately owned by cluster C-49 / finding T-JRN.15 (`PROPOSAL-QUEUE.md:535`) and is left to that proposal; this proposal establishes the invariant that makes forward-to-coordinator correct without wiring the transport.

This document stages the proposed code and test changes. It does not modify any spec, code, or doc file. Apply the changes in the "Proposed changes" section after sign-off.

## 1. Problem

The gateway's §10.1 per-session coordination model decouples two roles the spec assumes are unified: the in-memory pod binding that carries the live adapter connection, and the distributed coordination lease that names the coordinating replica.

The in-memory pod **binding** is a per-replica `podsession.Registry` (`pkg/gateway/podlifecycle/podsession/registry.go:14-17`) holding the live, un-shareable `*adapterclient.Client` gRPC connection (`binder.go:531-533`; `adapterclient/client.go:28`). It gates live serving: the pod executor returns `session %s is not bound to a pod` unless `registry.Get` succeeds (`pkg/gateway/session/executor/pod.go:134-136`), and the §4.7 adapter admits a single content consumer per session (`pod.go:35-37`).

The coordination **lease** is a distributed per-session record (`leasestore.go:90-92`, key `t:{tenant}:lease:session:{id}`) acquired lazily and exclusively by the background Sweeper. The only session-lease `Acquire` is `coordination.go:133`, one call per non-terminal session per sweep, `ErrHeld` maps to skip (`coordination.go:134-137`). The bind path never touches the leasestore: `registerBinding` (`start.go:2737-2757`) does `podRegistry.Put` plus `persistPodAssignment` and nothing lease-related, and the entire `sessionserver` package holds no `leasestore` reference. A cross-replica handoff runs only `RecordHandoff` (bump `coordination_generation`) plus `upsertMirror` (`coordination.go:144-150`). The coordination package imports neither `adapterclient` nor `podsession`, so `Sweep` cannot re-dial the pod or re-establish the binding.

The registry's own doc comment already asserts the intended model: "a coordinator handoff re-establishes the binding on the new coordinating replica" (`registry.go:12-13`), and the leasestore doc asserts "a replica acquires the lease before driving a session" (`leasestore.go:5-8`). Neither seam is wired.

Because the two roles can land on different replicas, an operation that must reach the live session breaks when it lands on a lease holder that lacks the binding. `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/messages` returns 500 `EXECUTOR_FAILURE` through the unbound executor (`messages.go:553`), `upload-to-session` returns 409 `TARGET_NOT_READY` whose own comment tells the client to "retry against the coordinating replica" (`upload_to_session.go:108-119`) even though under decoupling the coordinating replica may itself lack the binding, and interrupt fails open (it flips the row to `suspended` without signaling the runtime, `interrupt.go`).

The spec assumes unification. §7.2 forwards a `delivery: "immediate"` message to the session's coordinator and has the coordinator serve (`spec/07_session-lifecycle.md:330`). §4.6.1 states the coordinating replica is "the replica holding the session-coordination lease" and drives the eviction checkpoint through the `Checkpoint` RPC "under its held lease" (`spec/04_system-components.md:489`), which presupposes the lease holder holds the live connection that RPC needs. §10.1 already states that a coordinator handoff "re-adopts the still-running pod without a checkpoint restore" (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:180`) and that the new coordinator sends `CoordinatorFence` to the pod before any RPC (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:34`), and the pod's hold state "accepts `CoordinatorFence` RPCs from a new coordinator" as the only exit (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:49`). The connection following the lease is the spec's model; the §10.1 handoff protocol specifies the generation fence that guards it but does not itself specify the acquire-at-bind or re-establish-binding mechanism that makes the lease holder the replica that can serve. The gap is in code.

This proposal firms up the co-located model the spec already assumes. It scopes the lease acquire to the replica that holds the binding, re-establishes the binding on the new coordinator at handoff by re-adopting the still-running pod (re-dial, no checkpoint restore), and fences the reacquired pod, so the binding and the lease stay on the same replica across the session lifecycle. The off-holder REST forwarding that a fully co-located model still needs when a request first lands on a non-coordinator replica is finding T-JRN.15 in cluster C-49 (`PROPOSAL-QUEUE.md:535`) and is out of scope here.

## 2. Decisions

- **Co-location keeps the lease and the binding on one replica.** The binding holds an un-shareable live gRPC connection and the adapter admits a single content consumer per session (`pod.go:35-37`), so the invariant cannot be met by sharing the binding across replicas. It is met by keeping the lease and the binding on the same replica: the lease is acquired by the replica that holds the binding, and the binding is re-established on the new lease holder at handoff. The single-consumer Attach constraint fits this model, because only the coordinator ever attaches.
- **Re-adopt the still-running pod on handoff; do not restore from a checkpoint.** Proposal 0026 removed the resume-dedup machinery on the finding that a coordinator handoff re-adopts the still-running pod without a checkpoint restore and replays nothing, and §7.3 carves the at-least-once guarantee out for exactly this case (`spec/07_session-lifecycle.md:419`). Re-dialing a still-running pod re-establishes the gRPC connection the `CoordinatorFence` and begin-coordination path needs; it replays nothing, so it is distinct from a checkpoint restore and does not resurrect the removed `ResumeDedup`/`tool_call_sequence` machinery. The re-adopt reuses `Binder.reconnect`'s sandbox resolution and adapter dial (`binder.go:1105-1112`) but not its ordering: `reconnect` runs the §15.5 version handshake (`NegotiateVersion`, `binder.go:1114`) before any fence, which a hold-state pod rejects, so the takeover drives a fence-first re-adopt that dials and sends `CoordinatorFence` before the version handshake.
- **No forced-acquisition primitive is needed, so the leasestore holder invariant is preserved.** A non-holder cannot acquire a still-live lease: `leasestore.Acquire` returns `ErrHeld` and no forced-acquisition entry point exists (`leasestore.go:98-123`). Co-location reframes rather than violates that; it changes which replica acquires the lease and when. On the session-start path the lease is unheld, so the binding replica's acquire succeeds. On the crash-takeover path the prior holder's lease has already lapsed on its TTL, so the Sweeper's acquire succeeds and the takeover then re-adopts the pod. A resume request landing on a non-holder while a live holder still coordinates is the forward-to-coordinator case whose transport is deferred to C-49; it never forces a steal.
- **The coordination lease-holder invariant and tenant isolation are not weakened.** `Acquire`, `Renew`, and `Release` remain holder-checked and Lua-atomic (`leasestore.go:98-123`); the lease key stays tenant-scoped; the only new lease behavior is scoping which sessions this replica acquires, plus the crash-takeover fence, without any new lease write primitive.
- **Establish the invariant and the at-bind acquire / Sweeper-scoping / re-adopt-on-handoff mechanism here; leave the off-holder message-subsystem wiring to its own tracked proposal.** Co-location establishes the invariant (the coordinator is the replica that can serve) that makes "forward to the coordinator" correct. Wiring the off-holder REST forwarding for `messages`, `interrupt`, and `upload-to-session` over `ForwardMessage` is separately owned by C-49 / T-JRN.15 and is out of scope here.
- **Acquire the lease at bind on the binding replica, and reconcile the Sweeper into a bind-scoped renewer with crash takeover rather than a lazy universal acquirer.** The lazy per-non-terminal-session acquire (`coordination.go:133`) is what let the lease land on a replica that holds no binding. Acquiring the lease synchronously at bind, before the row becomes an adoptable running-pod session and before the binding is published, makes the binding replica the holder from bind time. The `Acquire` runs inside `registerBinding` on the two-step `/start` (`:1105`) and snapshotless resume (`:3682`) paths, where the row is still `ready` at that point, and is hoisted ahead of the running-commit on the single-call `/start` (before `store.Create` at `:846`) and the delegated-child materialize (before `store.Update` at `:1005`), and ahead of the direct `podRegistry.Put` on the checkpoint-restore resume (`:3745`), because those three paths reach the adoptable running-pod state before or without `registerBinding`. The Sweeper then renews the lease for the sessions this replica binds or already holds the lease for, and adopts only a lapsed lease of a still-running-pod session (state `running` or `input_required` with a persisted pod assignment); it does not acquire the lease of any `created`, `finalizing`, `ready`, or `suspended` session, so a session that is committed but not yet bound (a two-step `/start`, a delegated-child materialize, or a suspended session between suspend and resume) is never picked up by a peer sweep before its owning replica's at-bind `Acquire` runs. Deferring the initial acquire to the Sweeper alone would not suffice: it leaves a window of up to one sweep interval (default 15s, `coordination.go:71`) in which a fresh running session's lease is unheld, and a peer replica whose sweep fires first in that window would read the unheld lease as adoptable and acquire it, reproducing the exact lease-without-binding decoupling this proposal removes. The at-bind acquire closes that window, and its incremental cost is a single Redis `SET` on a path that already performs a synchronous Postgres write (`persistPodAssignment`, `start.go:2742`), negligible against the §6.3 pod-warm budget dominated by pod claim, credential assignment, and agent session start.

## 3. The co-located model after the change

Under the change the lease and the binding are on the same replica across three phases.

- **Session start.** The at-bind `Acquire` runs before the session row becomes an adoptable running-pod session and before the binding is published. On the two-step `/start` (`:1105`) and snapshotless resume (`:3682`) paths the row is still `ready` when `registerBinding` runs, so the `Acquire` inside `registerBinding` precedes the running-commit (`store.Update`/`transitionStart` at `:1126`). On the single-call `/start` and the delegated-child materialize the row is committed to `running` with `pod_assignment` by `store.Create` (`:846`) and `store.Update` (`:1005`) before `registerBinding`, so the `Acquire` is hoisted ahead of that commit; if it were left to `registerBinding` a peer Sweeper firing in the commit-to-`registerBinding` window would read the committed running-pod row with an unheld lease as adoptable and steal it. The Sweeper adopts only still-running-pod sessions and never acquires the lease of a `created`, `finalizing`, `ready`, or `suspended` row, so no peer sweep touches a session before it reaches `running`; the hoisted at-bind `Acquire` closes the window between the running-commit and the binding publish on the two early-commit paths, and the binding replica is the lease holder before its row is adoptable. Once the lease is held a peer replica's Sweeper reads it and skips the session on `ErrHeld` (`coordination.go:134`). Serving already works from t=0 through the local binding (`pod.go:134`), and the §10.1 generation fence, validated on every gateway-to-pod RPC (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:28`), guards split-brain independently of who holds the lease. The at-bind acquire adds one Redis `SET` to a warm-start path that already writes `pod_assignment` to Postgres (`start.go:2742`), negligible against the §6.3 pod-warm budget.
- **Steady state.** The Sweeper renews the lease for the sessions this replica holds a binding for (enumerated from `podRegistry`) and for the sessions it already holds the lease for after a crash takeover whose binding it has not yet published (`priorHolder == s.replicaID`, the narrow window before the takeover re-adopt calls `podRegistry.Put`), and it adopts a lapsed lease only for a still-running-pod session (`running` or `input_required` with a persisted pod assignment) it holds no binding for. A replica that holds no binding for a live session whose lease is still held skips it on `ErrHeld` (`coordination.go:134`) and never acquires it, so the lease and the binding stay co-located outside the crash-takeover re-adopt window. The per-binding renew is gated on the held gRPC channel's liveness: before renewing a bound session's lease the Sweeper probes the binding's channel state, and on a failed channel it evicts the binding from `podRegistry`, evicts the executor's cached Attach stream for the session, and releases the lease rather than renewing it, so a dead held connection surfaces the lease for re-adoption instead of pinning it to a replica that can no longer reach the pod. The executor eviction is required because `streamFor` reads its own `e.streams` cache before `podRegistry` (`pod.go:131` precedes `pod.go:134`) and drops a cached stream only on teardown in `Release` (`pod.go:307-309`); a `podRegistry` eviction alone would leave the stale dead stream cached, so a re-adopt on the same replica would keep serving over the dead stream instead of Attaching over the freshly published binding.
- **Cross-replica handoff (crash takeover).** When a replica crashes, its Redis lease key expires on its TTL and is deleted, so the pre-`Acquire` `Get` on another replica's Sweeper returns `leasestore.ErrNotFound` and the subsequent `Acquire` succeeds on the now-unheld lease (`coordination.go:127-133`). Because this replica holds no local binding for that still-running-pod session and was not the prior holder, the Sweeper treats the successful acquire as a crash takeover: it bumps `coordination_generation`, re-adopts the still-running pod through a fence-first re-adopt that dials the pod from its persisted `SandboxName` and sends `CoordinatorFence` as the first RPC over that connection (the §10.1 begin-coordination precondition, `spec/10_gateway-internals.md:34`) before any version handshake, because the crash-takeover pod has been in its §10.1 hold state since roughly one keepalive interval after the coordinator crash (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:47`) whereas the takeover fires only after the lease TTL lapses at four sweep intervals (`coordination.go:71,75`), and a hold-state pod rejects every inbound RPC except `CoordinatorFence` (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:49`) so `Binder.reconnect`'s pre-fence `NegotiateVersion` (`binder.go:1114`) would be rejected; only on the fence acknowledgement does it publish the re-established `BindResult` to the shared `podRegistry` and holds the connection open as the session's serving binding. Publishing after the acknowledgement honors the §10.1 precondition that the new coordinator sends no operational RPC until the fence returns a successful acknowledgement (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:34`), because the executor opens its first `Attach` only over a published binding. Holding the live gateway-to-pod gRPC channel from the re-adopt onward is what keeps the pod out of hold state: the pod enters hold state only on connection loss with no active coordinator (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:47`), so a takeover that establishes and retains the connection leaves the pod continuously coordinated and it never restarts its 120-second hold-state self-termination timer (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:50`). Publishing the `BindResult` lets a request that lands on the takeover replica serve from `registry.Get` (`pod.go:134`); the §4.7 `Attach` content stream stays lazily opened on the first `Send` (`pod.go:35-37,154`), so an idle taken-over session holds only the gRPC connection and not the single-consumer Attach slot. The takeover predicate is a successful `Acquire` that changed the holder to this replica for a still-running-pod session it does not bind (`acquired && !boundHere(row.ID) && priorHolder != s.replicaID`); the `priorHolder != s.replicaID` term keeps the bump and the re-adopt to once per handoff, because the next sweep finds the lease self-held and the binding published (`boundHere` now true) and renews it without re-bumping or re-adopting. In the narrow window between the successful `Acquire` and the `podRegistry.Put`, the taken-over lease keeps being renewed on the `priorHolder == s.replicaID` renew case, so it does not lapse and re-orphan the session. When the takeover fence instead fails all retries, the background takeover path relinquishes the lease exactly as the resume-path fence does (`coordfence.go:196-201`), per the §10.1 mandate that a new coordinator whose `CoordinatorFence` exhausts its retry budget stops extending the lease, releases the lock, and backs off with a jittered delay before reconsidering coordination (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:35`). The generation increment stays in Postgres, and the next coordinator to acquire the lease increments it again. Relinquishing is the recovery path the spec relies on: it surfaces the lapsed lease so a healthier, better-connected replica (or this replica after its backoff) can acquire it and fence the pod, rather than pinning the pod to a coordinator that cannot reach it (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:26`, `spec/04_system-components.md:489`). To keep the fixed sweep interval (`coordination.go:71`) from re-adopting inside the spec's backoff window, the Sweeper records a per-session adoption backoff after a relinquished takeover and skips re-adoption of that session until it elapses. Because the takeover re-establishes the serving binding eagerly, the executor's serving path is unchanged: it serves the taken-over session from the published binding at `registry.Get`, and re-adopting the still-running pod without a checkpoint restore (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:180`) happens once at takeover rather than lazily per request. A request landing in the narrow window before the takeover publishes hits the pre-existing fail-closed error (`pod.go:135`) and retries, bounded by the fence completion. A durable loss of the held connection after takeover is a fresh coordinator loss that the pod detects on its own gRPC keepalive (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:47`). The owning replica must detect the same failure, because `boundHere` is pure `podRegistry` map presence that no code evicts on connection death (`registry.go:33-38`; `Remove` is teardown-only and `streamFor` does not evict on an `Attach` error, `pod.go:154-157`), so a dead-connection binding would otherwise keep `boundHere` true and renew the lease forever on the `held` branch, leaving no lapsed lease for any peer's `adoptable` predicate to fire on and letting the pod self-terminate in hold state at 120s. The Sweeper therefore probes the held binding's gRPC channel state before renewing a bound session's lease: on a failed channel it evicts the binding from `podRegistry`, evicts the executor's cached Attach stream for the session, and releases the lease, reverting the session to a lapsed-lease still-running-pod orphan that the next Sweeper acquisition (a peer, or this replica) re-adopts and re-fences through the same fence-first re-adopt, re-establishing the binding again. Evicting the executor's cached stream is what lets the re-adopt on this same replica restore serving: `streamFor` consults its `e.streams` cache (`pod.go:131`) before `podRegistry` (`pod.go:134`) and drops a cached stream only in `Release` (`pod.go:307-309`), never on connection death, on a `podRegistry` eviction, or on a `Send` error (`pod.go:112-113` returns the error and leaves the entry cached), so without the companion executor eviction the same-replica re-adopt would republish a fresh `BindResult` that `streamFor` never reaches, keep serving over the stale dead cached stream, and never Attach over the new binding. A peer replica re-adopts with a fresh executor whose stream cache is empty and is unaffected; the companion eviction makes the same-replica path recover identically rather than leaving recovery contingent on which replica happens to acquire the lapsed lease. This is the same relinquish-to-recover mechanism the terminal-fence-failure path uses (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:26`, `spec/04_system-components.md:489`), applied to a dead held connection.

A request that first lands on a replica that does not hold the lease is the forward-to-coordinator case (`spec/07_session-lifecycle.md:330`). Co-location makes that forward correct, because the coordinator can now always serve. The `ForwardMessage` transport itself is C-49 / T-JRN.15 and is not wired here; until it lands, a resume request landing on a non-holder while a live holder still coordinates fails closed (it publishes no competing binding) rather than double-binding.

## 5. Proposed changes

### C1. Acquire the coordination lease at bind on the binding replica, and scope the Sweeper to renew bound sessions and adopt only lapsed orphans

**Target:** `pkg/gateway/sessionserver/start.go`: `registerBinding` (`:2737-2757`), the funnel for four bind paths (`:861`, `:1034`, `:1105`, `:3682`), and the checkpoint-restore resume publish (`s.podRegistry.Put` at `:3745`) that bypasses `registerBinding`, plus a `leasestore.LeaseStore` handle and the `replicaID` injected into the `Server`. The at-bind `Acquire` is factored into a helper that `registerBinding`, the two running-committed-before-`registerBinding` paths (single-call `/start`, delegated-child), and the checkpoint-restore publish all call. `pkg/gateway/coordination/coordination/coordination.go`: the `Sweep` acquire loop (`:113-152`), the `Options`/`Sweeper` struct (`:36-64`), and `NewSweeper` (`:68`). `pkg/gateway/session/executor/pod.go`: add an exported stream-eviction method (`CloseSend` plus `delete` on `e.streams[sessionID]`, mirroring the teardown in `Release` at `:307-309`) that the Sweeper's `evictBinding` seam calls on a dead-connection eviction, so the executor's `e.streams` cache does not shadow a same-replica re-adopt (`streamFor` reads `e.streams` at `:131` before `podRegistry` at `:134`). `cmd/lenny-gateway/stores.go`: the `Server` construction, to inject the `leasestore` already built there, and the `NewSweeper` call (`:1467`), to inject the `podRegistry` binding enumerator and the `evictBinding` seam wired over `podRegistry` and the `PodExecutor`.

**Rationale:** The lazy per-non-terminal-session acquire at `coordination.go:133` is one source of the decoupling: it lets any replica's Sweeper acquire a live session's lease regardless of which replica holds the binding. Two corrections together close the decoupling.

First, scope the Sweeper so it never acquires a lease for a session no replica has ever bound. The bind lifecycle is decomposed across `/create`, `/finalize`, `/start`, the delegated-child materialize, and resume, so a committed session row sits enumerable by the Sweeper in a non-terminal, never-bound state for an unbounded interval before its owning replica binds it: the two-step `/start` row is fetched in `ready` (`start.go:1063`) and bound only at `:1105`, the delegated child is committed in `session.StateCreated` (`:890`) and bound only at `:1034`, and a suspended session is unbound and unleased between suspend and resume. The Sweeper enumerates every non-terminal session of every tenant with no replica sharding (`coordination.go:108-113`, `session.IsTerminal` treats `created`, `finalizing`, `ready`, `starting`, and `suspended` as non-terminal), so if it acquired the unheld lease of such a row it would both land the lease on a replica that holds no binding and misclassify a never-served session as a crash takeover. The Sweeper therefore adopts an unheld or lapsed lease it holds no binding for only when the session is in a still-running-pod state (`running` or its `input_required` sub-state) and carries a persisted pod assignment (`pod_assignment`, `start.go:2742`); it renews the sessions it binds or already holds the lease for; and it skips every other non-terminal session (`created`, `finalizing`, `ready`, `suspended`, and any session whose lease a live foreign replica holds) without an `Acquire` attempt.

Second, acquire the lease synchronously at bind, on the binding replica, so a running session's lease is held before the row is observable as an adoptable running-pod session. The at-bind `Acquire` must precede the store write that commits the row to `running` with `pod_assignment` and precede the binding publish. On the two-step `/start` (`:1105`) and snapshotless resume (`:3682`) paths the row is still `ready` when `registerBinding` runs (the transition to `running` follows at `:1126` / on the resume caller), so the `Acquire` as the first step of `registerBinding` before `podRegistry.Put` and `persistPodAssignment` suffices. On the single-call `/start` and the delegated-child materialize the row reaches `running` with `pod_assignment` through `store.Create` (`:846`) and `store.Update` (`:1005`) **before** `registerBinding` at `:861` and `:1034`, so on those two paths the `Acquire` is hoisted to run before that commit; leaving it to `registerBinding` would expose a commit-to-`registerBinding` window in which a peer Sweeper reads the committed running-pod row with an unheld lease as adoptable, acquires it, misclassifies it as a crash takeover, bumps `coordination_generation`, and fences a pod that is still being launched, while the owning replica's later `Acquire` returns `ErrHeld` and must abort a session whose running row and token were already persisted. On the checkpoint-restore resume the `Acquire` is hoisted before the direct `podRegistry.Put` (`:3745`) for the same reason. Because `leasestore.Acquire` is idempotent for the same holder (`leasestore.go:100-104`), a subsequent `registerBinding` on a path whose `Acquire` was hoisted self-renews and does not re-`ErrHeld`. The lease gate is real: the binding replica publishes and persists only after it holds the lease, and on `ErrHeld` it publishes nothing and returns the error to abort the bind. Because the Sweeper no longer acquires never-bound sessions, the lease of a session between `/create` and `/start` (or between suspend and resume) stays unheld until its owning replica's at-bind `Acquire` runs, so no peer sweep grabs it first. The resume paths (`:3682`, `:3745`) can still meet a live foreign holder coordinating the session, in which case `ErrHeld` fails the bind closed rather than double-binding (section 4). The incremental cost is a single Redis `SET` on a path that already performs a synchronous Postgres write, negligible against the §6.3 pod-warm budget dominated by pod claim, credential assignment, and agent session start.

**Anchor (acquire-at-bind).** Factor the at-bind `Acquire` into a helper (`acquireCoordinationLease(ctx, tenantID, sessionID)` calling `leasestore.Acquire(ctx, tenantID, sessionID, replicaID, ttl)` on the injected leasestore) so the same canonical acquire runs at every bind-and-publish site, and place it so it precedes both the store write that commits the row to a running-pod state and the binding publish. Change `registerBinding` (`start.go:2737`) from a void call into one that returns an `error` and call the helper as its first step, **before** `podRegistry.Put` and `persistPodAssignment` (`start.go:2741-2742`), so the lease gate cannot be bypassed by an already-published binding. On success, publish and persist as today and return nil. On `ErrHeld` (a live foreign holder still coordinates), do not publish and do not persist: return the error so the caller fails the bind closed, matching the forward-to-coordinator interim behavior in section 4 until C-49 wires the forward hop. The five bind-and-publish sites resolve as follows.

- Two-step `/start` (`:1105`) and snapshotless resume-rebuild (`:3682`): the row is still `ready` when `registerBinding` runs, so the helper inside `registerBinding` already precedes the running-commit. `:1105` returns the bind error to its handler; `:3682` (`resumeOnPod`) skips `fenceResumedPod`, returns the error, and releases the fresh pod claim `startOnPod` made (`:3678`) through `rollbackBinding` (`start.go:2906`, `s.podBinder.Release`), the same seam the `/start` funnels already use (`:851`, `:1022`).
- Single-call `/start` (`:861`) and delegated-child materialize (`:1034`): the row is committed to `running` with `pod_assignment` by `store.Create` (`:846`) and `store.Update` (`:1005`) before `registerBinding`, so call the helper before those commits. On `ErrHeld` there (unreachable for a genuinely fresh single-call session whose lease is unheld, but a defensive fail-closed against a raced peer) abort before the commit and release the bound pod through `rollbackBinding`; on success the running-commit and the later `registerBinding` self-renew the already-held lease. Both handlers return the bind error rather than ignoring it.
- Checkpoint-restore resume (`:3745`): `resumeOnPod` publishes the serving binding directly through `s.podRegistry.Put(result.Result)` without calling `registerBinding`, and `bumpRecoveryGeneration` (`:3750`) then persists the running-state `pod_assignment`. Call the helper before the `Put`; on `ErrHeld` (a non-holder resume landing on a session a live foreign holder still coordinates, which `handleResume` at `:3170-3219` does not otherwise gate) skip `podRegistry.Put`, `bumpRecoveryGeneration`, and `fenceResumedPod`, release the restored pod through the resume release seam, and return the error so the restore fails closed.

Carry `// spec: 4.6.1 (coordinating replica holds the lease), 10.1 (per-session coordination lease)`.

**Anchor (Sweeper scoping).** In `Sweep` (`coordination.go:113-152`), gate the per-row `Acquire` (`:133`) so this replica attempts `Acquire` only in one of three cases: it holds a binding for the session (`held := boundHere(row.ID)`, renew), it is already the current lease holder (`priorHolder == s.replicaID` from the pre-`Acquire` `Get` at `:127-131`, renew a taken-over lease whose binding it has not yet published), or the session's lease is unheld or lapsed (`leasestore.ErrNotFound`) and the session is a still-running-pod session eligible for adoption (state `running` or `input_required` and a non-empty persisted pod assignment). A session whose lease a live different replica holds is skipped on `ErrHeld` (`:134-137`) as today; a `created`, `finalizing`, `ready`, or `suspended` session, and any session this replica neither binds, already holds, nor finds adoptable, is skipped without an `Acquire` attempt. Renewing when `priorHolder == s.replicaID` is required for correctness: in the narrow window after a crash takeover acquires the lease but before its re-adopt publishes the binding (section C2), this replica holds the lease with no local binding, so a gate of `held || leaseUnheld` alone would skip the renewal, let the taken-over lease lapse on its TTL, and re-orphan the session.

**Change (staged description).** Inject a binding predicate into the `Sweeper` (a small consumer-side interface sourced from `podRegistry.Get` and defined in the coordination package so the package does not import `podsession` broadly, exposing `boundHere(sessionID) bool`, a companion `connAlive(sessionID) bool` that reports the bound `BindResult`'s gRPC channel state, and an `evictBinding(sessionID)` method that removes the binding from `podRegistry` and drops the executor's cached Attach stream for the session in one call). In the acquire loop, compute `held := boundHere(row.ID)`, `leaseUnheld := errors.Is(getErr, leasestore.ErrNotFound)`, and `adoptable := leaseUnheld && isRunningPod(row)` where `isRunningPod` is true for a `running` or `input_required` session carrying a non-empty persisted pod assignment. When `held && !connAlive(row.ID)` (a bound session whose held gRPC channel has failed), call `evictBinding(row.ID)` and `Release` the lease rather than renewing it, so the session reverts to a lapsed-lease orphan a subsequent sweep re-adopts and re-fences. The `evictBinding` seam must drop the executor's cached stream as well as the `podRegistry` entry, because `streamFor` reads `e.streams` before `podRegistry` (`pod.go:131` precedes `:134`) and evicts a cached stream only in `Release` (`pod.go:307-309`), so a `podRegistry`-only eviction would let a same-replica re-adopt keep serving over the stale dead cached stream instead of Attaching over the freshly published binding; the executor exposes an eviction method (`CloseSend` plus `delete` on `e.streams[sessionID]`, mirroring the teardown Release already performs) that the seam calls. Otherwise attempt `Acquire` only when `held || priorHolder == s.replicaID || adoptable`, and `continue` on any other session. The crash-takeover edge is a successful `Acquire` that changed the holder to this replica for a session it does not bind, that is `acquired && !held && priorHolder != s.replicaID`, so route it through the C2 takeover handling (bump `coordination_generation`, drive the `CoordinatorFence`) rather than the mirror upsert alone. Anchoring the takeover on the holder-change edge (rather than on the per-sweep level condition `acquired && !held`) fires the generation bump and the fence once per handoff: on the next sweep the lease is self-held (`priorHolder == s.replicaID`), so the renew runs without a second bump or fence. The edge does not depend on the pre-`Acquire` `Get` observing the departed holder, because a lapsed Redis lease leaves `priorHolder` empty and the `!held` adoption of an `adoptable` session is itself the handoff signal. Keep the mirror upsert (`:150`) on every acquired session. Carry `// spec: 10.1 (per-session coordination lease), 4.6.1 (coordinating replica holds the lease)`.

**Anchor (resume-path fail-closed on `ErrHeld`).** `resumeOnPod` has two mutually-exclusive publish branches, and both must fail closed on `ErrHeld` because `handleResume` (`start.go:3170-3219`) applies no upstream holder gate. The snapshotless resume-rebuild branch (`:3670-3693`) establishes coordination through `fenceResumedPod`'s generation bump (`:3690`), which runs only after `registerBinding` (`:3682`); with the at-bind acquire now the first step of the error-returning `registerBinding`, a resume that meets a live foreign holder returns `ErrHeld`, `resumeOnPod` returns that error before `fenceResumedPod`, publishes no competing binding, and releases the fresh pod claim through `rollbackBinding`. The checkpoint-restore branch (`:3694-3758`, the standard §7.3 pod-restore recovery) publishes directly through `podRegistry.Put` (`:3745`) and then runs `bumpRecoveryGeneration` (`:3750`) and `fenceResumedPod` (`:3755`); the hoisted helper `Acquire` runs before the `Put`, and on `ErrHeld` the branch skips the `Put`, the generation bump, and the fence, releases the restored pod, and returns the error, so a non-holder resume that takes the snapshot branch does not double-bind a session a live foreign holder still coordinates. That is the interim forward-to-coordinator behavior recorded in section 4, usable in full only once C-49 wires the forward hop. A synchronous holder check separate from the at-bind acquire is therefore unnecessary on either branch; the at-bind `Acquire` is the holder check, and D1 is resolved to keep it.

### C2. Re-establish and hold the serving binding on the Sweeper's crash-takeover edge and fence the reacquired pod over it; retire the barrier fresh-dial fallback

**Target:** `pkg/gateway/coordination/coordination/coordination.go`: the crash-takeover path in the `Sweep` acquire loop (`:133-152`, a successful `Acquire` on a session this replica does not bind) and the `Sweeper` wiring (`:36-64`, `:68`), which gains a consumer-side re-adopt seam (a small interface, e.g. `ReadoptAndFence(ctx, tenantID, sessionID, generation) (publish func(), error)`, whose production implementation dials the pod from its persisted `SandboxName` and sends `CoordinatorFence` as the first RPC before any version handshake, then returns a `publish` callback the Sweeper invokes only after the fence acknowledges to run `podRegistry.Put`) so the coordination package still imports neither `adapterclient` nor `podsession` directly. The executor (`pkg/gateway/session/executor/pod.go`) is unchanged by C2: it serves the taken-over session from the eagerly published binding at `registry.Get` and needs no re-adopt path of its own (C1 adds its dead-connection stream-eviction method). `pkg/gateway/coordination/barrier/wiring.go`: the `Dial` fallback (`:67-80`). `cmd/lenny-gateway/stores.go`: the `NewSweeper` call (`:1467`), where the re-adopt seam is wired over the `Binder` and `podRegistry` already built there, and the barrier `PodDispatcher` wiring.

**Rationale:** Today `Sweep`'s handoff branch bumps the generation and the mirror only (`:144-150`); it re-establishes no connection and no binding, and the coordination package imports neither `adapterclient` nor `podsession`. Two seams are missing, and they are the same seam. First, nothing fences the reacquired pod on crash takeover, so a pod whose coordinator crashed self-terminates at `coordinatorHoldTimeoutSeconds` (120s) even though a new coordinator now holds the lease (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:50`); driving `CoordinatorFence` on the takeover edge is the genuine missing §10.1 begin-coordination step (`:34`). Second, the serving binding is never re-published on the new coordinator, so a subsequent request hits the `registry.Get` miss (`pod.go:135`). A fence over a connection that is then closed does not durably keep the pod out of hold state: the pod enters hold state on connection loss with no active coordinator (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:47`), so a takeover replica that fences and disconnects leaves the reacquired pod to re-enter hold state within one keepalive interval (~15s) and self-terminate an idle session at 120s. The takeover therefore re-adopts the pod through a fence-first re-adopt (dialing over `Binder.reconnect`'s sandbox resolution and adapter dial, then sending `CoordinatorFence` before any version handshake, because a hold-state pod rejects the pre-fence `NegotiateVersion` that unmodified `reconnect` runs), publishes the `BindResult` to the shared `podRegistry` on the fence acknowledgement, and holds the connection open as the session's serving binding. The one re-adopt seam thus supplies both the fence connection and the durable serving binding, keeping one canonical "reach a still-running pod without a local binding" path (the fence-first re-adopt over `Binder.reconnect`'s dial, driven at takeover) instead of the three that exist today (the executor miss, the barrier `Dial` fallback, and a transient Sweeper dial-and-close), and it lets the executor's `registry.Get`-miss stay a plain fail-closed error rather than a second re-adopt entry point.

**Anchor (crash-takeover re-adopt and fence).** On the crash-takeover edge — a successful `Acquire` that changed the holder to this replica for a still-running-pod session it does not bind (`acquired && !boundHere(row.ID) && priorHolder != s.replicaID`, with the session gated to `running` or `input_required` and a persisted pod assignment by the C1 `adoptable` predicate) — bump `coordination_generation` through `RecordHandoff` (`coordination.go:168`) and then re-adopt and fence over one held connection: resolve the pod from the persisted `SandboxName` and dial its adapter (reusing `Binder.reconnect`'s `resolveSandbox` + `DialAdapter`, `binder.go:1105-1112`, without opening an `Attach` stream), then send `CoordinatorFence(session_id, new_generation)` as the first RPC over that connection with the §10.1 5-second deadline and its 3-attempt retry (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:34-35`) before any §15.5 version handshake, because the crash-takeover pod is already in its §10.1 hold state and rejects every inbound RPC except `CoordinatorFence` (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:49`); `Binder.reconnect` unmodified runs `NegotiateVersion` before any fence (`binder.go:1114`) and so cannot fence a hold-state pod, which is why the takeover uses a fence-first re-adopt rather than the resume path's negotiate-then-serve `reconnect`. Only on a successful fence acknowledgement does it publish the re-established `BindResult` to the shared `podRegistry` and hold the connection open as the session's serving binding. Publishing after the acknowledgement honors the §10.1 hard precondition that the new coordinator sends no operational RPC to the pod until `CoordinatorFence` returns a successful acknowledgement (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:34`): the executor issues its first `Attach` only over a published binding, so no `Attach` reaches the pod before the fence lands. Holding the connection from the re-adopt through the fence and onward is what durably keeps the pod out of hold state: the pod re-enters hold state on any coordinator-connection loss (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:47`), so a fence over a connection that is then closed would leave an idle taken-over session to re-enter hold state and self-terminate at 120s, while a connection held from re-adopt onward leaves the pod continuously coordinated. The §4.7 `Attach` content stream stays lazily opened on the first `Send` (`pod.go:35-37,154`), so the held binding does not consume the single-consumer slot for an idle session. This is the orphan-adoption case, which is where a TTL-lapse crash takeover actually lands: the crashed coordinator's Redis lease key expires and is deleted (`leasestore.go:101` `SET ... PX`), so the pre-`Acquire` `Get` returns `leasestore.ErrNotFound` and `priorHolder` stays empty (`coordination.go:126-131`). The pre-existing `priorHolder != "" && priorHolder != s.replicaID` branch (`:144`) fires only in the narrow `Get`-then-`Acquire` race where the departing holder is still observable, so it does not cover the ordinary crash takeover; anchoring the bump, the re-adopt, and the fence on the `acquired && !boundHere && priorHolder != s.replicaID` edge is what makes them run under the failure this deliverable exists to handle, and the `priorHolder != s.replicaID` term keeps them from re-firing on every subsequent self-renew sweep of the taken-over session, because the published binding makes `boundHere` true on the next sweep (the once-per-handoff contract `RecordHandoff` documents at `:157-167`). Reuse the existing `coordfence` driver wired for the resume re-bind (`stores.go:1371`, `start.go:3914`) unmodified, driving it with the re-adopted connection's adapter as its fence client rather than adding a second fence path. Because the binding is published only after the fence acknowledges, a terminal fence failure publishes no binding, closes the re-adopt connection, and relinquishes the lease, so no serving binding lingers for a session whose fence never landed. On a terminal fence failure both the resume path and the background takeover path relinquish the coordination lease (`coordfence.go:196-201`), which is what §10.1 mandates: a new coordinator whose `CoordinatorFence` exhausts its retry budget stops extending the lease, releases the lock, and backs off with a jittered delay before reconsidering coordination (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:35`). On the resume path a waiting client aborts and a peer takes over cleanly; on the background takeover path there is no client, so recovery is the next Sweeper acquisition. Relinquishing is the recovery mechanism the spec relies on, because it surfaces the lapsed lease so a healthier replica whose path to the pod is intact can acquire it and fence, rather than pinning the pod to a coordinator that cannot reach it (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:26`, `spec/04_system-components.md:489`). The one hazard is the fixed sweep interval (`coordination.go:71`) re-adopting inside the spec's backoff window: after a relinquished takeover the very next sweep would re-satisfy the C1 adoption predicate (running/input_required + persisted `pod_assignment`, lease `ErrNotFound`, `priorHolder == ""`), re-acquire, and re-`RecordHandoff` and re-drive the re-adopt-and-fence, incrementing the generation once per sweep rather than once per §10.1 backoff. The Sweeper closes that by recording a per-session adoption backoff after a relinquished takeover and skipping re-adoption of that session until the backoff elapses, honoring §10.1's jittered 2s-to-16s delay. The generation increment stays in Postgres; the next coordinator to acquire the lease increments it again, superseding both, exactly as `spec/10_gateway-internals.md:35` specifies. The reacquired pod stays in hold state until a subsequent Sweeper acquisition fences it, and it self-terminates at `coordinatorHoldTimeoutSeconds` (default 120s) if no replica ever fences it, per the accepted failure mode in section 4.

**Anchor (executor serves from the eagerly published binding).** The crash-takeover re-adopts, fences, and publishes the serving binding to the shared `podRegistry`, so `streamFor` (`pod.go:128-160`) serves the taken-over session from `registry.Get` (`:134`) exactly as it serves a freshly bound session, and the executor needs no re-adopt path of its own. This keeps the request path free of any `CoordinatorFence` and free of the §10.1 precondition hazard: because the takeover publishes the binding only after the fence acknowledges, the executor never opens an `Attach` (an operational RPC) before the pod is fenced (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:34`). In the narrow window between the takeover's successful `Acquire` and its post-fence `podRegistry.Put`, a request landing on the takeover replica hits the pre-existing `registry.Get`-miss error (`:135`) and retries; the window is bounded by the fence completion (the §10.1 5-second deadline over at most 3 attempts), and routing a request that first lands on a non-coordinator replica to the coordinator is the deferred C-49 / T-JRN.15 forward-to-coordinator concern (section 6). The re-adopt itself replays nothing and restores no checkpoint (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:180`). The takeover re-adopt carries `// spec: 10.1 (coordinator handoff re-adopts the still-running pod), 4.7 (single content consumer per session / Attach content stream)`.

**Anchor (retire the barrier fresh-dial fallback).** With the takeover re-adopt publishing the serving binding, the coordinator always reaches the pod through the local binding, so the barrier `PodDispatcher`'s `Dial` fallback (`wiring.go:67-80`) is redundant. - **No building of the C-22 eviction trigger, and no simplification of its `coordinator_address` mirror plus fresh-dial workaround** (a proposal on another branch). Co-location makes the local binding available to the coordinator-driven eviction drive, which lets that workaround reduce, but that simplification is downstream and is revisited after this lands.

The takeover re-adopt reorders that seam into a fence-first entry point (dial, then `CoordinatorFence` before the version handshake) and adds a gRPC-channel liveness probe over the held binding, both of which are in-process control-flow additions that introduce no new wire field, RPC, or endpoint.

## 9. Resolved in adversarial review

Subsequent adversarial review rounds populate this section. Three challenge-round revisions are already folded into the staged changes above.

- **The at-bind acquire on the binding replica is retained, with the Sweeper acquire-scoping as a renewer.** An earlier draft dropped the synchronous at-bind acquire in favor of Sweeper acquire-scoping alone, on the reasoning that a fresh session's lease is provably unheld and the Sweeper would acquire it within one interval at no warm-start cost. The Sweeper then renews the lease for the sessions this replica binds and adopts genuine crash-takeover orphans (a lapsed lease this replica does not bind). Serving still gates on the binding (`pod.go:134-136`) and split-brain is still guarded by the generation fence (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:28`); the at-bind acquire adds the co-location guarantee at session start that those two do not provide, and on the resume path its `ErrHeld` doubles as the fail-closed holder check.
