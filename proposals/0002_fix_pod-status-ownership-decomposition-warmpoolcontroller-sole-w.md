# Proposal: Pod-status ownership decomposition: WarmPoolController-sole-written `Sandbox.status` (phase and conditions), a coarse occupancy phase enum, the binding kept as an etcd `SandboxClaim`, the per-task counters gateway-authoritative on the §11.2 proxy-mode posture off the CRD, and pod assignment kept in the gateway

- **Status:** Old. Not applied.
- **Date:** 2026-06-11.
- **Scope:** Carries two changes in one proposal: (1) the pod-status ownership decomposition, and (2) the task-mode between-task disposition (F-5.2.30), placed on the platform's existing Redis-counter pattern rather than on the CRD. The decomposition removes the second writer of `Sandbox.status`. The gateway stops writing `Sandbox.status.phase`/`status.activeSlots`/`status.tenantId` and also stops writing `Sandbox.status.conditions` (the `Terminated`/`Suspended` session conditions move into the Postgres session model, §7.2/§8.8); the WarmPoolController becomes the complete sole writer of `Sandbox.status`. The gateway writes only `SandboxClaim` (including the net-new `SandboxClaim.status.phase` binding disposition) and the Postgres session store; the chart drops the `sandboxes` main-resource `patch` and `watch` verbs (converging the main-resource grant to the `get`/`list` the §4.6.3 gateway RBAC paragraph at `spec/04_system-components.md:588` grants) and removes the `sandboxes/status` grant entirely (the gateway loses its last `sandboxes/status` write with the conditions move). The phase, slot-count, tenant-pin, and condition writes leave the gateway entirely. The §5.2 application-layer tenant pin is re-pointed from `Sandbox.status.tenantId` onto the gateway-stamped agent-Pod `lenny.dev/tenant-id` label. The binding stays an etcd `SandboxClaim`: it is not moved to Redis or Postgres. This preserves reliability (the binding survives a Postgres outage), gives the `lenny-sandboxclaim-guard` double-claim webhook its object, and gives the WarmPoolController its in-use signal for the drain handshake. Pod assignment stays with the gateway: the gateway reads idle inventory, stamps the `lenny.dev/tenant-id` label at claim, and reads the pod endpoint; assignment does not move to the WarmPoolController. Pod occupancy becomes a WarmPoolController level-triggered projection of `SandboxClaim` existence and disposition; a session-mode pod whose claim reaches `released`/`failed` reconciles through `draining → terminated` rather than back to `idle`, preserving the §6.2 one-session-only invariant, while a concurrent-workspace pod that loses its last slot recycles back to `idle` along the §6.2 line 164 edge. The `Sandbox.status.phase` enum is coarsened to occupancy only (`warming`, `idle`, `claimed`, `sdk_connecting`, `slot_active`, `draining`, `failed`, `terminated`); the fine session/task-lifecycle phases move off the CRD into the §7.2/§8.8 Postgres session model, and the three spec locations that still call those fine phases authoritative (§6.2:305, §15:643, §4.6.1:384) are corrected. The coarse set keeps `failed` as the pod-lifecycle terminal the WarmPoolController writes for warm-path and SDK-warm pod death where no session exists (`pkg/controller/sandbox/lifecycle/lifecycle.go:84,165,180,190`), distinct from the session-`failed` disposition that lives on the Postgres session model and on `SandboxClaim.status.phase`. The per-task hot path moves off the CRD onto the §11.2 proxy-mode counter posture (gateway-authoritative measurement plus a periodic Postgres checkpoint): the gateway increments the `tasksCompleted`/`scrubFailureCount` Redis counters itself from each task's per-task scrub outcome (it dispatches every task and already increments these counters today at `pkg/gateway/taskdriver/taskdriver.go:158-160`), so a malicious or misconfigured runtime cannot influence the residual-state retirement bound (`maxTasksPerPod`/`maxScrubFailures`) by under-reporting (the §11.2 proxy-mode property at `spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:41`). The counters are durably checkpointed to the per-pod `agent_pod_state` row in Postgres on the existing sync interval (the §11.2 `quotaSyncIntervalSeconds` mechanism), not per task, so the per-task control flow stays Redis-only (no per-task Postgres write, no per-task etcd write); on a Redis reset the durable floor is the Postgres checkpoint, and rehydration is `MAX(postgres_checkpoint, redis_current)`, gateway-authoritative (honoring the §12.4 rule that every Redis-backed role has a durable fallback). The gateway computes the between-task disposition from the Redis counters via `taskcleanup.Decide` distributed across replicas, and the per-task recycle makes no CRD write; only coarse retirement (a `maxTasksPerPod`/`maxScrubFailures`/`maxPodUptimeSeconds` limit reached, or `onCleanupFailure: fail`) crosses to the control plane via the existing claim-release → `draining` path the WarmPoolController already drives. The `ReportTaskCleanup` RPC stays (adapter → gateway): the adapter reports each task's per-task binary scrub outcome (this task's `succeeded`/`failed` result, plus `taskId` and `sessionId`), and the gateway counts from that per-task signal rather than trusting a pod-supplied cumulative total. The orphan-GC `returnToIdle` recycle is made mode-aware so a gateway-crash-orphaned session-mode pod is drained rather than blanket-idled (the one-session-only fix). The concurrent-workspace drains at §6.2 lines 165-167 also lose their gateway `Sandbox.status` writer: the uptime drains are written by the WarmPoolController from the pod `CreationTimestamp` against the pool's `maxPodUptimeSeconds`, and the unhealthy-threshold drain is written by the WarmPoolController reconciling a gateway-stamped `lenny.dev/drain-request` Pod annotation, so no concurrent pod is stranded once the gateway stops writing `Sandbox.status`. The Redis-unwired fallback concurrent-slot cap path is deleted, making the §5.2 Redis counter the single cap mechanism. `maxTaskRetries` (pod crash mid-task) stays gateway-side and untouched: that is session recovery rather than a between-task disposition (§6.2 lines 185-192). Unblocks F-5.2.30.

This document stages the proposed spec, CRD, proto, and chart edits for both the decomposition and the task-mode disposition. It does not modify any spec file. The staged spec edits in Section 7 land only after sign-off. Code, chart, proto, and test changes are recorded for the implementer and land with the implementation work rather than with the spec apply.

## 1. Problem

Two authorities write `Sandbox.status` today, contradicting the §4.6.3 ownership table.

**The §4.6.3 ownership table assigns `Sandbox.status.*` solely to the WarmPoolController.** The table row reads `Sandbox | status.* | WarmPoolController | State machine transitions, health` (`spec/04_system-components.md:574`). The §4.6.3 gateway RBAC paragraph (`spec/04_system-components.md:588`) grants the gateway `get`/`patch` on `Pods`, `create`/`get`/`delete` on `SandboxClaim`, and `get`/`list` on `Sandbox`, with no access to the `sandboxes/status` subresource. §4.6.3 SSA rule 2 (`spec/04_system-components.md:582`) prohibits `Force: true`/force-conflicts in reconciliation code.

**The shipped gateway writes `Sandbox.status` anyway.** The gateway SSA-applies `Sandbox.status.phase` (and `status.activeSlots`, `status.tenantId`) under field manager `lenny-gateway` with `client.ForceOwnership` across the claim window: `ApplyGatewayPhase` (`pkg/gateway/podclaim/gatewaystatus.go:46-56`, called from `claimer.go:114` and the binder `setPhase` chain at `pkg/gateway/podsession/binder.go:739-748`), the slot-reservation write (`pkg/gateway/podclaim/slotclaimer.go:549-551`), the slot-release write (`slotclaimer.go:684-688`), and the suspend write (`binder.go:1537`). The drain/reclaim yield uses a non-Apply `Status().Update` to hand `status.phase` back to the WarmPoolController (`binder.go:780-801`, `slotclaimer.go:295-310`). The WarmPoolController is the second authority, writing `Sandbox.status.phase` via non-forcing SSA Apply under field manager `lenny-warm-pool-controller` (`pkg/controller/sandbox/controller.go:621`→`:686`; `pkg/controller/warmpool/pod_reconciler.go:469-474`).

**The chart over-grants `sandboxes/status` to make the gateway code work.** The shipped chart grants the gateway `get`/`update`/`patch` on `sandboxes/status` (`charts/lenny/templates/gateway-deployment.yaml:130-132`), a grant absent from the §4.6.3 paragraph at `:588` and present nowhere in `spec/`. Code comments cite a "§4.6.3 gateway carve-out" (`gatewaystatus.go:16-25`, `binder.go:730`) that the normative table does not contain.

**The fine session-lifecycle phases are the half the gateway force-writes.** The single 21-value `Sandbox.status.phase` state machine (`pkg/sandbox/state/state.go:74-95`; the §6.2 fenced block at `spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:82-177`) conflates warm-pool inventory (`warming`, `sdk_connecting`, `idle`, `draining`, `terminated`), session/task lifecycle (`receiving_uploads`, `finalizing_workspace`, `running_setup`, `starting_session`, `attached`, `task_cleanup`, `suspended`, `resuming`, `resume_pending`, `awaiting_client_action`, and the terminal `completed`/`failed`/`cancelled`/`expired`), and the session-to-pod binding (`claimed`, `slot_active`). The WarmPoolController never writes the fine session phases: its lifecycle planner sets only coarse phases (`Idle`, `Failed`, `Draining`, `Terminated`, `SDKConnecting`) because `SetupWithManager` registers only `For(&Sandbox{}).Owns(&Pod{})` (`pkg/controller/sandbox/controller.go:714-719`) and has no view of session or task progress.

**Finding context.** F-5.2.30 (`BUILD-GAPS.md:3689`, DEFERRED High) is the task-execution driver that runs the between-task reuse loop, calls `taskcleanup.Decide`, writes the §6.2 disposition pod phase (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:147-156`), writes the `scrub_warning` pod annotation, and emits the retirement counters. The shipped code keeps this in the gateway, and the per-pod counters live in gateway process memory today (`pkg/gateway/taskdriver/taskdriver.go:101-104`), against the §10.1 statelessness rule (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:5`). The fix this proposal stages keeps the disposition computation in the gateway, where pod assignment and the task-dispatch loop already live, and takes the in-memory counters off the gateway by placing them on the platform's existing §11.2 proxy-mode counter posture: gateway-authoritative measurement on the Redis fast-path with a periodic Postgres checkpoint as the durable fallback (`spec/05_runtime-registry-and-pool-model.md:519-521`; `spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:41,44`; `spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:183` for the cumulative scrub-failure semantics). This reuses the same Redis fast-path and the same gateway-authoritative measurement the platform already uses for proxy-mode token-usage counters, where the gateway-observed counts are authoritative, written to Redis immediately, and the pod self-report is ignored for the bound (`spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:41`), and Postgres is the periodic checkpoint reconciled by `MAX(redis_current, postgres_checkpoint)` so a Redis outage never silently resets a security limit. The gateway increments `tasksCompleted`/`scrubFailureCount` itself from each task's per-task scrub outcome (it dispatches every task and already does this at `pkg/gateway/taskdriver/taskdriver.go:158-160`), so a malicious or misconfigured runtime cannot influence the residual-state retirement bound by under-reporting. The counters are checkpointed to the per-pod `agent_pod_state` row in Postgres on the existing §11.2 sync interval, not per task, so the per-task control flow stays Redis-only; on a Redis reset the durable floor is the Postgres checkpoint. The per-task binary scrub outcome still reaches the gateway from the adapter through the `ReportTaskCleanup` RPC (the adapter has no Kubernetes client, §10.3), but it carries this task's binary result rather than a cumulative total; the gateway counts from the per-task signal and feeds the gateway-side `taskcleanup.Decide` rather than a CRD write. The per-task recycle is gateway data-plane state (the gateway keeps the claim and dispatches the next task) and makes no per-task etcd write; only coarse retirement crosses to the control plane, via the existing claim-release → `draining` path the WarmPoolController already drives. F-5.2.30 is in-scope; the design is Section 4.6.

## 2. Decisions

The following decisions constrain this design. Four decisions are settled here and staged: the full conditions separation, the coarse phase enum, the counters gateway-authoritative on the §11.2 proxy-mode posture off the CRD, and keeping the binding as an etcd `SandboxClaim`.

- **The gateway stops writing `Sandbox.status` entirely; the WarmPoolController is the complete sole writer.** `ApplyGatewayPhase` (the `claimed` write at `claimer.go:114`, taken by both session-mode and task-mode whole-pod claims through `Claimer.Claim` at `pkg/gateway/sessionserver/start.go:1186`), the slot-reservation and slot-release SSA phase writes, the suspend phase write, the session-mode drain/reclaim `Status().Update` (`binder.go:780-801`), the concurrent-mode uptime drain `Status().Update` (`slotclaimer.go:295-310`, `drainExpiredPod`), and the concurrent-mode whole-pod unhealthy drain `Status().Update` (`slotbinder.go:382-405`, `DrainSandbox`) are all removed. Removing `ApplyGatewayPhase` removes the task-mode `claimed` write as well as the session-mode one, so the WarmPoolController occupancy projection (Section 4.2) writes the task-mode `claimed` occupancy for a task-mode `Sandbox` holding a live claim. The gateway's `Sandbox.status.conditions` writes (`Terminated`/`Suspended`, `binder.go:1479-1484,1547-1552`) are also removed (see the conditions decision below). The gateway no longer holds the per-pod task counters or writes the task-mode disposition pod phase; the disposition is computed gateway-side against the Redis counters (see the counter and disposition decisions below).
- **Full conditions separation: the `Terminated`/`Suspended` session conditions move into the Postgres session model (§7.2/§8.8), so the gateway loses its last `sandboxes/status` write.** The gateway writes `Sandbox.status.conditions` from inside the claim window today (the `Terminated`/`Suspended` conditions at `binder.go:1479-1484,1547-1552`, via `pkg/sandbox/condition` with `ForceOwnership`). These are session-lifecycle facts (a session ended; a session was suspended on interrupt), and session state already lives in the Postgres session model (§7.2, §8.8). Recording them there, on the session row that already carries the session-terminal disposition and the suspend transition, removes the gateway's final reason to touch `Sandbox.status`. The WarmPoolController stays the writer of the pod-lifecycle conditions it already owns (for example the orphan-claim reclaim condition at `gc.go:242-248`). With the gateway out of `Sandbox.status` for phase, slots, tenant, and conditions, the WarmPoolController is the complete sole writer the §4.6.3 table already assigns, and the chart drops the gateway `sandboxes/status` grant outright (rather than narrowing it). Why: a shared `Sandbox.status.conditions` writer is the same two-writer anti-pattern as the phase write, only on a different subfield; moving the session conditions to where session state already lives keeps one writer per subresource.
- **Coarse phase enum: `Sandbox.status.phase` carries occupancy only.** The fine session/task-lifecycle phases (`receiving_uploads`, `finalizing_workspace`, `running_setup`, `starting_session`, `attached`, `suspended`, `task_cleanup`, and the session terminals `completed`/`cancelled`/`expired`, plus the recovery phases `resuming`/`resume_pending`/`awaiting_client_action`) are removed from the `Sandbox.status.phase` enum in `pkg/sandbox/state/state.go` and from the §6.2 listing. The enum keeps only the coarse occupancy values the WarmPoolController writes: `warming`, `idle`, `claimed`, `sdk_connecting`, `slot_active`, `draining`, `failed`, `terminated`. The coarse set retains `failed` because the WarmPoolController genuinely writes `state.Failed` for warm-path and SDK-warm pod death where no session exists (`pkg/controller/sandbox/lifecycle/lifecycle.go:84,165,180,190`) and the §6.2 fenced block keeps the `warming/sdk_connecting → failed` edges; the session-`failed` disposition (a task-mode `onCleanupFailure: fail` retirement or a session failure) is recorded on `SandboxClaim.status.phase` and on the Postgres session row, not on `Sandbox.status.phase`. The fine session/task phases live solely in the §7.2/§8.8 Postgres session model. Three spec locations that still call the fine phases authoritative are corrected: §6.2:305 ("The authoritative state machine lives in the `Sandbox` CRD `.status.phase` and `.status.conditions`"), §15:643 (the fine phases "are tracked in the `Sandbox` CRD `.status.phase`"), and §4.6.1:384 ("The authoritative pod state machine lives on the referenced `Sandbox` CRD's `.status.phase`"). The `lenny-sandboxclaim-guard` admission guard's `claimBoundPhases` accept-set (`pkg/admission/sandboxclaim_guard/guard.go:94-102`) is reduced to the coarse bound phases `{claimed, slot_active}`, because the fine setup-chain phases no longer appear on `Sandbox.status.phase`. Why: the WarmPoolController never wrote the fine session phases (its lifecycle planner sets only coarse phases because `SetupWithManager` registers only `For(&Sandbox{}).Owns(&Pod{})`, `controller.go:714-719`), so they were always gateway-force-written values whose authority belongs with the session model; a single 21-value enum that conflated warm-pool inventory with session and task lifecycle is what produced the two-writer split in the first place.
- **The per-task counters and disposition live on the gateway-authoritative §11.2 proxy-mode posture rather than the CRD.** `Sandbox.status` carries only `Phase`, `ActiveSlots`, `TenantID`, and `SessionID` today (`pkg/apis/lenny/v1alpha1/sandbox_types.go:100,107,114,123`), and the task counters live in gateway process memory (`pkg/gateway/taskdriver/taskdriver.go:101-104`), against the §10.1 statelessness rule. The `tasksCompleted` and `scrubFailureCount` counters move onto the §11.2 proxy-mode counter posture: gateway-authoritative measurement on the Redis fast-path with a periodic Postgres checkpoint as the durable fallback. The gateway increments each Redis counter itself from the per-task scrub outcome (it dispatches every task and already increments these counters today at `pkg/gateway/taskdriver/taskdriver.go:158-160`); the pod's word is not trusted for the count. This mirrors §11.2 proxy mode, where gateway-observed counts are authoritative, written to Redis immediately, and pod self-reports are ignored for the bound (`spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:41`): a malicious or misconfigured runtime cannot influence the residual-state retirement bound (`maxTasksPerPod`/`maxScrubFailures`) by under-reporting. The gateway checkpoints each Redis counter to the per-pod `agent_pod_state` row in Postgres (the new nullable `tasks_completed`/`scrub_failure_count` columns) on the existing §11.2 sync interval (`quotaSyncIntervalSeconds`, `spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:44`), not per task, so the per-task control flow stays Redis-only — no per-task Postgres write and no per-task etcd write — and the periodic-not-per-task property preserves the performance characteristic. On a Redis reset the durable floor is the Postgres checkpoint: rehydration is `MAX(postgres_checkpoint, redis_current)`, gateway-authoritative, the same MAX rule §11.2 applies to proxy-mode quota recovery (`spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:44`); the pod self-report is ignored for the bound. This honors the §12.4 rule that every Redis-backed role has a durable fallback (`spec/12_storage-architecture.md:199`). The disposition is computed gateway-side by `taskcleanup.Decide` against the Redis counters, distributed across gateway replicas (the same replica set that runs the task-dispatch loop and holds pod assignment). The per-task recycle is gateway data-plane state: the gateway keeps the claim and dispatches the next task, and it makes no per-task CRD write. Only coarse retirement crosses to the control plane: when a `maxTasksPerPod`/`maxScrubFailures`/`maxPodUptimeSeconds` limit is reached or `onCleanupFailure: fail` applies, the gateway signals retirement via the existing claim-release path and the WarmPoolController writes `draining`. The `ReportTaskCleanup` RPC stays (adapter → gateway): the adapter reports each task's per-task binary scrub outcome (`succeeded`/`failed`, plus `taskId` and `sessionId`), and the gateway counts from that per-task signal rather than from a pod-supplied cumulative total. Why: putting a per-task counter on the CRD caused per-task etcd write amplification (one status write per task across the whole task-mode fleet) and a single-WarmPoolController-leader disposition bottleneck; the §11.2 proxy-mode posture is the platform's already-shipped, fault-isolated home for a high-write security counter the gateway measures directly, and computing the disposition where pod assignment and task dispatch already live removes the cross-component relay entirely.
- **The binding stays an etcd `SandboxClaim`.** The session-to-pod binding is not moved to Redis or Postgres; it remains the etcd `SandboxClaim` the gateway creates and deletes. Why: etcd is the platform's most-available store, so an etcd binding survives a Postgres outage (a Redis or Postgres binding would not); the `lenny-sandboxclaim-guard` double-claim webhook needs a Kubernetes object to admit against, which only the `SandboxClaim` provides (`spec/04_system-components.md:384`); and the WarmPoolController's drain handshake reads claim existence as its in-use signal for a session-mode pod, so the claim is the seam the occupancy projection reflects. The gateway gains the net-new `SandboxClaim.status.phase` binding-disposition writer (`bound` at claim, `released`/`failed` at release) on the gateway-owned `SandboxClaim.status` surface (`spec/04_system-components.md:590`). The Go binding state machine `pkg/sandboxclaim/state/state.go:23-32` ships only `Bound`, `Released`, and `Failed`, and `ValidTransitions()` is `{"", Bound}, {Bound, Released}, {Bound, Failed}` (`:58-64`); the spec/CRD `active` value (`spec/04_system-components.md:592`, `charts/lenny/crds/lenny.dev_sandboxclaims.yaml:170`) is not produced by the Go state machine and is left as-written, so the gateway writes `bound` at claim and the occupancy projection treats any non-terminal claim phase as a live claim without requiring the gateway to write `active`.
- **Pod assignment stays with the gateway.** The gateway reads idle inventory (the whole-pod candidate scan at `claimer.go:92` and the concurrent-slot candidate scan at `slotclaimer.go:374,377`), stamps the `lenny.dev/tenant-id` label at claim (`stampPodTenant`, `slotclaimer.go:102-117`), and reads the pod endpoint from the WarmPoolController-recorded `Sandbox.status` address. Assignment does not move to the WarmPoolController. Why: assignment is on the claim hot path, which the spec keeps off the controller (`spec/04_system-components.md:386`, "This keeps the controller off the claim hot path entirely — pod-to-session binding is resolved at the API-server level with no single-writer bottleneck"). The decomposition removes the gateway's `Sandbox.status` writes; the gateway keeps its read-and-assign role.
- **The concurrent-workspace drains at §6.2 lines 165-167 lose their gateway writer.** The uptime drains (§6.2 lines 166-167) become a WarmPoolController-derived transition from the pod `CreationTimestamp` against the pool's `maxPodUptimeSeconds`, and the unhealthy-threshold drain (§6.2 line 165) is written by the controller reconciling a gateway-stamped `lenny.dev/drain-request` Pod annotation (the gateway already holds `patch` on `Pods`). The gateway writes only `SandboxClaim` (including `SandboxClaim.status.phase`), the `lenny.dev/drain-request` Pod annotation (using its existing Pod `patch`), the Postgres session store, and the agent-Pod labels it already mutates (it creates and deletes the binding claim, `pkg/gateway/podclaim/claimer.go:140-158`). `SandboxClaim.status` is the gateway-owned surface in the §4.6.3 table (`spec/04_system-components.md:590`), so this preserves the WarmPoolController-sole assignment of `Sandbox.status.phase` at `spec/04_system-components.md:574`.
- **The orphan-GC recycle is made mode-aware.** `ClaimGarbageCollector.returnToIdle` (`pkg/controller/warmpool/gc.go:205-253`) recycles a claimed pod to `idle` unconditionally today. Under the new occupancy model this lets a gateway-crash-orphaned session-mode pod be reused across sessions, a one-session-only / cross-tenant-leak violation. The orphan-GC must be mode-aware: it drains session-mode pods (routing them to `draining`, the same terminal-disposition path the occupancy projection drives) and never blanket-idles them. Concurrent-workspace pods may still recycle to `idle`. Why: the §6.2 one-session-only invariant (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:24`) requires a settled session-mode pod to be terminated and replaced rather than recycled, and the orphan path is a settle path the gateway no longer attends to.
- **The Redis-unwired fallback concurrent-slot cap path is deleted, and `Sandbox.status.activeSlots` is dropped.** On the `Counter==nil` path the gateway's `Sandbox.status` SSA Apply doubles as the optimistic-lock cap check (`slotclaimer.go:546-557`: a conflict triggers undo-and-retry; the pre-read at `:514` reads `status.activeSlots`). Removing the gateway status write leaves nothing incrementing `status.activeSlots`, so that gate becomes vacuous. The §5.2 atomic Redis Lua `GET`-compare-`INCR` on `lenny:pod:{pod_id}:active_slots` (`spec/05_runtime-registry-and-pool-model.md:519`) is the single cap mechanism and is authoritative. The `Counter==nil` else-branch is removed and a nil `Counter` for a concurrent-mode claim is a configuration error. The code itself already disclaims the fallback as non-spec-aligned (`slotclaimer.go:220-223`, `cmd/lenny-gateway/main.go:2444-2447`). With the cap moved to the Redis counter and the gateway no longer writing it, `Sandbox.status.activeSlots` is a read-without-a-writer, so it is dropped from the CRD (Section 4.5, Section 7) and the production reads of the field (`pkg/gateway/podclaim/slotclaimer.go:377,514,518,622`, `pkg/podregistry/crd.go:509`, and `pkg/podlifecycle/agentsandbox.go:254`, enumerated in Section 4.5) are re-pointed onto the Redis slot counter or dropped as the single source.
- **Pod occupancy is a WarmPoolController level-triggered projection of `SandboxClaim` existence.** The WarmPoolController has no `SandboxClaim` watch today (`SetupWithManager` registers only `For(&Sandbox{}).Owns(&Pod{})`, `pkg/controller/sandbox/controller.go:714-719`; the only `SandboxClaim` references are `List`-only at `finalizer.go:60` and `warmpool/gc.go:151`), so adding the watch and the projection is net-new controller work. The coarse `lenny.dev/state` label is already a WarmPoolController-derived projection of `Sandbox.status.phase` via `state.CoarseState` (`pkg/controller/sandbox/controller.go:478-484`, `syncPodStateLabel` at `:496-516`); the change re-sources the occupancy phase from claim existence rather than from a gateway write.
- **The narrow cross-context seam is `SandboxClaim` existence.** The gateway creates and deletes the claim; the WarmPoolController reflects occupancy from it. No new client-facing protocol surface is added.
- **v1 ships one canonical implementation per concern with no tier-dependent branches and no migration shim.** No deployments exist in the wild. The gateway's `Sandbox.status` writers are deleted rather than gated behind a flag, and the dual `Counter`-wired/`Counter==nil` slot path is collapsed to the single Redis-counter path.

## 3. Design overview

The gateway's authority over `Sandbox.status` is removed: it writes only `SandboxClaim`, the Redis counters and the Postgres session store, and the agent-Pod labels and annotations it already mutates. It creates the binding claim at `claimer.go:103` and deletes it on release, and it owns the session and task state machine in Postgres (§7.2, §8.8). Removing the gateway's `Sandbox.status` phase, slot-count, tenant-pin, and condition writes leaves the WarmPoolController as the complete sole writer of that subresource, which is the assignment the §4.6.3 table already records. The session-lifecycle conditions (`Terminated`/`Suspended`) the gateway wrote on `Sandbox.status.conditions` move onto the Postgres session row in the same model that already holds the session-terminal disposition and the suspend transition (§7.2/§8.8). The `Sandbox.status.phase` enum is coarsened to occupancy only: it carries `warming`, `idle`, `claimed`, `sdk_connecting`, `slot_active`, `draining`, `failed`, and `terminated`, and the fine session/task-lifecycle phases live solely in the Postgres session model. The coarse set keeps `failed` as the pod-lifecycle terminal for pre-attached pod death (`pkg/controller/sandbox/lifecycle/lifecycle.go:84,165,180,190`). Pod assignment stays with the gateway: it reads idle inventory, stamps the tenant-id label at claim, and reads the WarmPoolController-recorded pod endpoint; the decomposition removes only the gateway's status writes.

The WarmPoolController gains a `SandboxClaim` watch and computes pod occupancy as a level-triggered reconciliation of claim existence, claim disposition, and concurrent-workspace lifecycle policy. A never-bound warm `Sandbox` with no referencing claim reads `idle`; a `Sandbox` referenced by one or more live claims reads the occupancy value (`claimed` for a session-mode or task-mode pod, `slot_active` for a concurrent-workspace pod). The projection is mode-aware (it reads `Sandbox.spec.executionMode`, enum `session;task;concurrent` at `pkg/apis/lenny/v1alpha1/sandbox_types.go:45`) to preserve the §6.2 one-session-only security invariant: a session-mode `Sandbox` whose referencing claim has reached the gateway-recorded terminal disposition (`SandboxClaim.status.phase` of `released` or `failed`) reconciles through its terminal phase to `draining` and then `terminated`, and is never returned to `idle`. A task-mode `Sandbox` reads `claimed` while a live claim references it; a session spans multiple sequential tasks on one pod under one `claim-<sessionID>` (the SDK receives a new `TaskID` under the same `SessionID` after each `task_ready` signal, `spec/15_external-api-surface.md:1461`). The claim stays live across the between-task boundary, the gateway-side recycle makes no CRD write, and the occupancy projection keeps reading `claimed` until the session terminates or a retirement records `released`/`failed` on `SandboxClaim.status.phase` and crosses to the control plane. A concurrent-workspace `Sandbox` recycles: when its last slot's claim is deleted it returns from `slot_active` to `idle` along the §6.2 line 164 edge, and the controller drives the §6.2 lines 165-167 concurrent-workspace drains itself, from the pod uptime against the pool's `maxPodUptimeSeconds` (lines 166-167) and from a gateway-stamped `lenny.dev/drain-request` Pod annotation on the unhealthy-threshold trigger (line 165). The generic occupancy path emits `idle` for a `Sandbox` currently in a warm-inventory phase or a concurrent-workspace `slot_active` `Sandbox` with no live claim, and does not overwrite an in-flight `draining` or terminal phase. The coarse `lenny.dev/state` label projection (`syncPodStateLabel`) is unchanged in mechanism; its phase input now comes from the claim-existence projection rather than from a gateway write.

The task-mode between-task disposition is computed gateway-side, off the CRD. After each task the adapter reports this task's binary scrub `Result` to the gateway via `ReportTaskCleanup` (Section 4.6); the gateway increments the pod's Redis `tasksCompleted`/`scrubFailureCount` counters itself from that per-task signal (it dispatches every task and already does this at `pkg/gateway/taskdriver/taskdriver.go:158-160`; the pod's word is not trusted for the count, mirroring §11.2 proxy mode at `spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:41`) and runs `taskcleanup.Decide` against those counters plus the pool `taskPolicy` (`maxTasksPerPod`, `maxScrubFailures`, `maxPodUptimeSeconds`, `onCleanupFailure`, and the runtime `preConnect` capability) and the pod uptime. §6.2 routes the between-task boundary to one of four dispositions: `idle` for a non-preConnect recycle (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:147`), `sdk_connecting → idle` for a preConnect re-warm so the returned idle pod is SDK-warm (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:154`, preserving the invariant at `spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:183` that "every idle pod in a preConnect pool is SDK-warm"), `draining` when a retirement limit is reached (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:149-151`), and `failed` under `onCleanupFailure: fail` (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:156`). The shipped disposition function `taskcleanup.Decide` already encodes this branch (`pkg/sandbox/taskcleanup/taskcleanup.go:64-110,173-266`) and is unchanged; this design changes only where its inputs come from (the Redis counters rather than gateway process memory) and removes the gateway's `Sandbox.status` write for the disposition. A recycle disposition is gateway data-plane state: the gateway keeps the claim live, re-warms the SDK in-pod if needed, and dispatches the session's next task, making no CRD write. A retirement disposition (`draining` or `failed`) crosses to the control plane through the existing claim-release path: the gateway records `released`/`failed` on `SandboxClaim.status.phase` and the WarmPoolController occupancy projection drives the pod to `draining` and then `terminated`. The Redis counters carry `tasksCompleted` and `scrubFailureCount` on the §11.2 proxy-mode posture (gateway-authoritative measurement on the Redis fast-path with a periodic Postgres checkpoint to the per-pod `agent_pod_state` row on the existing `quotaSyncIntervalSeconds` interval, not per task; rehydration is `MAX(postgres_checkpoint, redis_current)`), so a Redis outage never silently resets a residual-state security limit: the Postgres checkpoint is the durable floor on a Redis reset, and the §12.4 durable-fallback rule holds (`spec/12_storage-architecture.md:199`). The §6.2 cumulative scrub-failure persistence through re-warm (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:183`) holds because the counter is durable in Redis with the Postgres checkpoint rather than in gateway memory.

The gateway records the session-terminal disposition on its own surface. `SandboxClaim.status.phase` is the gateway-owned binding state (`spec/04_system-components.md:590`). The current §4.6.3 `released`/`failed` bullets (`spec/04_system-components.md:593-594`) describe a referenced `Sandbox` that has already transitioned to `idle` or `draining`/`terminated` at the moment `released`/`failed` is recorded, and a claim deleted shortly after entering `released` or right after the Postgres persist; this design reverses that ordering, so Section 7.6 restages both bullets. On session completion or failure the gateway sets `SandboxClaim.status.phase` to `released` or `failed` rather than writing `Sandbox.status`, the referenced `Sandbox.status.phase` is still at its session-terminal occupancy phase (`claimed`) when the gateway records that disposition, and the claim survives until the WarmPoolController has driven the session-mode pod through `draining → terminated`. No production code writes `SandboxClaim.status.phase` today: the `SandboxClaim` phase type at `pkg/sandboxclaim/state/state.go:20` ("State is the SandboxClaim phase, written to `SandboxClaim.status.phase`") has zero production importers, and the only `SandboxClaim` writes in the gateway claim path are the CREATE/DELETE in `pkg/gateway/podclaim/claimer.go`, which set `spec` only. (`pkg/podregistry/crd.go` writes `Sandbox.status.phase`, a different subresource on a different CRD, and references `SandboxClaim` nowhere.) The gateway gains the `SandboxClaim.status.phase` writer as net-new work in the claim/release path.

The fine session-lifecycle phases (`receiving_uploads`, `finalizing_workspace`, `running_setup`, `starting_session`, `attached`, `suspended`, `task_cleanup`, `resuming`, `resume_pending`, `awaiting_client_action`, and the session terminals `completed`/`cancelled`/`expired`) are removed from the `Sandbox.status.phase` enum and live solely in the §7.2/§8.8 Postgres session model. ## 4. Detailed design

### 4.1 Removing the gateway `Sandbox.status` writers

The gateway writers to remove are `ApplyGatewayPhase` and the phase-setting in `gatewayStatusPatch` (`pkg/gateway/podclaim/gatewaystatus.go`), the `claimer.go:114` phase write (the `SandboxClaim` CREATE at `claimer.go:103` is the claim mechanism and remains), the slot-reservation and slot-release SSA phase writes (`slotclaimer.go:549-551`, `:686-688`), the session-mode drain `Status().Update` (`binder.go:780-801` `b.drain`), the concurrent-mode uptime drain `Status().Update` (`slotclaimer.go:295-310` `drainExpiredPod`, the §6.2 lines 166-167 `slot_active → draining` and `idle → draining` over-uptime drain per its doc comment at `slotclaimer.go:286-294`), the concurrent-mode whole-pod unhealthy drain `Status().Update` (`pkg/gateway/podsession/slotbinder.go:382-405`, `DrainSandbox`, called from `pkg/gateway/sessionserver/start.go:1354` on the §6.2 line 165 / §5.2 `spec/05_runtime-registry-and-pool-model.md:531` whole-pod replacement trigger), the binder `setPhase` chain and `failPhase` Apply (`binder.go:739-748`, `:757`), the suspend Apply (`binder.go:1537`), the fallback-claim phase Apply (`binder.go:1414-1417`), and the `Sandbox.status.conditions` writes (`binder.go:1479-1484` `Terminated`, `binder.go:1547-1552` `Suspended`, via `pkg/sandbox/condition`). The session and task lifecycle these phases tracked, and the `Terminated`/`Suspended` session conditions, are already maintained or are moved into the Postgres session store the gateway owns (§7.2, §8.8); §15 (`spec/15_external-api-surface.md:626`) already returns only session/task-model states from `GET /v1/sessions/{id}` and never the §6.2 pod phases. The condition writes move to the Postgres session row: the gateway records the session-terminal disposition and the suspend transition on the session model it already owns, so an operator reads them from the session API (`GET /v1/sessions/{id}` and `GET /v1/admin/sessions/{id}`) rather than from `Sandbox.status.conditions`. The pod-lifecycle conditions the WarmPoolController already writes (for example the orphan-claim reclaim condition at `gc.go:242-248`) stay WarmPoolController-owned.

Removing `drainExpiredPod` and `DrainSandbox` leaves the three concurrent-workspace drain transitions at §6.2 lines 165-167 with no writer. These triggers do not correspond to a single `SandboxClaim` reaching a terminal disposition: §6.2 line 165 fires on the gateway's rolling per-pod fail-or-leak count crossing `ceil(maxConcurrent/2)` while the pod typically still holds live slots; §6.2 line 166 fires on `maxPodUptimeSeconds` against the pod `CreationTimestamp` while slots are live; and §6.2 line 167 fires on `maxPodUptimeSeconds` against an idle pod with zero claims. Their writer is relocated to the WarmPoolController as part of the occupancy projection (Section 4.2), because the controller is the sole writer of `Sandbox.status.phase` and already reads the inputs both triggers need. The uptime drains (§6.2 lines 166-167) become a WarmPoolController-derived transition: the controller reads the pod `CreationTimestamp` (`pod_reconciler.go:383,433`) against the `ConcurrentWorkspacePolicy.MaxPodUptimeSeconds` the PoolScalingController already stamps on the pool (`pkg/controller/poolscaling/poolstoresource.go:83-85`) and emits `slot_active → draining` or `idle → draining`. The unhealthy drain (§6.2 line 165) keeps a gateway-owned signal because the fail-or-leak count is observed by the gateway: the gateway stamps a `lenny.dev/drain-request` annotation on the agent Pod when the rolling count crosses the threshold (the gateway already holds `patch` on `Pods` at `spec/04_system-components.md:588`, and the WarmPoolController already watches the backing Pod via `Owns(&Pod{})` at `controller.go:716`), and the WarmPoolController reconciles that annotation into `slot_active → draining`. This reuses the existing gateway Pod-patch grant and the existing controller Pod watch, adding no new RBAC surface or CRD field. The session-mode drain (`b.drain`) is the only drain whose disposition the gateway records on `SandboxClaim.status.phase`.

The session-terminal disposition that the removed session-mode drain (`b.drain`) recorded moves to `SandboxClaim.status.phase` (the gateway-owned binding surface, `spec/04_system-components.md:590`). On session completion, cancellation, or expiry the gateway sets `SandboxClaim.status.phase` to `released`, and on session failure it sets `failed`, per the §4.6.3 enumeration at `spec/04_system-components.md:593-595`. The WarmPoolController occupancy projection (Section 4.2) reads that terminal disposition and drives the session-mode pod through `draining → terminated` along the legal `state.ValidTransitions` edges (`pkg/sandbox/state/state.go:243-245,258-261`), preserving the §6.2 one-session-only invariant (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:24`) that a settled session-mode pod is terminated and replaced rather than recycled to `idle`. The claim survives until the WarmPoolController reaches `terminated`; only then does the gateway delete it. The concurrent-mode drains do not flow through this claim-disposition path: their writer is the WarmPoolController-derived uptime check and the gateway-stamped `lenny.dev/drain-request` Pod annotation described above, both of which the occupancy projection reconciles.

The chart edit removes the `sandboxes/status` rule (`gateway-deployment.yaml:130-132`) entirely. With the phase, slot-count, tenant-pin, and condition writes all removed from the gateway, no gateway code path writes the `sandboxes/status` subresource, so the `get`/`update`/`patch` grant is deleted rather than narrowed. On the `sandboxes` main resource (`gateway-deployment.yaml:122-129`) the chart drops both `patch` and `watch`, leaving `get`/`list`. The `watch` verb is dropped because the gateway holds no `Sandbox` informer or watch in code (no `Sandbox` Watch/Informer exists in `pkg/gateway`), and §4.6.3:588 grants the gateway only `get`/`list` on `Sandbox`; retaining `watch` would leave the chart over `spec/04_system-components.md:588` as-written. The gateway's §5.2 tenant pin is stamped on the agent Pod label `lenny.dev/tenant-id` (`stampPodTenant`, `slotclaimer.go:102-117`, backed by the existing gateway `patch` on `Pods` at `spec/04_system-components.md:588`); the redundant SSA label write on the `Sandbox` main resource (`slotclaimer.go:588-606`) is removed so the gateway needs no `patch` on `Sandbox`. The §4.6.3 gateway RBAC paragraph at `spec/04_system-components.md:588` grants the gateway `get`/`list` on `Sandbox` with mutations limited to Pod labels and `SandboxClaim`, so dropping the `sandboxes` `patch` and `watch` verbs converges the chart's `Sandbox` main-resource grants to that paragraph's `get`/`list` as-written. The RBAC addition is the gateway `sandboxclaims/status` subresource grant for the net-new binding-disposition write, staged in §4.6.3:588 in Section 7.1. The chart RBAC change and the code-writer change ship together: removing the `sandboxes/status` grant while any gateway code still issued a status write would return HTTP 403 from the running gateway, so the writer removals (including the condition writes) and the grant removal land in the same change.

### 4.2 Occupancy as a claim-existence projection

The WarmPoolController watches `SandboxClaim` and sets `Sandbox.status.phase` to the occupancy value as a level-triggered reconciliation of claim existence, claim disposition, and lifecycle policy. The projection branches on `Sandbox.spec.executionMode` (enum `session;task;concurrent`, `pkg/apis/lenny/v1alpha1/sandbox_types.go:45`). A never-bound warm `Sandbox` in a warm-inventory phase (`warming`, `sdk_connecting`, `idle`) referenced by no claim reads `idle`; a `Sandbox` referenced by one or more live claims (a `SandboxClaim.status.phase` that is not a terminal binding phase, where the Go binding state machine ships only `bound` as the non-terminal phase, `pkg/sandboxclaim/state/state.go:23-32`) reads `claimed` (session mode or task mode, both of which take the whole-pod claim path through `Claimer.Claim` at `pkg/gateway/sessionserver/start.go:1186`) or `slot_active` (concurrent mode). The projection reads any non-terminal binding phase as a live claim, so the spec/CRD `active` value (`spec/04_system-components.md:592`), which the Go state machine does not produce, is also read as live if ever written. The occupancy phase is a coarse value: the projection writes only `warming`, `idle`, `claimed`, `sdk_connecting`, `slot_active`, `draining`, `failed`, or `terminated`, the enum the WarmPoolController already produces (`failed` is the pre-attached pod-death terminal the lifecycle planner writes, `pkg/controller/sandbox/lifecycle/lifecycle.go:84,165,180,190`). The task-mode between-task disposition is computed gateway-side off the Redis counters (Section 4.6); the WarmPoolController does not read a scrub outcome or a task counter, so the occupancy projection for a task-mode pod is purely existence-driven.

A session-mode `Sandbox` whose referencing claim has reached a terminal disposition (`SandboxClaim.status.phase` of `released` or `failed`) is driven through its terminal phase to `draining` and then `terminated` along the `state.ValidTransitions` edges (`pkg/sandbox/state/state.go:243-245,258-261`: `{Claimed,Draining}`, `{Draining,Terminated}`), never back to `idle`; this is the §6.2 one-session-only invariant (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:24`), and the projection that follows it replaces the gateway's removed `b.drain` write (`binder.go:780-801`). The session-mode pod is never recycled to `idle`: the projection emits `idle` from a `claimed` value only for the task-mode recycle and the concurrent-recycle cases below, both gated on `executionMode`, never for a session-mode pod.

A task-mode `Sandbox` reads `claimed` while a live claim references it. A task-mode session spans multiple sequential tasks on one pod under one `claim-<sessionID>` (the SDK receives a new `TaskID` under the same `SessionID` after each `task_ready` signal, `spec/15_external-api-surface.md:1461`); the claim persists across the between-task boundary because the pod keeps the binding while the gateway dispatches the next task. The gateway-side recycle makes no CRD write, so the occupancy projection keeps reading `claimed` until the session terminates or a retirement records `released`/`failed` on `SandboxClaim.status.phase`. The gateway runs the between-task disposition against the Redis counters (Section 4.6) and, on a recycle disposition, keeps the claim live and dispatches the next task without any CRD write; the occupancy projection reads the live claim and writes `claimed` for the duration of each task's execution. The recycle to `idle`/`sdk_connecting` between tasks is the gateway's in-pod re-warm rather than a `Sandbox.status` phase write: while the pod sits between tasks under a live claim, the coarse occupancy projection still reads `claimed` (the claim is live), and the gateway's task-dispatch loop selects the next task for this bound session rather than the candidate scan re-selecting the pod. Only when the session terminates, or when the gateway-side disposition retires the pod (a `maxTasksPerPod`/`maxScrubFailures`/`maxPodUptimeSeconds` limit reached, or `onCleanupFailure: fail`), does the gateway set `SandboxClaim.status.phase` to `released`/`failed`; the occupancy projection then drives the pod through `draining` to `terminated` along the existing `{Claimed,Draining}`/`{Draining,Terminated}` edges. A task-mode claim deleted with no retirement (a gateway-crash orphan rather than a clean session end) is handled by the mode-aware orphan-GC in Section 4.7, which drains the session-mode/task-mode pod rather than blanket-idling it.

A concurrent-workspace `Sandbox` recycles rather than terminates, because concurrent mode is not one-session-only. When a concurrent-mode `Sandbox` in `slot_active` has zero live referencing claims and is healthy, the projection reconciles it back to `idle` along the `{SlotActive,Idle}` edge (`state.go:232`, the §6.2 line 164 `slot_active → idle` recycle), replacing the gateway's removed slot-release recycle write (`slotclaimer.go:680-688`). The concurrent-workspace drains at §6.2 lines 165-167 are written by the projection from the relocated triggers in Section 4.1: a `slot_active` `Sandbox` carrying the gateway-stamped `lenny.dev/drain-request` annotation (§6.2 line 165 unhealthy threshold) or exceeding `ConcurrentWorkspacePolicy.MaxPodUptimeSeconds` against its pod `CreationTimestamp` (§6.2 line 166) reconciles to `draining` along `{SlotActive,Draining}` (`state.go:233`), and an idle concurrent-workspace `Sandbox` exceeding the uptime cap (§6.2 line 167) reconciles to `draining` along `{Idle,Draining}` (`state.go:242`); from `draining` the existing `{Draining,Terminated}` edge retires the pod once its slots have drained.

The generic existence-driven projection emits `idle` only for a `Sandbox` currently in a warm-inventory phase (`warming`, `sdk_connecting`, `idle`) or a concurrent-mode `slot_active` `Sandbox` with zero live claims, and it never overwrites an in-flight `draining` or terminal phase. The watch is added at `controller.go:714-719` as `Watches(&SandboxClaim{}, ...)` with a handler that maps a claim to its referenced `Sandbox` via `.spec.sandboxRef`. The occupancy phase is computed in the `Sandbox` reconciler from the `executionMode`, the claim set, the claims' `status.phase`, the pod `CreationTimestamp` against the pool uptime cap, and the `lenny.dev/drain-request` Pod annotation; `syncPodStateLabel` (`:496-516`) keeps its role as the downstream label projection. The WarmPoolController RBAC paragraph at `spec/04_system-components.md:586` already grants `list`/`delete` on `SandboxClaim`; the projection adds `get`/`watch` so the controller can read individual claims and react to claim create, status, and delete events.

The net-new `SandboxClaim` watch is the only added reconcile-trigger source, and in concurrent mode the per-slot claims for one pod fan into the same `Sandbox` reconcile. The watch uses the controller-runtime rate-limited workqueue (the default `DefaultControllerRateLimiter`) so a burst of per-slot claim events for one `Sandbox` coalesces into bounded `Sandbox` reconciles rather than a hot loop, and `--max-concurrent-reconciles` is raised above the default 1 at Tier 3 (the §17.8.2 controller-tuning value) so occupancy reconcile keeps pace with the Tier 3 claim rate (~30 claims/s for a hot pool). `lenny_controller_workqueue_depth` ([§16.1](16_observability.md#161-metrics)) is the signal that the projection is falling behind. Double-claim correctness is unaffected by reconcile lag: the `lenny-sandboxclaim-guard` deterministic-name CREATE plus existing-claim scan is the admission-time gate (Section 4.3), and the occupancy projection is the eventual-consistency observer rather than the correctness boundary.

### 4.3 Admission guard accept-set after the enum coarsening

The `lenny-sandboxclaim-guard` CREATE single-claim check (deterministic `claim-<sessionID>` name plus existing-claim scan, `claimer.go:140-164`; `pkg/admission/sandboxclaim_guard/guard.go`) is authoritative and unchanged. The guard's PATCH/PUT stale-write check reads `Sandbox.status.phase` against `claimBoundPhases` (`guard.go:94-102`, `:259`), and the slot_active CREATE exemption reads it at `:230`. The webhook reads the phase generically via `referencedSandboxPhase` (`pkg/admission/webhook/sandboxclaim_guard.go:55-72`, `:110-122`), so no read site needs re-pointing.

The whole-pod candidate scan at `pkg/gateway/podclaim/claimer.go:92` reads `sb.Status.Phase != string(state.Idle)` to skip non-idle pods for session-mode and task-mode claims. Its predicate is unchanged: with the gateway no longer writing the `claimed` phase, the scan now reads the WarmPoolController-written occupancy projection (Section 4.2), which writes `claimed` for both a session-mode and a task-mode `Sandbox` holding a live claim, so a claimed pod of either mode still reads a non-`idle` phase and the scan skips it. The occupancy flip is now an async WarmPoolController reconcile rather than a synchronous gateway write, so the scan can briefly observe a pod as `idle` between the gateway's `SandboxClaim` CREATE and the controller's `claimed` write. The deterministic `claim-<sessionID>` CREATE and the existing-claim scan in the `lenny-sandboxclaim-guard` webhook (`guard.go:233-241`) remain the authoritative double-claim defense (`spec/04_system-components.md:384`): a second claim CREATE against an already-claimed pod is rejected at admission regardless of the occupancy phase the scan observed, so the async flip does not regress double-claim safety. The candidate scan is an availability optimization that selects a likely-idle pod; the CREATE guard is the correctness gate.

The guard's `claimBoundPhases` accept-set is reduced to the coarse bound phases `{claimed, slot_active}`. ### 4.6 The task-mode disposition: Redis counters, gateway-computed disposition, coarse retirement signal (F-5.2.30)

**The flow: adapter → gateway → Redis counters → gateway disposition.** A task-mode session runs multiple sequential tasks on one pod under one `SandboxClaim` (`claim-<sessionID>`, created once at session start; the SDK receives a new `TaskID` under the same `SessionID` after each `task_ready` signal, `spec/15_external-api-surface.md:1461`, and the pod cycles between `idle` and `attached` for up to `maxTasksPerPod` tasks, `spec/05_runtime-registry-and-pool-model.md:415,451`). The claim lifecycle is per session; the between-task disposition fires per task. On each task's completion the adapter runs the §5.2 scrub in-pod (`pkg/adapter/scrub`, adapter-internal and unchanged), producing a binary scrub `Result` (`succeeded` or `failed`, matching `pkg/adapter/scrub.Result`). The disposition (recycle the pod to the pool versus retire it) is computed gateway-side, where pod assignment and the task-dispatch loop already live:

1. The adapter reports this task's binary scrub `Result` to the gateway via the `ReportTaskCleanup` RPC on the existing adapter→gateway `GatewayControl` service (the `ExtendLease` Adapter→Gateway precedent in §4.7; `schemas/lenny-adapter.proto` service `GatewayControl`). The adapter sends it immediately after the in-pod scrub completes. The request carries the `sessionId` (so the gateway resolves the per-session binding claim `claim-<sessionID>` and the pod it backs, mirroring the `ExtendLease` precedent at `pkg/adapter/gatewaycontrol/gatewaycontrol.go:204-206`; `taskId` is distinct from `sessionId` in task mode, `spec/15_external-api-surface.md:1461`), the `taskId`, and the binary `outcome` (`succeeded`/`failed`). It carries no cumulative count: the gateway counts from the per-task signal itself (step 2). The response acks. The adapter reports the per-task scrub outcome only; the warn-versus-fail disposition is derived gateway-side from a failed scrub plus the pool `onCleanupFailure` policy, because the adapter does not know that policy. The RPC is idempotent: a retry with the same `taskId` is safe (the gateway records the last applied `taskId` and skips a re-increment for a `taskId` already counted).
2. The gateway counts each task itself: on the per-task report it increments the pod's Redis `tasksCompleted` counter by one and, on a failed scrub, the `scrubFailureCount` counter by one. The pod's word is not trusted for the count. This is the §11.2 proxy-mode posture (`spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:41`): the gateway-observed count is authoritative, written to Redis immediately, and a malicious or misconfigured runtime cannot influence the residual-state retirement bound (`maxTasksPerPod`/`maxScrubFailures`) by under-reporting, because the only signal the runtime supplies is this task's binary scrub outcome and the gateway dispatched every task. The gateway already increments these counters today (`pkg/gateway/taskdriver/taskdriver.go:158-160`); this design moves the counter store from gateway process memory to Redis while keeping the gateway as the authoritative incrementer. The counters are durably checkpointed to the per-pod `agent_pod_state` row in Postgres (the new nullable `tasks_completed`/`scrub_failure_count` columns) on the existing §11.2 sync interval (`quotaSyncIntervalSeconds`, `spec/11_policy-and-controls.md:44`), not per task, so the per-task control flow stays Redis-only with no per-task Postgres write. On a Redis reset the durable floor is the Postgres checkpoint: rehydration is `MAX(postgres_checkpoint, redis_current)`, gateway-authoritative, the same MAX rule §11.2 applies to proxy-mode quota recovery and the §12.4 durable-fallback rule requires (`spec/12_storage-architecture.md:199`); a stale checkpoint (up to one sync interval old) never resets the counter below actual usage, so a pod past `maxScrubFailures` stays retired after recovery. The pod self-report plays no part in the rehydration. The counters are distributed across gateway replicas through Redis, so any replica that handles a `ReportTaskCleanup` for the pod reads and updates the same counters, and any replica checkpoints to the same `agent_pod_state` row.
3. The gateway runs `taskcleanup.Decide` (a pure function with no Kubernetes client, `pkg/sandbox/taskcleanup/taskcleanup.go:18-23`, unchanged) against the Redis counters, the binary scrub outcome, the pod uptime from the pod `CreationTimestamp` it reads during the dispatch loop, and the pool `taskPolicy` (`maxTasksPerPod`, `maxScrubFailures`, `maxPodUptimeSeconds`, `onCleanupFailure`, the runtime `preConnect` capability) and `lenny.dev/host-schedulable` label. `Decide` returns the §6.2 between-task disposition: `idle` for a non-preConnect recycle, `sdk_connecting` for a preConnect re-warm so the returned idle pod is SDK-warm, `draining` when a retirement limit (`maxTasksPerPod`, `maxScrubFailures`, `maxPodUptimeSeconds`) is reached or the host node is unschedulable, and `failed` under `onCleanupFailure: fail` (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:147-156`).
4. On a recycle disposition (`idle` or `sdk_connecting`), the gateway keeps the live claim, re-warms the SDK in-pod when the disposition is `sdk_connecting`, and dispatches the session's next task. This is gateway data-plane state: the gateway makes no `Sandbox.status` or `SandboxClaim.status` write for the recycle, and the WarmPoolController occupancy projection continues to read the live claim as `claimed`. On a retirement disposition (`draining` or `failed`), the gateway crosses to the control plane through the existing claim-release path: it sets `SandboxClaim.status.phase` to `released` (for the `draining`/`task_count_limit`/`uptime_limit`/`scrub_failure_limit` retirements) or `failed` (for `onCleanupFailure: fail`), and the WarmPoolController occupancy projection (Section 4.2) drives the pod through `draining` to `terminated` along the existing `{Claimed,Draining}`/`{Draining,Terminated}` edges. The gateway also emits the §16.1 retirement and scrub metrics (`lenny_task_pod_retirement_total`, `lenny_task_pod_scrub_failure_count`, `lenny_task_scrub_failure_total`) from the same replica that ran `Decide`; these stay gateway-attributed in §5.2/§16.1.

**Why off the CRD.** The per-task recycle is the high-write hot path (one event per task across the whole task-mode fleet). Carrying its counters and disposition on `Sandbox.status` made every task a control-plane etcd write and routed every disposition through a single WarmPoolController leader. The §11.2 proxy-mode counter posture (gateway-authoritative measurement on the Redis fast-path with a periodic Postgres checkpoint) is the platform's already-shipped home for a high-write security counter that must survive a store outage, and computing the disposition where pod assignment and task dispatch already run removes the cross-component relay entirely. The control plane sees only the coarse retirement: a `released`/`failed` `SandboxClaim.status.phase` write at the rate of pod retirement (one per `maxTasksPerPod` tasks at most), not at the rate of task completion.

**The lost-report backstop.** A crashed or wedged adapter that never reports the scrub `Result` leaves the pod under a live claim. The gateway dispatches every task and holds a per-task dispatch deadline for it in coordinator memory, so a task that exceeds the per-pool `taskPolicy.cleanupTimeoutSeconds` plus a bounded grace without a `ReportTaskCleanup` is a gateway-side timeout: the gateway treats the missing report as a failed scrub. The gateway advances the pod's Redis `scrubFailureCount` by one (the per-task gateway-authoritative increment, the same operation a failed-scrub report drives), runs `Decide`, and (when the failure crosses a retirement limit or `onCleanupFailure: fail` applies) retires the pod via the claim-release path. This reuses the gateway's existing per-session dispatch tracking rather than a new control-plane watchdog. The in-memory deadline carries no Postgres write per dispatch or per report; it is checkpointed periodically (the next paragraph), so the per-task control flow stays Redis-only. The §4.7 `task_complete_ack_timeout` (30 seconds, `spec/04_system-components.md:708`) already bounds the adapter's wait for the runtime ack inside the pod, so the gateway-side timeout bounds only the adapter-to-gateway report.

The backstop survives a gateway crash. The dispatch deadline is per-coordinator in-memory state, so a coordinating-replica crash would otherwise leak a pod whose adapter is also dead while the session stays live (the orphan-GC reclaims a claim only after its session is no longer active in Postgres, `gc.go:166-180`, so a live-session pod with a dead adapter is outside the orphan-GC's reach). For the deadline to survive a coordinator crash it needs a durable home on the session row, and no such column exists today: the sessions table (`migrations/0001_initial_schema.up.sql:74-98` and every later `ALTER TABLE sessions` migration) carries no per-task dispatch-deadline, task-id, or task-progress column. This design stages a new nullable `task_dispatch_deadline TIMESTAMPTZ` column on the sessions row (the `migrations/0168_sessions_task_dispatch_deadline` migration in Section 13). The coordinator checkpoints the in-memory deadline to that column on the same periodic `quotaSyncIntervalSeconds` interval that checkpoints the task counters (Section 4.6 step 2), not per task, so the per-task control flow issues no per-task Postgres write for the deadline. The new coordinator re-arms the backstop on handoff by reading `task_dispatch_deadline` from the durable session row at handoff step 0, the same priming point that reads `last_seq` and `last_checkpoint_workspace_bytes` (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:32,399`); a checkpoint that is at most one sync interval stale advances the timeout by at most one sync interval, bounded the same way §11.2 bounds proxy-mode quota recovery staleness, and a stale-late checkpoint never shortens the deadline below the live value because the re-arm re-derives the deadline from the dispatch the coordinator resumes. The column is a per-session durable deadline directly analogous to `resume_eligible_until` (`migrations/0055_session_retry_policy_resume.up.sql`). A crashed-coordinator plus dead-adapter plus live-session pod is therefore retired by the re-armed timeout rather than leaked.

**Out of scope for this flow.** `maxTaskRetries` (pod crash mid-task) is session recovery: the gateway claims a fresh pod and re-dispatches the task from the beginning (§6.2 lines 185-192). That path stays gateway-side and is untouched here, because it is a recovery decision the gateway already owns rather than a between-task inventory disposition.

### 4.7 Mode-aware orphan-GC recycle

`ClaimGarbageCollector.returnToIdle` (`pkg/controller/warmpool/gc.go:205-253`) recycles a claimed pod to `idle` unconditionally when it reclaims an orphaned `SandboxClaim` (a claim older than the orphan timeout whose session is no longer active in Postgres, `gc.go:166-180`). Under the occupancy model this is a one-session-only violation: a session-mode pod orphaned by a gateway crash carries a settled session's residual state, and recycling it to `idle` hands it to the next claim, a cross-tenant-leak risk the §6.2 one-session-only invariant (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:24`) exists to prevent. Today the gateway's removed `b.drain` write was the retirement path for a settled session-mode pod; the orphan path is the same settle path when the gateway crashed before recording the disposition, so it must drain rather than idle.

The orphan-GC is made mode-aware. `returnToIdle` reads the backing `Sandbox.spec.executionMode` and:

- For a **session-mode** or **task-mode** pod, it drives the pod to `draining` (the terminal-disposition path the occupancy projection already drives, along the `{Claimed,Draining}` edge) rather than `idle`, so an orphaned exclusive pod is terminated and replaced rather than reused across sessions. The simplest implementation delegates to the mode-aware occupancy projection: the GC records the orphan disposition (or stamps the same drain signal the projection consumes) and lets the projection write `draining`, keeping a single writer of the drain transition.
- For a **concurrent-workspace** pod, it may still recycle to `idle`, because concurrent mode is not one-session-only and a healthy concurrent pod with no live slots is a legitimate idle pod (the §6.2 line 164 recycle).

The existing `returnToIdle` guard that skips a pod already `idle` or `draining` (`gc.go:215-218`) is retained. The orphan-claim reclaim condition the GC records (`gc.go:242-248`) stays WarmPoolController-owned, on the pod-lifecycle condition surface the controller keeps.

## 5. Why §10.3 and the gateway statelessness rule stay intact

The occupancy write is performed by the WarmPoolController from `lenny-system` with its existing apiserver access and its existing `Sandbox.status` write grants, so no agent-pod apiserver path is opened and the §10.3 zero-RBAC posture is unchanged. The `ReportTaskCleanup` relay does not open an agent-pod apiserver path either: the adapter dials the gateway's `GatewayControl` gRPC server (the same mTLS adapter→gateway link as `ExtendLease`), and only the gateway and the WarmPoolController touch the apiserver. The gateway's `lenny.dev/drain-request` annotation write for the §6.2 line 165 unhealthy drain uses the gateway's existing `patch` on `Pods` from `lenny-system`, so it opens no new RBAC surface. Moving the per-pod task counters from gateway process memory onto the §11.2 proxy-mode counter posture (Section 4.6) takes durable state off the gateway process, which the §10.1 statelessness rule (`spec/10_gateway-internals.md:5`) requires: the counters live in Redis with a periodic Postgres checkpoint to the per-pod `agent_pod_state` row as the durable fallback, the same Redis fast-path and periodic-Postgres-checkpoint mechanism the proxy-mode token-usage counters use, so any gateway replica reconstructs them on reconnect from Redis or the Postgres checkpoint (`MAX(postgres_checkpoint, redis_current)`). The gateway increments the counters itself from each task's per-task scrub outcome, so the residual-state bound is gateway-authoritative and the pod self-report is not trusted for the count. The `lenny.dev/drain-request` annotation is derived from the gateway's in-memory rolling fail-or-leak counter, which is the concurrent-mode counter rather than a task-mode counter and is left in place for the concurrent drain path.

### 7.2 `spec/04_system-components.md` §4.6.1 (occupancy projection, after the `SandboxClaim` admission webhook paragraph at line 384)

Anchor: the `lenny-sandboxclaim-guard` paragraph at `spec/04_system-components.md:384` describes the webhook reading `Sandbox.status.phase` via `.spec.sandboxRef`. Insert the following paragraph after it to define the occupancy projection:

```markdown
**Pod occupancy projection (`SandboxClaim`-existence-driven).** The WarmPoolController watches `SandboxClaim` resources and sets the occupancy value of `Sandbox.status.phase` as a level-triggered reconciliation of claim existence, claim disposition, the `Sandbox.spec.executionMode`, and concurrent-workspace lifecycle policy. The phase enum is coarse: the projection writes only `warming`, `idle`, `claimed`, `sdk_connecting`, `slot_active`, `draining`, `failed`, or `terminated`, and `failed` is the pod-lifecycle terminal the WarmPoolController writes for pre-attached pod death where no session exists. A never-bound warm `Sandbox` in a warm-inventory phase (`warming`, `sdk_connecting`, `idle`) referenced by no `SandboxClaim` reconciles to `idle`; a `Sandbox` referenced by one or more live `SandboxClaim` resources (a `status.phase` that is not a terminal binding phase, `bound` or `active`) reconciles to the occupancy value (`claimed` for a session-mode or task-mode pod, `slot_active` for a concurrent-workspace pod). A session-mode `Sandbox` whose referencing `SandboxClaim` has reached a terminal disposition (`status.phase` of `released` or `failed`) reconciles through its terminal phase to `draining` and then `terminated` along the legal pod-lifecycle edges, and is never returned to `idle`, preserving the one-session-only invariant in [Section 6.2](06_warm-pod-model.md#62-pod-state-machine) that a settled session-mode pod is terminated and replaced rather than recycled. A task-mode `Sandbox` reconciles to `claimed` while a live `SandboxClaim` references it; a task-mode session spans multiple sequential tasks on one pod under one claim, and the gateway keeps the claim live across the between-task boundary while it dispatches the next task, so the pod reads `claimed` for the duration of each task's execution. // ReportTaskCleanup is the adapter's report of one task's §5.2
```
```proto
// between-task scrub outcome to the gateway, so the gateway can count
// the task and compute the between-task disposition. The adapter has no
// Kubernetes client (spec §10.3), so it reports over the existing
// GatewayControl gRPC link (the ExtendLease precedent). The adapter
// sends it after the in-pod scrub completes, carrying only this task's
// binary scrub Result. The gateway counts the task itself: it
// increments the pod's Redis tasksCompleted by one and, on FAILED, the
// scrubFailureCount by one, on the §11.2 proxy-mode counter posture
// (gateway-authoritative measurement; the pod self-report is not
// trusted for the count, so a malicious runtime cannot under-report the
// maxTasksPerPod/maxScrubFailures bound). It then runs taskcleanup.Decide
// against the counters and the pool taskPolicy, and on a retirement
// records released/failed on SandboxClaim.status.phase. The adapter
// reports the binary scrub outcome only; the warn-versus-fail
// disposition is derived gateway-side from a FAILED scrub plus the pool
// onCleanupFailure policy via taskcleanup.Decide. Idempotent: a retry
// with the same task_id is safe (the gateway records the last applied
// task_id and skips a re-increment for a task_id already counted).
// spec: §4.7, §5.2, §6.2.
rpc ReportTaskCleanup(ReportTaskCleanupRequest) returns (ReportTaskCleanupResponse) {}

message ReportTaskCleanupRequest {
  // session_id identifies the task-mode session so the gateway can
  // resolve the per-session binding claim claim-<sessionID> and the pod
  // it backs. The SandboxClaim is named deterministically per session
  // (claimName(sessionID) = "claim-" + sessionID), and in task mode the
  // SDK receives a new TaskID under the same SessionID after each task
  // (spec §15.1), so task_id is distinct from session_id and the gateway
  // holds no taskId->session index. This mirrors the ExtendLease
  // precedent, whose request carries SessionId session_id = 1 and whose
  // handler resolves identity from it (pkg/gateway/leasecontrol:
  // sessionID := req.GetSessionId().GetValue()).
  SessionId session_id = 1;
  // task_id is the completed task's id. The gateway uses it for the
  // idempotency check (skip a re-increment for a task_id already
  // counted), so a retried report does not double-increment the counter.
  string task_id = 2;
  // outcome is the binary §5.2 scrub Result reported by the adapter for
  // this task: SUCCEEDED or FAILED, matching pkg/adapter/scrub.Result
  // (succeeded/failed). The gateway counts from this per-task signal: it
  // increments tasksCompleted by one per report and scrubFailureCount by
  // one on FAILED. The request carries no cumulative count — the gateway
  // is the authoritative counter (§11.2 proxy-mode posture), and the
  // durable fallback is the periodic Postgres checkpoint of the gateway's
  // own Redis counters to the per-pod agent_pod_state row, not a pod
  // self-report. The adapter reports the scrub outcome only; the
  // warn-versus-fail disposition is derived gateway-side from a FAILED
  // scrub plus the pool onCleanupFailure policy via taskcleanup.Decide.
  ScrubOutcome outcome = 3;
```

## 8. Non-goals

- Adding a tier-dependent or dual-mode code path. v1 ships one implementation; the gateway's `Sandbox.status` writers are deleted rather than gated.
- Adding a migration shim, legacy flag, or backward-compatibility path for the removed gateway carve-out. No deployments exist in the wild.
- Introducing a `/v1/tasks` ingestion route. Task-mode work is ingested through the existing session path against an `executionMode: task` pool (`spec/05_runtime-registry-and-pool-model.md:475`; `pkg/gateway/sessionserver/start.go`).
- Adding a new client-facing protocol surface. The cross-context seam is `SandboxClaim` existence (on the existing `SandboxClaim` CRD) and the `ReportTaskCleanup` RPC on the existing adapter→gateway `GatewayControl` service. Neither is client-facing: clients submit task-mode work through the session API and never see the report.
- Relaxing the §10.3 agent-pod zero-RBAC posture. No agent-pod apiserver path is opened; the WarmPoolController performs the `Sandbox.status` write from `lenny-system`.
- Capping concurrent slots from anything other than the §5.2 Redis counter. The respecified-fallback alternative (re-sourcing the fallback cap to an active-`SandboxClaim` count) was considered and rejected: the spec describes a single atomic-`INCR` cap, the `Counter==nil` branch is non-spec-aligned by its own code comments (`slotclaimer.go:220-223`, `cmd/lenny-gateway/main.go:2444-2447`), and adding a second cap path would reintroduce the dual-mode pattern the single-implementation rule excludes. The branch is deleted instead.
- Reducing the `Sandbox.status.phase` enum and relocating session/task-lifecycle authority to Postgres as part of this change. The second-writer removal does not require it; the relocation is a reviewer-owned open decision (Section 11) with its own blast radius.
- Touching `maxTaskRetries` (a pod crash mid-task). That path is session recovery rather than a between-task disposition: the gateway claims a fresh pod and re-dispatches the task from the beginning (`spec/06_warm-pod-model.md:185-192`). It stays gateway-side and is untouched.
- Moving the per-task counters or disposition onto the CRD. The counters live on the §11.2 proxy-mode counter posture (gateway-authoritative measurement on the Redis fast-path, with a periodic Postgres checkpoint to the per-pod `agent_pod_state` row as the durable fallback, reconciled by `MAX(postgres_checkpoint, redis_current)`) and the disposition is computed gateway-side, so the per-task hot path makes no etcd write and no per-task Postgres write. A CRD-resident `taskState` counter and a WarmPoolController-leader disposition were considered and rejected: they put per-task write amplification on etcd and routed every disposition through a single leader, against the platform's fault-isolation and the existing quota-counter precedent.
- Moving the binding off etcd. The session-to-pod binding stays the etcd `SandboxClaim`; it is not relocated to Redis or Postgres. The etcd binding survives a Postgres outage, anchors the `lenny-sandboxclaim-guard` double-claim webhook, and is the WarmPoolController's in-use signal for the drain handshake.
- Moving pod assignment to the WarmPoolController. Assignment stays on the gateway claim hot path (`spec/04_system-components.md:386`); the decomposition removes only the gateway's `Sandbox.status` writes, not its read-and-assign role.
