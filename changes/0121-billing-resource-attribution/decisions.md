# Decisions: 0121 Billing resource attribution

## Open decisions

The M3 readiness review of 2026-11-19 raised decisions 23 to 32. The records of BR-Q1 and O1, which the owner settled as decisions 18 and 17, are under "Records of BR-Q1 and O1" at the end of "Decisions".

23. **The shipped classes and the slot memory floor.** The shipped `small` class states 1Gi of memory for the whole pod. After decision 7's mapping subtracts `netagent`'s 128 MiB request, `keepalive`'s request, and `adapterResources`, a single slot keeps less than 896 MiB, which is below the 1088 MiB that **Checks at load.** requires (the 1Gi floor of [WM O1](../0153-workspace-materialization/decisions.md#open-decisions) plus the 64Mi tmpfs reservation). The gateway and the controller would refuse to start on the chart defaults, and the embedded stack's echo pool uses `small` (`pkg/embedded/stack/agentplacement.go`). `medium` (2Gi) passes with one slot and fails the pool-write check with two, and `large` (4Gi) fails it with four. How do the shipped classes and the floor fit together?
   - (a) Move the floor check from load to pool write and reconcile, and resize `small` so that a one-slot pool passes. WM D11 and PL-5 state that pool and resource-class validation refuses a class below the floor, so `0153-workspace-materialization` and `0118-agent-pod-layout` change in the same step.
   - (b) Keep the check at load, and resize `small` so that one slot passes (at least 1088 MiB plus 128 MiB, `adapterResources`, and `keepalive`'s request), or drop `small` and point the embedded stack at `medium`.
   - (c) Lower the default of `runtimes.minSlotMemoryBytes`.

   **Recommendation: (b), with `small` resized.** Moving the check under (a) does not remove the need to resize `small`, because the embedded echo pool uses `small` with one slot and would be refused at pool write, and (a) departs from the settled words of WM D11 and PL-5. (c) pre-empts the validation on managed nodes that WM D11 waits for. The review recommended (a), and its verification recommended resizing under either. Under every option, "Resource classes in configuration" gains a table of each shipped class's per-slot shares at one, two, and four slots. Blocks 0121.S1, and through it 0121.S2 to 0121.S7, 0137.S1, 0145.S4, and 0153.S7. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0121-F1)

24. **Periodic events and the billing fallbacks.** While Postgres refuses billing writes, the read-back rule of **Emitter progress.** re-appends every pending window on every emitter cycle. Each append lands in the tenant's Redis billing stream, which the tree trims at about 50,000 entries (`DefaultStreamMaxLen` in `pkg/billing/billingstore/failover/redisstream`) whether or not the flusher has inserted them, or, when Redis is also down, in the bounded in-memory buffer. The duplicates evict staged token usage and session events, which §11.2.1 states are never dropped, and a full buffer makes the replica refuse session-progressing requests with `503`. How do the periodic events use the fallbacks?
   - (a) The emitter writes periodic events to the Postgres primary alone, treats a primary failure as a retry on the next cycle, and never routes a periodic event to the Redis stream or the in-memory buffer. The source rows are durable, and billing sinks publish after the Postgres write, so no sink is bypassed. This departs from the reasoning recorded under decision 9, whose rejected alternative cites a writer "outside the gateway's Redis fallback and billing sinks".
   - (b) Keep the fallbacks, record on each progress row that an append was staged, and skip that window until the read-back finds it or the flusher reports the entry gone.

   **Recommendation: (a).** It removes the duplicate appends and the staged copies of periodic events with no new state. Under (a), the `chaos` case of a replica crash with a periodic event in the in-memory buffer becomes a case of the primary-only path. Blocks 0121.S2, and the emitters of 0121.S3, 0121.S4, 0121.S6, and 0121.S7. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0121-F2)

25. **The platform tenant in Postgres.** `owned_rollout` idle minutes (decision 16) and the platform-owned `platform_chunks` packs (decision 20) are attributed to the platform tenant, `platform`. The tree has no `tenants` row and no billing sequence for it: migration `0174_default_tenant_billing_audit_sequences` creates a billing sequence for `default` and describes `platform` as a pseudo-tenant used only for the platform-admin audit chain, `billing_events` and `artifact_store` both reference `tenants(id)`, and `pkg/billing/billingstore/pgstore` draws `nextval` on the tenant's billing sequence. How does the platform tenant exist so that billing events and ledger rows can name it?
   - (a) A reserved `tenants` row and a billing sequence created by migration, excluded from tenant lists, authentication, quota, erasure, and tenant deletion, as `__preflight__` and the `platform` audit sequence are today. A `platform-admin` reads its events with `?tenant_id=platform`, and the reconciliation lists the `/platform/` prefix under which `0131-incremental-checkpoint` stores platform packs.
   - (b) A separate operator-cost table and stream outside `billing_events`.

   **Recommendation: (a).** It keeps one stream and one reader, which decisions 16 and 20 assume. Blocks 0121.S3 (`owned_rollout`), 0121.S4 (`platform_chunks`), and 0131.S4. The question is shared with `0131-incremental-checkpoint` ("Quota") and `0140-runtime-revisions` (RV-3, decision D8), and `0149` distinguishes its operator tenant from the platform tenant. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0121-F3)

26. **A pod held during an interrupt suspension.** **Carrier.** counts suspend as a release, and the close rule under **Durability.** closes a window at the suspend transition. A session suspended by an interrupt keeps its pod for `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds` (900 s by default, §6.2), and under `0146-session-lifecycle` for longer when the checkpoint fails. Neither `session.pod_usage` nor `pool.idle_usage` counts that pod during the hold. Who pays for it?
   - (a) The session's tenant, in `session.pod_usage`, until the pod is released: suspend leaves the list of releases, the window closes at the release that ends the hold, and the close rule drops the suspend transition.
   - (b) The operator, with the window closed at the suspend transition and the hold reported in no event.

   **Recommendation: (a).** The tenant's session holds the pod, and decision 1 prices what a tenant holds. Blocks 0121.S2. The question touches the suspension steps of `0146-session-lifecycle`. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0121-F5)

27. **The adapter's and `keepalive`'s chart values, and the memory limits of the mapping.** **Container mapping.** names `adapterResources` as a chart value that the chart does not carry and gives no default, and it gives `keepalive` "its own minimal chart request" with no key and no default. It gives each slot "a memory request and limit of the remaining memory", while a class states `requests.memory` and `limits.memory` separately and `netagent` has a 128 MiB request and a 512 MiB limit. What are the chart keys and defaults, and which class figure bounds the containers' memory limits?
   - (a) `adapter.resources` and `keepalive.resources` as chart keys, in the spelling of `netagent.resources` and `storeFill.resources`, with defaults the owner sets. Each slot's memory request is the class's `requests.memory` less the other containers' requests, divided by the slot count, and each slot's memory limit is the class's `limits.memory` less the other containers' limits, divided by the slot count, so the containers' memory limits sum to the class's `limits.memory`.
   - (b) The same keys, with each slot's memory request and limit both taken from `requests.memory` less the other containers' requests, as written today, so the pod's effective memory limit exceeds the class's `limits.memory` by the platform containers' limit headroom (384 MiB for `netagent` alone).

   **Recommendation: (a).** It keeps decision 7's "a class states the pod's total" true for limits as well as requests. The defaults of both values need the owner's figures. Blocks 0121.S1 and 0145.S4. The arithmetic is shared with SI-9 of `0145-session-isolation`, which states the same rule and does not name `keepalive`, and `keepalive`'s request comes from decision 25 of `0128-forensics-evidence-preservation`, which says only "a minimal request". (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0121-F6)

28. **The future of `billing.dualControlThreshold`.** Corrections of this design's event types always take dual control. `0133-llm-upstreams-and-cost-budgets` removes `tokens_input` and `tokens_output` from `token_usage.checkpoint` and puts operator corrections of `llm_usage` under `billing.dualControlCreditThreshold`, and it states that `billing.dualControlThreshold` "keeps tokens and pod minutes". With both changes applied, no corrected field remains for that threshold to sum, and 0133's sentence can be read as putting `session.pod_usage` corrections under the threshold, which contradicts this design's rule. Is the threshold retired?
   - (a) Retire `billing.dualControlThreshold`, and state in §11.2.1 one dual-control rule per event type: the credit threshold for `llm_usage`, and dual control always for this design's event types.
   - (b) Keep it with an explicit list of the fields it sums.

   Decision 22 of 0133 since removed `llm_usage` and `billing.dualControlCreditThreshold`, so the credit threshold of option (a) no longer exists: option (a) retires `billing.dualControlThreshold` with dual control always for this design's event types.

   **Recommendation: (a)**, when no field remains for it to sum. Blocks the §11.2.1 dual-control edits of 0121.S2 and of the step of `0133-llm-upstreams-and-cost-budgets` that edits **Correction semantics**. The other change records the same question. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: connectors-llm-billing-C7, 0121-F13)

29. **Operating rules of the accrual and the emitter.** The design leaves these rules unstated:
   - (i) A controller started without a Postgres connection when billing runs. (a) It refuses to start. (b) It starts and accrues nothing, so every minute reaches `unsampled_minutes`. Recommendation: (a), as the review proposes.
   - (ii) The emitter's cycle length, which decision 9 names only as "one emitter cycle". Recommendation: a chart value, such as `billing.emitterIntervalSeconds`, with a default the owner sets.
   - (iii) The shortest interval between two accrual writes for one subject, so that a busy pool does not write one row per reconcile. Recommendation: a chart value, such as `billing.idleAccrualMinSampleSeconds`, with a default the owner sets.
   - (iv) The clock of a sample time. Recommendation: Postgres's clock in the conditional update, so that two leaders do not disagree on time.
   - (v) A window whose counts and `unsampled_minutes` are all zero. Recommendation: no event.
   - (vi) How often `lenny_resource_class_undefined_total{pool}` counts. Recommendation: once per transition of a pool into `ResourceClassUndefined`, rather than once per reconcile.
   - (vii) A class removed from configuration that a stored runtime's `allowedResourceClasses` or `defaultPoolConfig.resourceClass` still names. Recommendation: the controller reports the runtime under `ResourceClassUndefined`, and the write check refuses new pools that name the class.

   Blocks 0121.S1 for (vi) and (vii), 0121.S2 for (ii), and 0121.S3 and 0121.S7 for (i), (iii), (iv), and (v). (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0121-F11 item 9, 0121-F19, 0121-F23)

   - (b) The report stays uncorrected and the documentation states it, and the task record's `podMinutes` stays a separate figure.

## Decisions

The owner has settled decisions 1 to 22, and the design above is written to them. Decisions 6 to 22 each state the settled option, its cost, and the alternative the owner rejected.

- **Decision 1: pricing by actual resources.** Settled. Billing lets an operator price usage by actual resources, and events record the resource class name and the pod's actual resources, because an operator can redefine a class at any time.
- **Decision 2: idle billing for owned pools.** Settled. A periodic billing event reports a tenant's owned pools' idle and reserved pod minutes, attributed to the owning tenant. This is `pool.idle_usage`. Decision 11 extends it to pinned idle pods on shared pools.
- **Decision 3: storage usage.** Settled, and amended by the owner with decision 10. A periodic `storage.usage` event per tenant and artifact type from the `artifact_store` ledger, as bytes held and byte-hours, with a daily reconciliation against the object store that reports differences as a metric and an alert and emits `billing_correction` where appropriate. The design states the cases the ledger misses, bills pod ephemeral disk by its request with sampled use, and leaves forensic escrow copies to the operator under [forensics decision 16](../0128-forensics-evidence-preservation/decisions.md#decisions). The amendment, in the owner's words "bill reserved disk only", bills pod ephemeral disk by its reserved request and removes sampled use. The forensic escrow copies this decision names are the security-hold copies, and decision 14 bills the separate §12.8 legal-hold escrow copies.
- **Decision 4: resource classes in configuration.** Settled, in the owner's words "Ability to override the default classes is important. In fact, I think there shouldn't be any values hardcoded in the code." The code holds no class, the chart ships `small`, `medium`, and `large` as defaults an operator changes or replaces, the embedded stack carries the same defaults, a pool naming an undefined class is refused at write, the controller checks each class against the tmpfs reservation, and the specification states the classes as chart defaults and defines the override.
- **Decision 5: resource fields on session usage.** Settled. Session usage events carrying `pod_minutes` gain the class name, the pod's actual CPU and memory requests and limits at the time, the isolation profile, and, for a pod serving several sessions, its `maxConcurrentSessions`. The field is `maxConcurrentSessionsPerPod` since the owner's rename of 2026-11-12.

### Records of BR-Q1 and O1

These entries stood under "Open decisions" until the M3 readiness review of 2026-11-19 moved them here. The owner settled them as decisions 18 and 17.

**BR-Q1. A class's own ephemeral storage request.** The checkpoint design's decision D3, settled on 2026-11-13, sets the pod's ephemeral storage request equal to the sum of its disk-backed volumes' size limits ([IC decision D3](../0131-incremental-checkpoint/decisions.md#decisions)). A class may state an optional `requests.ephemeral-storage` ("Fields" under "Resource classes in configuration"), and no decision states whether that request adds to the sum.

- (a) The class's request adds to the sum. It covers the containers' writable layers and logs, which no volume's size limit counts. The shipped classes state none, so at the defaults the request equals the sum, and the totals under "Ephemeral storage" hold.
- (b) The request equals the sum exactly, and a class carries no ephemeral storage fields. The writable layers and logs then have no reservation on the node.

**Recommendation: (a).** The kubelet counts the writable layers and logs against the node's ephemeral storage, and under (b) the scheduler reserves nothing for them. (a) gives the same request as (b) for every shipped class and gives the operator one field to reserve that space. (b) follows the wording of decision D3 literally and removes two fields from the class.

**Settled (2026-11-13): (a)** (decision 18). The owner answered "yes ~1 GiB" and asked whether the value is configurable, and then "ok; the ~1 GiB is a field on each resource class". Each resource class carries its own `requests.ephemeral-storage`, 1 GiB in the shipped classes and configurable per class, added on top of the volume sum. The shipped classes now state a request, so the totals under "Ephemeral storage" include it.

**O1. The carrier of dedicated node capacity (BR-7).**

- (a) A new periodic event, `node.dedicated_usage`, per tenant, node class, and window, emitted by the gateway's leader-elected emitter from a `dedicated_node_accrual` table that the WarmPoolController fills by sampling labelled nodes, as it accrues idle pods for `pool.idle_usage`.
- (b) A new `pool_ownership`-like subject on `pool.idle_usage` that adds the unrequested capacity of a tenant's dedicated nodes to its idle figure.
- (c) A per-tenant metric with no billing event, which the operator prices from monitoring.

**Recommendation: (a).** The quantity is node capacity, which belongs to no pool, so (b) would attribute a node to a pool arbitrarily when a tenant's dedicated nodes serve several pools.
