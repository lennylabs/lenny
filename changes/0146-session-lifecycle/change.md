## Problem

"Terms", after "Scope", defines the vocabulary this document uses.

Revision 9 of the message delivery design made a child session behave as a top-level session whose client is its parent. A child no longer ends when it answers: it ends its turn and waits for its client's next message, as a top-level session does. That change reaches the session state machine in ways revision 9 did not state, and the review of 2026-11-11 found them:

- A session that waits for its client is `running` today, and nothing in its state says that no turn is open. Revision 9 removed `input_required`, the only state that said a session was waiting.
- `suspended` covers an interrupt the runtime acknowledged, an interrupt that timed out, and an idle suspension, told apart by a reason field on the session row ([§6.2](../../spec/06_warm-pod-model.md#62-pod-state-machine)) **[source]**. What may wake the session and what a resume restores depend on that field, so the state hides lifecycle meaning.
- Any message resumes a `suspended` session, "whatever its `delivery` value and whichever source sent it" ([§7.2](../../spec/07_session-lifecycle.md#72-interactive-session-model), delivery path 6) **[source]**. A child's message would therefore wake a parent its client had suspended.
- An interrupt "does NOT cascade" to children, and the specification leaves it to the runtime to pass one on (§6.2) **[source]**. Under revision 9 a runtime can only do that if its model calls a tool, so a client that suspends a session leaves its children working.
- A child closed by its parent has no defined terminal state or result. `POST /terminate` records `completed` and `DELETE` records `cancelled` ([§15.1](../../spec/15_external-api-surface.md#151-rest-api)) **[source]**, and nothing reads the difference inside the gateway.
- When a parent ends, `cascadeOnFailure` decides its children's fate ([§8.10](../../spec/08_recursive-delegation.md#810-delegation-tree-recovery)) **[source]**. Its `await_completion` and `detach` values assume a child that ends by itself, and a child that now waits for a client that no longer exists would wait until `cascadeTimeoutSeconds`.
- A parent that waits for its children ends its turn. The rule that keeps a parent from idle suspension counts only an `await_children` call as activity (§6.2) **[source]**, so a parent whose children work would release its pod and pay a restore for each child event.

This design states one session lifecycle that a top-level session and a child share, with the transitions, the suspension of a tree, the close rule, the cascade when a parent ends, and the admission rules over the new states.

## Scope

**Added:**

- The states `idle`, `dormant`, and `blocked` ("SL-1").
- Tree suspension: suspending a session suspends its subtree ("SL-4", L2), and a recovering top-level session can be suspended ("SL-4", L18).
- A suspension that releases the pod at once when the runtime does not acknowledge it, after the gateway refuses and cancels the session's model and platform calls and halts the slot's egress, and the rule that frames from a paused session open no turn ("SL-3", L15, L24).
- A resume notice that restarts the work a suspension or a pod loss cut, and a report to the session's client that a pod loss cut the turn ("SL-3", L8, L16).
- The close rule, which derives `completed` or `cancelled` from what happened to the session's work ("SL-7", L9, L21).
- Child events for a child in `awaiting_client_action` and for a child the root's client suspends directly ("SL-8", L7).
- Redelivery of `ended` and end-of-turn child events after a parent's restore ("SL-12", L19), and the recovery rules of a tree whose sessions recover with no order between them, which decision 15 of [`0166-delegation-primitives`](../0166-delegation-primitives/decisions.md#decisions) assigns to this change ("SL-12").
- The hand-off of a root that releases a child with `handoff: true`, with the successor it names ("SL-7", decisions 21, 32, and 33 of 0166).
- The reach of the cascade, a force-terminate, a tree terminate, and a suspension over a session that left its parent by `session.transfer` or `session.release` ("SL-4", "SL-9", decisions 32 and 46 of 0166).

**Changed:**

- `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/interrupt` becomes `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/suspend`, and the MCP tool `interrupt_session` becomes `suspend_session` ("SL-4", L5).
- `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/terminate` becomes `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/close` under the close rule, and the MCP tool `terminate_session` becomes `close_session` ("SL-7", L9).
- Which messages wake a waiting or paused session, and how long a message held for one lives ("SL-5", L3).
- The idle-suspension rule, which becomes the move to `dormant` from `idle`, or from `running` with the open turn cut, and which keeps an idle parent's pod while a descendant works ("SL-6", L11, L17).
- The `interrupt_request` timeout, at which the adapter no longer writes a session state ("SL-3").
- `cascadeOnFailure` becomes `onParentEnd`, with the values `close_all` and `let_finish` ("SL-9", L10).
- The concurrency caps and the resume admission check, restated over the new states, and the budget-key re-arm, which retires under 0170 ("SL-10", L20, L36).
- The A2A state mapping, with a `paused` metadata flag for the paused states, and the states that the MCP Tasks projection of [`0134-mcp-protocol-upgrade`, "MP-4"](../0134-mcp-protocol-upgrade/change.md#mp-4-tasks) maps ("SL-11", L22).
- A session whose slot volume passes the disk watch's threshold ends `failed` ("SL-2").

**Removed:**

- The `input_required` sub-state, which revision 9 of the message delivery design removes with `lenny/request_input`, and every specification rule that names it ("SL-11").
- The suspension-reason field of the session row ("SL-1").
- The `detach` cascade value (L6).
- The subtree deadlock detector, its `deadlock_detected` event, `maxDeadlockWaitSeconds`, and `DEADLOCK_TIMEOUT`, because no wait on another session remains for it to detect ("SL-8").

**Out of scope:**

- What a message delivers and the frames that carry it, which [`0135-message-delivery`](../0135-message-delivery/change.md#md-22-parents-and-children) states in "MD-22" and "MD-24".
- A tool with which a parent suspends a child. v1 has none (L12).
- The session grant, its caveats, the platform operations with which a runtime creates and manages its children, `wakeOn`, `events.peek`, and the two ways a session leaves its parent, which [`0166-delegation-primitives`](../0166-delegation-primitives/change.md) states. This change cites them and states the lifecycle rules 0166 assigns to it in "DP-7".
- The admin tenant suspension of `POST /v1/admin/tenants/{id}/suspend`, which drains a tenant's sessions and is unrelated to session suspension (§15.1) **[source]**.

## High-level design

Every session, top-level or child, goes through the same states. A session is `running` while it works on a turn and `idle` once the turn ends, waiting for its client. If nothing happens on the session for a while, in a turn or between turns, the gateway saves the workspace and gives the pod back, and the session becomes `dormant`. A turn that was open is cut. The session still waits for its client, and its next message brings it back after a restore.

A client can suspend a session. The session stops its current turn and pauses, and so does every session below it, which becomes `blocked`. A runtime that cannot be told to stop, or does not stop, loses its pod at once. Only the root's client can lift a suspension, by resuming the session or sending it a message. A child's message never wakes a paused session, and messages held for a paused session wait for it. When the tree resumes, each session whose turn the suspension cut receives a notice and carries on with its work. A session restored after a pod loss receives the same notice, and its client learns that the turn was lost.

A session ends in one of these ways: the runtime says it is complete, its client closes it, its client cancels it, a limit expires it, or it fails. A close records `completed` when the session's last turn finished, with that turn's message as its result, and `cancelled` when the close or an earlier suspension or failure cut its work off. When a parent ends, its children are closed at once by default, or allowed to finish the turn they are on. A force-terminate of a session, or a tree terminate, closes every session below it at once. A root that hands its work to one of its children ends, and the child it released becomes its successor, to which its client sends the next message.

The rest of this section states each part in the design's terms.

**States (SL-1).** `running` has an open turn. `idle` waits with its pod, and `dormant` waits without it. `suspended` is a session the root's client paused, and `blocked` is a session paused under a suspended ancestor. Setup, recovery, `awaiting_client_action`, and the terminal states keep their meaning. `input_required` and the suspension-reason field leave.

**Transitions (SL-2).** One table lists every transition with its event.

**Suspension and the open turn (SL-3).** A suspension stops the open turn, so a paused session never holds an open turn, and frames a paused session writes change nothing. A runtime that does not acknowledge the stop has its slot's egress halted, its workspace checkpointed, and its pod released at once. On resume, and after a restore that a pod loss caused, a session whose turn was cut receives a resume notice that opens a turn.

**Tree suspension (SL-4).** `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/suspend` records the suspension on the session and moves each live descendant to `blocked`. A session that becomes live under a paused ancestor lands in `blocked`. Only the root's client lifts a suspension.

**Messages (SL-5).** Any message wakes a waiting session. A paused session wakes only for the root's client, and holds every other message until the session ends.

**Idle pods (SL-6).** A session with no activity for `maxClientIdleSeconds` becomes `dormant`: an `idle` session unless one of its descendants is `running`, and a `running` session with its turn cut.

**Closing (SL-7).** A session has one close operation, the gateway derives its terminal state, and `DELETE` stays an explicit cancel. A root that releases a child with `handoff: true` ends with that child as its successor.

**Parents and children (SL-8).** The parent's state and its `wakeOn` decide when child events reach it, and the child's state decides which child events the parent receives.

**When a parent ends (SL-9).** The parent's inbox closes, then `onParentEnd` closes its children at once (`close_all`) or lets each finish its turn (`let_finish`). A force-terminate and a tree terminate close every descendant whatever `onParentEnd` holds. A transferred session stays within its root's reach, and a released session leaves it.

**Admission, caps, and timers (SL-10).** The caps count every non-terminal state except `dormant`, `suspended`, and `blocked`, and the resume admission check runs whenever one of those states moves toward a pod.

**External mappings (SL-11).** `idle` and `dormant` map to A2A `input-required`, and `suspended` and `blocked` map to `working` with `metadata.paused: true`. The MCP Tasks projection belongs to MP-4 of the MCP protocol upgrade design: each message a client sends is one task, which completes at the end of the turn that consumes it, so a waiting session has no task in `input_required` (L32).

**Recovery (SL-12).** Each session of a tree recovers on its own, with no order between them. After a parent's restore, the gateway resends each `ended` and end-of-turn child event the parent consumed after its checkpoint, each question still pending at the parent, each `awaiting_client_action` event of a child still in that state, and each open call to a tool the parent offers, and it then tells the parent's runtime which children it has.

```
  setup ──start──> running ──end-of-turn message──> idle ──maxClientIdleSeconds──> dormant
                     ▲  │                            │                               ▲  │
                     │  └── maxClientIdleSeconds, turn cut ──────────────────────────┘  │
                     └────── message, or output ─────┘<──── message (after restore) ────┘

  running, idle, dormant ──root's client suspends this session──> suspended
  running, idle, dormant ──root's client suspends an ancestor───> blocked
  suspended ──resume, or message from the root's client──> idle (or restore first)
  blocked   ──the suspension root resumes─────────────────> idle (or restore first)

  running ──complete: true──> completed
  any non-terminal state ──close──> completed (last turn finished) or cancelled (work cut off)
                         ──cancel──> cancelled     ──limit──> expired     ──failure──> failed
```

## Requirements

| ID | Requirement |
| :-- | :-- |
| SLR-1 | A top-level session and a child session have the same states and the same transitions. A child's session grant narrows what it may do and changes no transition. |
| SLR-2 | A session is in exactly one state at a time, and the state alone decides what may wake it, whether it counts against the concurrency caps, and whether it holds an open turn. |
| SLR-3 | No paused state holds an open turn, and no frame that a paused session's runtime writes opens a turn or changes the session's state. |
| SLR-4 | When the root's client suspends a session, every descendant in `running`, `idle`, or `dormant` is `blocked` before the suspension call returns. A session that becomes live while it has a paused ancestor, at a setup commit, a restore commit, or a resume that the root's client did not request for that session, lands in `blocked` under its nearest paused ancestor. |
| SLR-5 | No message from a session in the tree, and no system message, moves a session out of `suspended` or `blocked`. |
| SLR-6 | Resuming a suspension root resumes, top-down, every descendant `blocked` under it that resume admission admits, and no descendant in `suspended`. A descendant that admission refuses stays `blocked`, and so does every session `blocked` under it (L25). |
| SLR-7 | A session whose turn a suspension or a pod loss cut receives exactly one resume notice, before any message held for it, unless a message from the root's client to that session lifted the suspension, or the resume or the restore delivers the cut turn's causing message to the runtime again: after the closing frame of a stopped turn left it unconsumed, or after a restore whose redelivery the adapter's deduplication did not drop. |
| SLR-8 | A close records `completed` only when no turn is open, no call of the session to a tool its client offers is open, and the session's last turn ended with an end-of-turn `message` that carries no `error`, and otherwise `cancelled`. A cancel always records `cancelled`. |
| SLR-9 | An `idle` session never releases its pod while one of its descendants is `running`. |
| SLR-10 | At a parent's terminal transition, the parent's inbox closes before its children are closed, so no child event opens a turn in a terminal session. |
| SLR-11 | Every `ended` and end-of-turn child event a parent consumed after the checkpoint it resumes from reaches the restored parent again, and so does each question still pending at the parent, each `awaiting_client_action` event of a child still in that state, and each open call to a tool the parent offers, each with its original id. |
| SLR-12 | The concurrency caps count exactly the non-terminal sessions outside `dormant`, `suspended`, and `blocked`, and every move from one of those states toward a pod passes the resume admission check. |
| SLR-13 | A paused session's platform tool calls that act on another session are refused, so a runtime that did not stop at the suspension starts and steers no session. |
| SLR-14 | A message held for a `dormant`, `suspended`, or `blocked` session expires only when the session ends, and a child event never expires while its parent lives. |
| SLR-15 | A suspension root that ends keeps every descendant `blocked` under it, and only a timeout or a cascade ends them. |
| SLR-16 | A paused session that loses its pod stays in its paused state without a pod, and its restore waits for its resume. |
| SLR-17 | A session in `running` or `idle` with no qualifying activity for `maxClientIdleSeconds` becomes `dormant`, with any open turn cut, or `expired` when it cannot become `dormant`. An `idle` session with a `running` descendant is the exception. |
| SLR-18 | A suspension that the runtime does not acknowledge, or cannot, releases the session's pod without a hold once its checkpoint succeeds, and at the latest at `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds` (L24). |
| SLR-19 | When a pod loss cuts a turn and the restore does not deliver the turn's causing message to the runtime again, the session's client receives exactly one report that the turn was lost. |
| SLR-20 | A tree suspension that the gateway recorded on the suspension root reaches every live descendant, after a coordinator crash as well. |
| SLR-21 | A descendant in `awaiting_client_action` does not expire while an ancestor is suspended. |
| SLR-22 | Only the authenticated principal that owns the root acts as the root's client, whatever a message's `from` member names. A principal that carries a session claim, which the gateway gives a runtime's platform tool calls, is never the root's client, even when its subject owns the root. |
| SLR-23 | Once the gateway finds that a suspension cannot take hold, it admits no new platform call of the session from the pod in use, it cancels those in flight, and it halts the slot's egress, which stops its model calls, until that pod is released or, for the halt, until `UnsealSession` resumes the session on that pod. A restored pod carries no refusal and no halt (L24, CR-4). |
| SLR-24 | A turn stopped by a suspension consumes only the message ids its closing frame lists, and every other `delivered` message of the session returns to `pending` (L26). |
| SLR-25 | A close and a cancel apply the session's `onParentEnd` to its children, as every terminal transition does. |
| SLR-26 | Each turn end, each commit to `running` or `idle`, and each adoption of a session in `running` or `idle` is a delivery point for held messages. The first commit to `idle` after a restore, at the restore's commit or, for a restore that landed in a paused state, at the resume, is the redelivery point for `delivered` messages. |
| SLR-27 | A question or a tool-use approval pending when a suspension stops the session's turn is withdrawn before the paused state's write returns. No later answer or decision reaches the session, and no timeout or unattended policy answers a withdrawn approval (L28, L29). |
| SLR-28 | When a `blocked` session ends, every session whose `paused_ancestor_id` names it is re-pointed to the ended session's own `paused_ancestor_id`, so no `blocked` session is left under a terminal session that is not `ended_suspended`. |
| SLR-29 | Each session of a tree recovers on its own, and no restore waits for another session's. A child that commits out of `resume_pending` or `resuming` into any state other than `running` answers a pending `events.wait` that covers it. |
| SLR-30 | A root that releases a child with `handoff: true` ends with `successorSessionId` naming the released session, its event stream ends with a `handoff` event naming the successor, and every later message to it is refused with `409 SESSION_HANDED_OFF` naming the successor. |

## Current state

### Specification

**States [source].** §7.2 lists the session states `created`, `finalizing`, `ready`, `starting`, `running`, `suspended`, `resume_pending`, `resuming`, `awaiting_client_action`, `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled`, and `expired`, with `input_required` as a sub-state of `running`. It lists `running → completed (agent finishes)` with no signal that causes it.

**Suspension [source].** §6.2 "`suspended` state" lists `running → suspended` on `interrupt_request` with `interrupt_acknowledged`, on the `interrupt_request` deadline, at which "adapter forces suspended", and when `maxClientIdleSeconds` elapses, the idle suspension, with a hold of zero. The session row records why a session is suspended: `InterruptAcknowledged`, `InterruptTimeout`, or `Idle` ("Graceful pod release during extended suspension"). A session suspended by an interrupt holds its pod for `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds`, 900 seconds by default, and a session suspended for idleness releases it as it is suspended. `maxSessionAge` and `maxClientIdleSeconds` pause during `suspended`, and `gateway.maxSuspendedSessionSeconds`, 604,800 seconds by default, expires a suspended session ("`suspended → expired` trigger mechanism"). `maxClientIdleSeconds` defaults to 900 seconds. "`interrupt_request` does NOT cascade to children. ... Runtime decides whether to propagate a received interrupt to its children." The specification does not say whether an interrupt ends the open turn. §15.4.3's `Interrupt` row says that below Full level "the runtime process receives no signal". The §28.5.3 `interrupt_request` row and the `CH-RUNTIMEOPS` card's **Degradation.** say that at the deadline the "adapter transitions the session to `suspended` anyway". "Pod failure while `suspended`" restores a suspended session that loses its held pod through §7.3 and lands it in `running`.

**Waking a suspended session [source].** §7.2 delivery path 6: "the message resumes the session, whatever its `delivery` value and whichever source sent it". **Backlog resume.** resumes a session suspended for idleness that holds any message in its DLQ or inbox, and a session suspended for another reason that holds a DLQ message accepted after its suspension.

**Held messages [source].** The §7.2 dead-letter table gives a message held for a recovering session a TTL of `maxResumeWindowSeconds`, 900 seconds by default, and scores each DLQ entry by its acceptance time plus that TTL.

**Idle activity [source].** §6.2 runs the `maxClientIdleSeconds` clock in `running`, with or without an open turn, and resets it on qualifying events: a client message the adapter writes to the runtime, an approval decision, an elicitation answer, or an attach; `agent_output` and `tool_use` events; each `lenny/await_children` invocation and each partial result of its stream ("This ensures that a parent session actively blocked in `await_children` is not idle-suspended."); and evidence of model calls from the LLM proxy or `ReportUsage`. An idle suspension proceeds only when the adapter's activity window also finds the session idle (§4.7.1 **Session seal.**). That window counts the session's `CH-MSGSOCK` reads and writes, each `heartbeat_ack` with `busy: true`, each `llm_request_completed`, and every `CH-MCP-PLATFORM` and `CH-MCP-CONNECTOR` call while it is in flight. §28.5.3 requires a runtime to report `busy: true` for a session "while it does any work for that session". A session whose runtime is `type: mcp`, or whose pool runs `executionMode: service`, cannot be idle-suspended, and the clock expires it with the `max_idle_time` reason.

**Ending a session [source].** §15.1's precondition table gives `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/interrupt` the precondition `running` ("Only valid while the agent is actively executing"), `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/terminate` every non-terminal state with the result `completed` ("Graceful shutdown"), and `DELETE /v1/sessions/{id}` every non-terminal state with the result `cancelled`, which "always records a `cancelled` terminal state for audit and billing purposes". §15.2 offers `interrupt_session`, `terminate_session`, and `cancel_session`. No billing, retention, or seal rule of the specification reads the difference between `completed` and `cancelled`.

**Cascade [source].** §8.10 "Cascading behavior" applies `cascadeOnFailure` at every terminal transition of the parent, with the values `cancel_all`, the default, `await_completion`, and `detach`, and `cascadeTimeoutSeconds`, 3600 seconds by default. The orphan cleanup job cancels or expires orphaned sessions, and `maxOrphanTasksPerTenant`, 100 by default, makes a `detach` cascade fall back to `cancel_all` when it would exceed the tenant's orphan count.

**Child outcomes [source].** §7.3 records each child's terminal outcome in `session_tree_archive` while its parent is in `awaiting_client_action`, and §8.10's re-await protocol replays settled results from the archive before live results. A resumed parent receives `children_reattached` after `session.resumed` (§7.2). §8.10 injects a `child_failed` event into the parent's stream when a child fails.

**Admission [source].** §11.1 **Resume admission.** runs the creation checks at every `suspended → running` and `suspended → resume_pending` write, and refuses a client message with `429 QUOTA_EXCEEDED` when they fail. §8.3 **Defense-in-depth TTL.** re-arms the TTL of every budget key of a root's tree when the root enters or leaves `suspended` or leaves `awaiting_client_action` for `resume_pending`, and a root that spends longer than `delegation.budgetKeyTTLSeconds`, 8 days by default, outside `suspended` since the last re-arm meets `BUDGET_KEYS_EXPIRED`.

**Deadlock detection [source].** §8.8 "Subtree deadlock detection (heuristic)" marks a subtree `deadlocked` when every running task in it is blocked in `input_required` or in `await_children` waiting only on `input_required` children, and emits `deadlock_detected`, after which `maxDeadlockWaitSeconds` ends with `DEADLOCK_TIMEOUT`. §9.2 "Interaction with `input_required` deadlock detection" relates it to elicitation chains.

**External mappings [source].** §8.8's "Session-level state mapping (supplementary)" maps `suspended` to `working` with `metadata.suspended: true` on MCP Tasks and A2A, `awaiting_client_action` to `input_required` and `input-required`, and the pre-running states to `submitted`. `input_required` appears in §6.2, §6.4, §7.1, §7.2, §7.3, §8, §9.2, §10.1, §10.4, §11.3, §15, §15.1, §15.4, §18, §21, and §29.

## Detailed design

### SL-1 States

| State | Meaning | Open turn | Holds a pod | Counts against the caps |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `created`, `finalizing`, `ready`, `starting` | Setup, as today. A child passes through them inside `session.create`. | No | As today | Yes |
| `running` | Working on a turn. | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| `idle` | Waiting for its client, with its pod. Replaces "`running` with no open turn". | No | Yes | Yes |
| `dormant` | Waiting for its client, with its pod released. Replaces the idle suspension. | No | No | No |
| `suspended` | The root's client suspended this session. | No | For `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds` after an acknowledged suspension or the suspension of an `idle` session, otherwise until its checkpoint succeeds and at most `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds`, and then no. Under an effective `checkpointPolicy` of `sealOnly` or `none`, it keeps its pod after every kind of suspension until it resumes or ends (IC-8). | No (open decision SL-Q3 for a paused session that holds its pod) |
| `blocked` | The root's client suspended an ancestor, and this session is paused under it. | No | As for `suspended` | No |
| `resume_pending`, `resuming` | Being restored onto a pod, as today. | No | No, then yes | Yes |
| `awaiting_client_action` | Retries are exhausted, as today. | No | No | Yes |
| `completed`, `cancelled`, `failed`, `expired` | Terminal, as today. | No | No | No |

**Row fields [design].** The session row drops the suspension-reason field of today and gains:

- `suspension_cause`, `client`, `budget`, or `external_limit`, set on a `suspended` session: `client` when the root's client suspended it, `budget` when a limit of [`0170-budgets`](../0170-budgets/change.md) suspended it under `onExhausted.running: suspend` ("Budget suspension" in "SL-4", L35), and `external_limit` when a reporter's limit notice or the runtime's `external_limit` frame reported that it hit a limit outside Lenny ("External limit suspension" in "SL-4", L39). A session suspended for `external_limit` also records the signal's source (`reporter` or `runtime`), the host, the status, the message, and any `retryAfter` or `resetsAt`, which the client reads and Lenny never acts on. The gateway records `resetsAt` alone: a signal that names `retryAfter` and no `resetsAt` takes `resetsAt` as the time the signal occurred plus `retryAfter`, its `occurredAt` for a reporter's notice and the time the adapter received a runtime's frame (L43).

- `paused_ancestor_id`, set on a `blocked` session to its nearest paused ancestor, which may itself be `blocked` ("SL-4", L25). It replaces revision 2's `suspension_root_id`.
- `suspension_requested`, with a cause, `client`, `budget`, or `external_limit`: `client` on a top-level session in `resume_pending`, `resuming`, or `awaiting_client_action` that the root's client suspends (L18), `budget` on a session in `resume_pending` or `resuming` that holds a limit a budget suspension exhausts (L41), and `external_limit` on a session in `resume_pending`, `resuming`, or `awaiting_client_action` that a limit notice names (L42). It is cleared when the session becomes `suspended`, which takes the requested cause, and otherwise `client` when its client resumes it, `budget` when the limit gains room, and `external_limit` when the root's client resumes it.
- `ended_suspended`, set on a session that reaches a terminal state while `suspended`, so the sessions `blocked` under it stay `blocked` ("SL-9").
- `last_turn_end`, one of `none`, `message`, `error`, and `cut`, which records how the session's last turn ended: with an end-of-turn `message`, with one that carries `error`, or cut. The close rule reads it ("SL-7"). Which `turn_end.status` value reports a cut turn, and what the turn interrupt R19 of `0138-rest-only-client-api` writes to `last_turn_end`, is open decision SL-Q6.
- `resume_notice_due`, with the cause `suspension` or `pod_loss`, set when a suspension or a pod loss cuts a turn and cleared when the resume notice is written ("SL-3").

The pod reference the row carries today stays, and decides whether a paused session resumes at once or through a restore (L14).

**Disk watch [design].** The adapter's per-slot disk watch measures each disk-backed volume of a slot and acts at `adapter.diskWatchThresholdPercent` of the volume's `sizeLimit`, 90% by default, before the kubelet evicts the whole pod ([`0118-agent-pod-layout`, "PL-5"](../0118-agent-pod-layout/change.md#pl-5-volumes-size-limits-and-eviction) states the trigger and the measurement). The slot release then runs on that slot container alone, so the pod's other sessions keep running. The specification has no code for this end, so the code is new. The owner confirmed this end as written on 2026-11-13 (L34).

### SL-3 Suspension and the open turn

The owner settled L8 on 2026-11-11, and L15 and L16 after the review of revision 1 **[design]**.

**Stopping the turn.** A suspension of a `running` session stops its open turn. The gateway first writes the paused state with `last_turn_end: cut` and `resume_notice_due` with the cause `suspension`, so the paused session holds no open turn from that write on (SLR-3). What happens to the pod then depends on the runtime:

- A runtime that declares `interrupt` receives `interrupt_request` on `CH-MSGSOCK`. When it answers `interrupt_acknowledged` within the deadline, it has stopped at a safe point, and the session holds its pod for `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds`, as an interrupt suspension does today.
- A runtime whose session's effective capabilities lack `interrupt` receives no signal, so the suspension cannot take hold, and the gateway does not wait for a signal.
- A runtime that declares `interrupt` and does not acknowledge within the deadline is in the same position at the deadline.

When a suspension cannot take hold, the session's coordinating replica stops the session's work outside the pod first and then releases the pod (L15, L24):

1. The gateway refuses each new call of the session to the platform tool server, and cancels the calls in flight, answering each in-flight `tool_call` on every connection of the slot with `CANCELLED`, so no platform call keeps the seal from taking (SLR-23). §4.7.1 **Session seal.** answers "not sealed" while such a call is in flight, whatever the window. The gateway then halts the slot's egress: it calls `HaltSessionEgress` naming the session on the adapter, the adapter sends `halt_slot` for the session's slot to `netagent` on `CH-EGRESSHALT`, and `netagent` closes the slot's open tunnels and in-flight requests, refuses the slot's new requests with `503`, and answers `halted`. The gateway continues with step 2 once the RPC answers or reaches its deadline of 5 seconds, because the release of step 3 stops the slot's traffic in any case, and it counts the outcome in `lenny_egress_halts_total` ([CR-4](../0124-connector-removal/change.md#cr-4-egress-halt-at-suspension)). The refusal and the halt apply to the pod in use and end when that pod is released. The halt also ends at the `UnsealSession` that resumes the session on the same pod. The pod a later restore claims carries neither.
2. It seals the session with an activity window of zero, so the adapter seals it whatever its recent activity.
3. It checkpoints the workspace and releases the pod, in the sequence of today's idle suspension (SLR-18), except that a failed checkpoint and a suspend write that misses its condition do not call `UnsealSession`, which today's idle suspension calls on both. On a pod with several slots the checkpoint waits in the pod's checkpoint queue while another slot's attempt runs, and the wait counts against the attempt's deadline ([`0131-incremental-checkpoint`, "Staggering across slots"](../0131-incremental-checkpoint/change.md#staggering-across-slots)).
4. When the checkpoint fails, the gateway keeps the pod and retries at the evaluation interval of "Graceful pod release during extended suspension". It does not unseal the session, so the seal, the refusal, and the egress halt of step 1 stay in force while it retries. At `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds` it releases the pod in any case, and the session resumes from its last good checkpoint.

After this design, the gateway calls `UnsealSession` only to resume a session on its pod, which lifts the halt, and on the failure path of the move to `dormant` ("SL-6"), which sends no halt, so its unseal finds no halted slot ([CR-4](../0124-connector-removal/change.md#cr-4-egress-halt-at-suspension) **Lifting.**).

A runtime's model calls, to an operator's LLM gateway or to a provider ([LU-7](../0133-llm-upstreams-and-cost-budgets/change.md#lu-7-calls-outside-the-gateway)), and its calls to operator services are egress traffic, which the halt of step 1 stops at the suspension. The adapter's and `netagent`'s own connections keep running, so the checkpoint and the release proceed. On a pool without slot attribution `netagent` cannot tell the slots apart, so the adapter sends no `halt_slot`, `HaltSessionEgress` answers that the halt is unavailable, and only the pod release stops the slot's egress.

**Under `sealOnly` or `none`.** A session whose effective `checkpointPolicy` is `sealOnly` or `none` has no checkpoint to release its pod with, and [IC-8](../0131-incremental-checkpoint/change.md#ic-8-checkpoint-policy-interval-and-change-tracker) keeps a suspended session's pod under those policies. When its suspension cannot take hold, the gateway applies step 1 and seals the session as above, skips steps 3 and 4, and keeps the pod until the session resumes or ends. The refusal and the egress halt of step 1 stay in force on that pod meanwhile, and the halt lifts when the gateway unseals the session to resume it on that pod. On a pool without slot attribution, a runtime that calls a provider outside the proxy or an operator service can keep calling it until then, which "Limits" states.

**A running helper run.** A mid-session overlay, the helper run that writes a mid-session upload or a child-file import into the workspace, can be running in the session's slot when a suspension releases the pod. Before the checkpoint of step 3, the adapter closes the run's cancel pipe, and the supervisor kills the helper's process group and answers `setupdone` with `setup_cancelled`. The helper writes no result. The checkpoint and the release proceed after that `setupdone`, and the adapter runs the helper's `discard` on the overlay directory the run left, as it does after every cancelled helper run, while the checkpoint skips that reserved name in any case ([WM-11](../0153-workspace-materialization/change.md#wm-11-the-worker-broker-requests-and-checkpoints), [WM-8](../0153-workspace-materialization/change.md#wm-8-atomicity-under-the-fixed-layout)). The upload or the import that the run carried fails, and its caller receives the failure. The same order holds for a setup command that is still running. The release acts on the session's slot container alone, so the other sessions of a pod with several slots keep running ([SI-3](../0145-session-isolation/change.md#si-3-runtime-processes-slot-release-and-setup-commands)).

**Cancelled calls.** A call that step 1 cancels before the gateway commits its effect has no effect, and one whose effect the gateway already committed keeps it, and the resume notice names it. For the platform operations of [`0166-delegation-primitives`, "DP-1"](../0166-delegation-primitives/change.md#dp-1-session-grant):

- A cancelled `session.create` whose child the gateway had already created keeps the child, which is recorded as the session's child and lands `blocked` with its task held, as step 4 of "SL-4" states. The resume notice names it with its session id and its tags. A create the gateway had not committed creates nothing. A `session.create` repeated after the resume with the same idempotency key returns the child the first call created.
- A cancelled `events.wait` returns the child events it had taken to `pending` in the session's inbox, under the custody of a wait that "MD-3" states, and they are delivered after the resume. A cancelled `events.peek` takes nothing.
- A cancelled `session.send`, `session.close`, `session.set_limits`, `session.transfer`, `session.release`, `questions.answer`, `questions.forward`, or `tools.result` follows the general rule: the operation took effect if the gateway committed it, and the notice names it.

**Pending questions and approvals (L28, L29).** When a suspension stops a turn, the gateway withdraws every question and every tool-use approval of the session that is still pending (SLR-27):

- A question the session raised through `ask_question` or a `request` with `kind: "question"` is expired as §9.2 "Elicitation Timeout Semantics" item 6 expires the elicitations of a session that leaves its pod, whether or not the pod is released yet. The root's client's view drops it, and a later `respond_to_elicitation` or `dismiss_elicitation` for it is refused with `404 ELICITATION_NOT_FOUND`.
- An approval, from a `request` with `kind: "approval"` or an approval-gated `tool_call` that the gateway holds, is withdrawn in the same way. The gateway emits `tool_use` with the phase `withdrawn`, so the client's view and the MCP approval prompt drop it, and a later `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/tool-use/{toolCallId}/approve` or `.../deny` for it is refused with `404 RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`. The approval's timeout and the unattended policy of `harnessApprovals` no longer answer it. An approval that [SO-5a](../0142-security-oversight/change.md#so-5a-tool-use-approvals) of `0142-security-oversight` routes to a security approver with `approver: security` is withdrawn under the same rule (L29): its `security_approvals` row records the outcome `withdrawn` with its audit record, its `approvals.timeoutSeconds` timeout no longer denies it, and a later resolution through the approvals API or the owner's `deny` is refused with `404 RESOURCE_NOT_FOUND`.

While the pod is still held, the gateway ends the runtime's wait: an `ask_question` call returns an error that names the withdrawal, a `request` with `kind: "question"` receives a `reply` with `outcome: "withdrawn"`, a `request` with `kind: "approval"` receives a `reply` with `decision: "withdrawn"`, and a held `tool_call` receives an error result that names the withdrawal, as a denial does. The resume notice names each withdrawn question and approval, so the runtime may ask again or retry the tool.

The `Interrupt` RPC's timeout writes no session state. The adapter returns `INTERRUPT_TIMEOUT`, and the gateway decides what follows: the release above for a suspension, and the `queue` fallback for a message with the `interrupt` intent ("MD-5").

A session that is `idle` at its suspension has no turn to stop and holds its pod for `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds`. A `dormant` session has no pod to hold.

**Frames from a paused session.** A runtime can write frames between the write of the paused state and its stop or the pod's release. Such a frame opens no turn and changes no session state (SLR-3):

- `message` and `message_part` frames reach the transcript, and the gateway makes no child event and no result from them. The end-of-turn `message` with which a runtime that declares `interrupt` closes the stopped turn leaves `last_turn_end: cut`, produces no `end_of_turn` child event, and is never a result.
- `consumed` reports are recorded, because they state which messages the runtime holds. The end-of-turn `message` that closes the stopped turn consumes only the ids it lists, and every other `delivered` message of the session returns to `pending`, so the resume delivers it again (L26, SLR-24). The rule of "MD-4" that an end-of-turn `message` without `consumed` consumes every `delivered` message does not apply to it. When the closing frame leaves the stopped turn's causing message unconsumed, the message's return to `pending` restarts the work at the resume, so the gateway clears `resume_notice_due` and writes no resume notice, as after a restore that delivers the causing message again.
- Every platform tool call that acts on another session, `session.create`, `session.send`, `session.close`, `session.transfer`, and `session.release` among them, is refused with `409 SESSION_PAUSED` (SLR-13).

A turn that ended with its end-of-turn `message` before the write of the paused state is not cut, and the session receives no resume notice.

**Resume notice.** When a session whose row carries `resume_notice_due` resumes, or its restore commits in a live state, the gateway writes a system message to it before any message held for it: `from.kind: system`, `notice: "resumed"`, and a text by cause. After a suspension the text is "The session was suspended and has now resumed. Continue the work you were doing when it was suspended." After a pod loss it is "The session was restored after its pod was lost, and the turn in progress was cut. Continue the work you were doing." The notice also names each call that "Cancelled calls" says it names and each question and approval that "Pending questions and approvals" withdrew. The message takes the session's default intent and opens a turn, and the gateway clears the flag when it writes the message (SLR-7). The gateway writes no notice when the resume or the restore delivers the cut turn's causing message to the runtime again, which restarts the work: after the closing frame of a stopped turn left it unconsumed, or after a restore whose redelivery the adapter's deduplication did not drop ("After a pod loss"). Held messages follow the notice, so a correction held for the session, from its parent or from the root's client, reaches the runtime after the notice and overrides it. When a message from the root's client to the session itself lifts the suspension, the gateway writes no notice, because that message says what to do next. A session idle at its suspension receives no notice, because it had no work in progress.

**After a pod loss (L16).** A restore loses the open turn, and a runtime that reported its causing message `consumed` would otherwise sit `idle` with nothing to do. When the restore commits in a live state, the session receives the resume notice, and its client receives one report that the turn was lost and that the session was told to continue (SLR-19). A parent receives an `end_of_turn` child event with `error: "turn_lost"` and the text "The child's turn was lost with its pod. The child was restored and told to continue its work." ("SL-8"). A top-level client receives `turn_end` with `error: "turn_lost"` on the session's event stream. The client can wait for the continued turn, send a correction, or close the session. A restore that lands in a paused state reports at the resume. The gateway decides between the notice and a redelivery after the adapter's deduplication of "MD-3": when the adapter writes the cut turn's causing message to the restored runtime again, the redelivery restarts the work as it does today, so the gateway clears `resume_notice_due` and writes neither the notice nor the report. When the adapter drops the redelivered id because the checkpoint's consumed set holds it, and relays `consumed` for it, the notice and the report follow.

**Without the notice.** A resumed tree would sit idle until the root's client sent a message and the root's model steered each child again. The notice restarts each session on its own work.

### SL-4 Tree suspension

The owner settled L2, L5, and L12 on 2026-11-11, and L18 after the review of revision 1 **[design]**.

**Routes.** `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/suspend` replaces `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/interrupt`, and MCP `suspend_session` replaces `interrupt_session`; the MCP tool moves to the MCP adapter under [`0138-rest-only-client-api`](../0138-rest-only-client-api/change.md). The turn interrupt of that design, R19, never suspends: it refuses a runtime without `interrupt` with `409 INTERRUPT_UNSUPPORTED` and an unacknowledged `interrupt_request` with `504 INTERRUPT_TIMEOUT`, and an adapter that must stop the turn then calls this route (its O11). The precondition is `running`, `idle`, `dormant`, or `blocked`, and for a top-level session also `resume_pending`, `resuming`, or `awaiting_client_action` (L18). A `suspended` session with `suspension_cause: budget` or `external_limit` accepts the call, which sets `suspension_cause: client` and neither restarts nor extends its pod hold (L37, L39). Any other `suspended` session, and a child in a recovering state, answer `409 INVALID_STATE_TRANSITION` with its current state. The call returns once the suspension is recorded on the session's row and every descendant in a live state is paused (SLR-4).

**Steps.** When the root's client suspends a session:

1. The gateway writes the suspension on the suspension root's row first: `suspended` under "SL-3", or `suspension_requested` for a top-level session in `resume_pending`, `resuming`, or `awaiting_client_action`. That write is the durable record of the tree suspension. Once the session is `suspended`, it opens no turn and sends no `session.send`.
2. The gateway moves each descendant in `running`, `idle`, or `dormant` to `blocked`, with `paused_ancestor_id` set to its nearest paused ancestor, top-down, stopping each open turn under "SL-3".
3. A descendant already `suspended` stays `suspended`, because its own suspension came first, and its descendants stay under it.
4. A descendant in a setup state, `resume_pending`, or `resuming` continues. When its setup or its restore commits, it lands in `blocked` under its nearest paused ancestor (SLR-4). A child whose `session.create` was in flight at the suspension is such a descendant, and its task stays held until it resumes.
5. A descendant in `awaiting_client_action` keeps waiting, and its `maxAwaitingClientActionSeconds` clock stops while an ancestor is suspended (SLR-21).

**Across replicas.** The replica that serves the suspension call writes each descendant's row itself, with a compare-and-set on the state it read, and it forwards the stop of each open turn, with the `interrupt_request`, the egress halt, the seal, and the checkpoint of "SL-3", to the descendant's coordinating replica over `CH-HOLDERFORWARD`. A row write that loses its compare-and-set re-reads the descendant and applies step 2, 3, or 4 to the state it finds. The call returns after the row writes, as "Routes" states, and does not wait for the forwarded turn stops, so a deep tree spread over replicas does not wait for one interrupt deadline after another. A restore or a setup commit re-reads the nearest paused ancestor inside its own guarded write, so a descendant that step 2 skipped as `resuming` and whose restore commits after step 1 lands in `blocked` (SLR-4).

**A partial suspension.** A coordinator crash can stop the gateway between step 1 and the last move of step 2. The gateway leader's backlog sweep (§7.2 **Backlog resume.**) also finds every descendant in `running`, `idle`, or `dormant` that has a paused ancestor: it selects the rows that are `suspended` or `blocked` or carry `suspension_requested` or `ended_suspended`, and walks down from each to its live descendants, so the sweep's cost follows the number of paused sessions rather than the number of live ones. It hands each to that session's coordinating replica, which moves it to `blocked` and stops its turn under "SL-3", sending `interrupt_request` and doing the egress halt, the seal, and the checkpoint as for a direct suspension. A session without a coordinating replica has no pod, and the leader writes `blocked` itself. The next sweep therefore finishes the suspension (SLR-20).

**Pods and timers.** A session whose runtime acknowledged `interrupt_request`, and a session that was `idle` at its suspension, holds its pod for `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds`, then checkpoints and releases it, as today. A session whose effective `checkpointPolicy` is `sealOnly` or `none` has no checkpoint to release its pod with, so it keeps its pod after a client's suspension until it resumes, ends, or reaches `maxSuspendedSessionSeconds`, while a budget suspension, or a compute tick that a budget refuses while it is paused, expires it at the end of the budget's hold ("Budget suspension (L35)"), as [IC-8](../0131-incremental-checkpoint/change.md#ic-8-checkpoint-policy-interval-and-change-tracker) states; how the caps treat it is open decision SL-Q3. Whether the release at the end of the hold waits for the exports already running, as "MD-18" of `0135-message-delivery` states, is open decision SL-Q7. Every other paused session releases its pod as soon as its checkpoint succeeds, and the gateway retries a failed checkpoint until `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds` forces the release ("SL-3", L24). Each session of the tree whose suspension cannot take hold has its slot's egress halted under step 1 of "SL-3" (CR-4). A session whose runtime acknowledged, and a session `idle` at its suspension, receives no halt. A paused session's `maxSessionAge` and `maxClientIdleSeconds` clocks stop, and `maxSuspendedSessionSeconds` runs from its entry to the paused state. Budget keys carry no TTL under 0170, so no state re-arms them ("SL-10", L36).

**Who suspends and who lifts.** In v1 only the root's client suspends a session: it suspends the root, or any descendant directly through REST. The root's client is the authenticated principal that owns the root, and neither a message's `from` member nor a `fromSessionId` makes a caller the root's client (SLR-22). A principal that carries a session claim is never the root's client, even when its subject owns the root: the gateway gives a runtime's platform tool calls the owning user's subject with a session claim and the caller type `agent` (`WithSessionPrincipal` in `pkg/gateway/core/session/sessionserver/tools_seam_core.go` today), so the predicate refuses that caller on the suspend, resume, and close routes and never lets its message lift a suspension, whichever session it runs in. 0170 "BG-10" applies the same predicate to the session budget, charge, and principal budget routes, and 0138's R21 to the transfer and release routes. [TS-3](../0149-tenant-scoping-identity/change.md#ts-3-session-environment-context-and-cross-environment-sharing) of `0149-tenant-scoping-identity` (0149.S3) also allow-lists the intra-pod tool surface to the §9.1 platform tools and the operations of 0166 "DP-1", which keeps the client lifecycle tools off a runtime's surface (F-9.1.13); this predicate holds without it. A parent has no operation to suspend a child (L12), and 0166 adds none. Only the root's client lifts a suspension, by `POST /resume`, `resume_session`, or a message to the suspended session. A resume of a top-level session in `awaiting_client_action` by its client lifts a suspension recorded on it, and when the session's restore commits in a live state, the descendants `blocked` under it resume as in a tree resume ("Resuming a tree"). A parent's `session.send` to a child in `suspended` or `blocked` is held until the suspension is lifted.

**Sessions that left their parent.** A session that left its parent under 0166 "Leaving the parent" in "DP-2" (decisions 32 and 46) is reached by a tree suspension as follows. A transferred session stays in its root's tree with the root's client as its client, so a suspension of the root reaches it and its descendants as descendants of the root, and a suspension of its former parent, or of any session between the root and its former parent, does not reach it. The root's client may also suspend it directly, as any descendant. A released session is a root of its own, so no suspension in its former tree reaches it, and a suspension of the released session reaches its own descendants. A leave of a `blocked` session recomputes its nearest paused ancestor in its new position. A released session has no ancestor, and a root cannot be `blocked` ("SL-10"), so it becomes `suspended` and only its client's resume lifts the pause. A transferred session is re-pointed to its nearest paused ancestor on the path from the root, and when that path holds none it resumes through resume admission, as a `blocked` session does when the session its `paused_ancestor_id` names enters a live state ("SL-2").

**Budget suspension (L35, L37, L40, L41, L44).** A limit of [`0170-budgets`](../0170-budgets/change.md) under `onExhausted.running: suspend` suspends the session that holds the limit, at the trigger that "Budget suspension" in 0170 "BG-6" states, and this section's mechanism carries it out. This section owns the states, the hold and its end, and what a client's resume and suspension do, and 0170 owns the trigger, what gives room, and the hold's length:

- The suspension root is the session that holds the exhausted limit: the tree's root for a limit of the tree's session budget, the capped session for a cap, and, for a limit of a shared budget, the session whose charge the limit refused, or each live session that the budget binds for a limit that counts only `externalCost` (0170 decisions 73 and 83). It takes `suspension_cause: budget`, and the gateway runs steps 1 to 5 of "Steps" on it and its descendants, except that step 2 leaves a `dormant` descendant `dormant` (L41), so every session that charges the limit pauses together and no session of the subtree runs while it is paused. An `idle` session's descendants are paused as well, whatever SLR-9 would hold, because the budget pauses the subtree rather than releasing one pod.
- Each paused session keeps its pod for `budgets.suspendPodHoldSeconds` in place of `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds`, and then checkpoints and releases it as "Pods and timers" states. The checkpoint passes an exhausted storage limit under 0170 "BG-6". A session under `sealOnly` or `none` expires with the reason `budget_exhausted` at the end of the hold.
- A compute tick that a budget refuses on a session that is already `suspended` for its client, or `blocked`, and still holds its pod starts the same hold for it, whatever its cause: the session keeps its pod for at most `budgets.suspendPodHoldSeconds` from that tick, and then checkpoints and releases it, or expires with the reason `budget_exhausted` under `sealOnly` or `none` (L37).
- **A holder that is not live (L41).** When the session that holds the exhausted limit is not `running` or `idle`, the suspension follows its state, and the limit gaining room resumes only the sessions that a budget suspended, so no `dormant` or waiting session wakes unasked:
  - `blocked`: it becomes `suspended` with `suspension_cause: budget`, with `paused_ancestor_id` cleared and its pod hold not restarted. When its ancestor resumes first, it stays `suspended` until it resumes itself, and when it resumes while its ancestor is still paused, it lands in `blocked` under its nearest paused ancestor.
  - `resume_pending` or `resuming`: it takes `suspension_requested` with the cause `budget`, and step 2 pauses its live descendants under it. Its restore completes and lands in `suspended` with `suspension_cause: budget`, keeps its pod for the hold, and continues on that pod when the limit gains room within the hold. After the hold, the root's client resumes it, as **Room** below states (L44).
  - `dormant`, `awaiting_client_action`, or a terminal state: it keeps its state, and each highest `running` or `idle` session under it becomes a suspension root of its own with `suspension_cause: budget`, its subtree paused under it. Resume admission refuses the holder's wake or retry while the limit has no room ("SL-10"), the limit gaining room wakes nothing, and an `awaiting_client_action` holder's `maxAwaitingClientActionSeconds` clock keeps running. A terminal holder with no live session under it pauses nothing: the charge counts, and 0170's BGR-9 bounds the overshoot.
  - `suspended` for its client, for an external limit, or for another budget limit: it keeps its cause, and resume admission refuses its resume while any limit on its path has no room.
- **`end` (L41).** Under `onExhausted.running: end`, the holder expires with the reason `budget_exhausted` in any non-terminal state, and its children follow "When a parent ends (SL-9)".
- **Room (L44).** When the limit gains room while a session that the budget suspended still holds its pod in the hold, the gateway resumes it through resume admission, which admits it only when every limit on its path has room, and "Resuming a tree" resumes the subtree. After its pod is released, room resumes nothing, because a user does not expect a session paused for days to wake by itself: the root's client resumes the session, and resume admission refuses it while the limit has no room. A granted extension of the limit, and a raise of the limit or cap that the session holds by `PATCH /v1/sessions/{id}/budget` or `session.set_limits`, resume it at any time, because each acts on that session. An administrator's raise, adjustment, or reset, a void, and a new period resume only the sessions still within their hold (0170 "BG-6" **Room.**). A client's resume of the session passes resume admission as well, which refuses it with `BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` while the limit has no room ("SL-10"). A client's suspension of the session sets `suspension_cause: client`, after which only the root's client resumes it, and it neither restarts nor extends the hold (L37).
- The session's parent receives the `suspended` child event with the reason `budget`, and the `resumed` child event when the subtree resumes ("MD-22").

**External limit suspension (L39, L42, L43, L45, L46).** A limit that a gateway or service enforces outside Lenny suspends a session when a registered reporter's limit notice names it ([`0170-budgets`](../0170-budgets/change.md), "BG-12" **Limit notices.**) or its runtime sends the `external_limit` frame ([`0139-runtime-protocol`](../0139-runtime-protocol/change.md), "RP-9"). This section owns the effect, 0170 the notice and its checks, and 0139 the frame:

- The session is the suspension root, with `suspension_cause: external_limit`, and the gateway runs steps 1 to 5 of "Steps" on it and its descendants, exactly as for a budget suspension: the subtree pauses together, each paused session keeps its pod for `budgets.suspendPodHoldSeconds` and then checkpoints and releases it, the checkpoint passes an exhausted storage limit under 0170 "BG-6", and a session under `sealOnly` or `none` expires with the reason `external_limit` at the end of the hold.
- Nothing resumes it automatically. No budget gaining room, and no `resetsAt` that the signal names or the gateway computes from its `retryAfter`, lifts it, and no resume is refused before that time, which is advice to the client (L43). A session left suspended expires at `maxSuspendedSessionSeconds`, as any suspended session does. Only the root's client lifts it, and only by an explicit resume: `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/resume`, or the MCP adapter's `resume_session`, carrying `"acknowledgeExternalLimit": true`, which passes resume admission ("SL-10"). A resume without the field is refused with `409 EXTERNAL_LIMIT_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT_REQUIRED`, category `PERMANENT`, whose `details` carry the recorded `status`, `message`, and `resetsAt`, and a message from the root's client is held and lifts nothing, so a client that sends its next message without reading why the session stopped does not resume it (L45). No administrator route resumes a session in v1, so an operator who wants it resumed asks the client.
- **Any state (L42).** A signal suspends the session it names in any non-terminal state. Nothing resumes an external limit automatically, so no session wakes unasked:
  - `dormant`: it becomes `suspended` with no pod, and its `dormant` descendants stay `dormant`. A message from its parent or its children wakes nothing, the root's client's acknowledged resume lifts the suspension through resume admission (L45), and the session keeps the `maxSuspendedSessionSeconds` clock that started when it became `dormant` (L46).
  - `resume_pending`, `resuming`, or `awaiting_client_action`: it takes `suspension_requested` with the cause `external_limit`, so its restore lands in `suspended`, and only the root's client's acknowledged resume lifts it (L45).
  - `blocked`: it becomes `suspended` with `paused_ancestor_id` cleared, so it stays `suspended` when its ancestor resumes.
  - `suspended` with `suspension_cause: budget`: it takes `external_limit`, so the limit gaining room no longer resumes it.
  - `suspended` for its client or for an external limit keeps its cause, and a terminal session changes nothing.
- The gateway records a reporter's notice as 0170 "BG-12" states, and counts a runtime's frame in `lenny_external_limit_notices_total` with `source: runtime` without a record of its own, so a runtime that repeats the frame writes no record per frame. The adapter forwards at most one frame per session every 10 seconds, as 0139 "RP-9" states. A client's suspension of the session sets `suspension_cause: client`, after which only the root's client resumes it, and it neither restarts nor extends the hold.
- The session's parent receives the `suspended` child event with the reason `external_limit`, and the `resumed` child event when the subtree resumes ("MD-22"). The client receives the cause and the recorded detail on the session's `state_change` event, with the detail's `source`, `reporter` or `runtime`, so a client shows a runtime's `message` as the runtime's words rather than the platform's ("SL-11").

**A descendant suspended directly.** When the root's client suspends a descendant, the descendant's parent receives a `suspended` child event, because the parent is that child's client and would otherwise see its messages held without a reason. When the root's client resumes it, the parent receives a `resumed` child event ("MD-22").

**Resuming a tree.** Resuming the suspension root resumes the root, and each session that enters a live state then resumes the descendants whose `paused_ancestor_id` names it, top-down, each through resume admission ("SL-10"). A descendant in `suspended` keeps its own suspension, and the sessions `blocked` under it stay `blocked`. A descendant that admission refuses stays `blocked`, and the sessions `blocked` under it stay `blocked` under it until it resumes (L25). Each resumed session receives the resume notice of "SL-3" first, and its held messages follow at the delivery points of "MD-5", the commit to `idle` among them, oldest first.

### SL-5 Messages to a waiting or paused session

The owner settled L3 on 2026-11-11, and L2 makes the hold a safety net under the tree suspension **[design]**.

| State | A message from the root's client | A message from the session's parent | A child event or a system message |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `idle` | It is written and opens a turn. | It is written and opens a turn. | It is written and opens a turn. |
| `dormant` | It wakes the session through resume admission and a restore, then opens a turn. | It wakes the session in the same way. | It wakes the session in the same way. |
| `suspended` | It resumes the session and the descendants `blocked` under it, then opens a turn, except for a session suspended for an external limit, for which it is held until an acknowledged resume (L45). | It is held. | It is held. |
| `blocked` | It resumes this session, then opens a turn. | It is held. | It is held. |
| `awaiting_client_action` | It resumes the session through a restore, then opens a turn, except while an external limit is recorded on the session, when it is held until an acknowledged resume (L45). | It resumes the child through a restore, then opens a turn (L7), except while an external limit is recorded on the child, when it is held until the root's client's acknowledged resume and starts no restore (L48). | It is held. |

The table describes a message that opens a turn. A message with the `append` intent of "MD-1" and "MD-14", from any sender, and a child event outside the parent's `wakeOn` (0166 "DP-4"), open no turn: each waits in the inbox for the session's next turn, wakes no `dormant` session, and is held in a paused state as the table states.

A held message stays `pending` in the session's DLQ and is delivered at the next delivery point after the session is live again, which for a restored or resumed session is its commit to `idle` (SLR-5, SLR-26).

**How long a held message lives (SLR-14).** A message held in the DLQ takes a TTL of `maxResumeWindowSeconds`, 900 seconds by default, today, and a pause can last up to `maxSuspendedSessionSeconds`, seven days by default. A message held for a `dormant`, `suspended`, or `blocked` session therefore takes no TTL: it expires with `target_terminated` when the session ends, and the DLQ's `maxDLQSize` cap still applies. A child event never expires while its parent lives, in any state, because the child cannot send it again. Other messages held for a recovering session keep today's TTL. The DLQ drops its oldest entry on overflow and orders entries by acceptance time plus the TTL, so which entry the cap evicts when child events fill it, and the order of a held message that takes no TTL, are open decision SL-Q11.

**Backlog resume** (§7.2) changes to match. It wakes a `dormant` session that holds any message. It resumes a `suspended` or `blocked` session only for a held message from the root's client, never a session suspended for an external limit, which only an acknowledged resume lifts (L45), and a `blocked` session whose `paused_ancestor_id` names a session in a live state (L25). A suspension root that ended keeps its descendants `blocked` (L4, SLR-15). The same sweep finishes a partial tree suspension ("SL-4").

A provider adapter's conditional send to a `dormant` session, R16 of [`0138-rest-only-client-api`](../0138-rest-only-client-api/change.md), takes the `dormant` row: the send wakes the session through resume admission and a restore, and its message opens a turn once the restore lands.

The delivery paths of "MD-5" read these states: the resume path covers `dormant`, `suspended`, and `blocked`, and `awaiting_client_action` for a message from the session's client or the root's client, under this table, and the direct, steer, interrupt, and queue paths cover `idle` and `running`.

### SL-6 Idle pods and dormancy

The owner settled L11 and L14 on 2026-11-11, and L17 after the review of revision 1 **[design]**.

**The idle clock.** `maxClientIdleSeconds` runs in `running` and `idle`, as §6.2 runs it in `running` today, with or without an open turn. Every qualifying activity resets it, and the adapter's activity window of §4.7.1 **Session seal.** confirms the decision before the pod is released, as it confirms an idle suspension today. When the clock fires:

- An `idle` session with no `running` descendant becomes `dormant` (SLR-9).
- A `running` session has its open turn cut, with `last_turn_end: cut`, and becomes `dormant` at once (L17, SLR-17). It has already been silent for a whole window, and a stop in `idle` would hold its pod for a second one. Its client receives `turn_end` with `error: "idle_timeout"`, and a parent receives an `end_of_turn` child event with the same error, so a parent waiting in `events.wait` learns that the turn ended. No resume notice follows, because the gateway knows of no work in progress, and the client's next message says what to do.

The gateway checkpoints the workspace and releases the pod in the sequence that writes `dormant`, as it does for an idle suspension today, and the checkpoint takes its place in the pod's checkpoint queue as every attempt does ([`0131-incremental-checkpoint`, "Staggering across slots"](../0131-incremental-checkpoint/change.md#staggering-across-slots)). A helper run or setup command still running in the session's slot is cancelled through the cancel pipe first, as under "A running helper run" in "SL-3", and so is one running when any other pod release of the session begins. A session restored with no checkpoint of its own, such as one whose pod was lost before its first checkpoint, returns through the snapshotless rebuild, in which the helper's `materialize` rebuilds the workspace from the stored plan as the slot UID before its setup commands run ([WM-13](../0153-workspace-materialization/change.md#wm-13-resume-and-derive)). IC-8 states that a session under `sealOnly` or `none` whose pod fails or is evicted resumes with `resumeMode: conversation_only` and `workspaceLost: true` from the §4.4.5 minimal-state record, which the tree writes only when an eviction checkpoint fails; which path such a session takes is open decision SL-Q8.

**Qualifying activity.** The rules of §6.2 and §4.7.1 stay, with the frame names of the message delivery design ("MD-24"):

- Each message the adapter writes to the runtime: a client message, a child event, or a system message.
- Each session-scoped frame the runtime writes: `message`, `message_part`, `activity`, `consumed`, `request`, `tool_call`, and `tool_result`, and each `reply` the adapter writes.
- Each `heartbeat_ack` with `busy: true` for the session.
- Each `llm_request_completed`, as §6.2 states today. Model calls through egress are no activity under decision 22 of 0133, and a runtime that waits on a model reports `busy: true` on its heartbeats (decision L38).
- Every platform tool call while it is in flight on any connection of the slot, `events.wait` and `ask_question` among them, and every export in progress ("MD-18"). `events.peek` is the exception: its call and its result add no activity of their own (decision 10 of 0166).
- A call to a tool that the session's client offers, while it is open, for up to `clientToolHoldSeconds`. [`0122-client-tools`](../0122-client-tools/change.md) owns the hold, after which the gateway withdraws the call with `tool_call_withdrawn`, so a session whose client never answers can become `dormant` (decision 24 of 0166). A call that a child makes to a tool its parent offers counts in the child in the same way.
- A `request` that waits for its `reply`, from the request until the reply arrives or the request expires, so a person who takes longer than the window to approve or to answer does not cost the session its turn (L17).

§6.2's separate rule that counts each `await_children` call and each partial result of its stream as activity leaves, because the in-flight rule covers a parent that waits inside `events.wait`.

Traffic through `netagent` is not qualifying activity (O8 of [`0124-connector-removal`](../0124-connector-removal/decisions.md#owner-decisions)). A runtime whose harness waits on a call to an operator service keeps its session active under the rule for runtime authors below, and a question it forwards from the service is a `request` that counts until its `reply` arrives ([CR-3](../0124-connector-removal/change.md#cr-3-external-services)).

**The rule for runtime authors.** A runtime that works through a long task without writing frames reports `busy: true` on each heartbeat, as §28.5.3 already requires, or sends activity updates, or streams message parts. A runtime that does none of these for `maxClientIdleSeconds` has its turn cut.

**Sessions under `sealOnly` or `none`.** A session whose effective `checkpointPolicy` is `sealOnly` or `none` ([`0131-incremental-checkpoint`, "IC-8"](../0131-incremental-checkpoint/change.md#ic-8-checkpoint-policy-interval-and-change-tracker)) never becomes `dormant`, because no checkpoint would restore its workspace on another pod. It stays `idle` on its pod while it waits, and when the clock fires, from `idle` or with its turn cut from `running`, it becomes `expired` with the `max_idle_time` reason, and its seal runs under `sealOnly`. The owner settled this on 2026-11-13 as option (a): "They stay idle on their pod; the idle timeout ends the session instead of making it dormant." Every other session can become `dormant`, because every runtime is an agent runtime under [`0119-agent-runtimes-only`](../0119-agent-runtimes-only/change.md), and the `type: mcp` and service-mode exceptions of earlier revisions are gone.

**Working descendants.** An `idle` session does not become `dormant` while any of its descendants is `running` (SLR-9). A budget suspension is no move to `dormant`: it pauses the session and its descendants together under "Budget suspension" in "SL-4", so SLR-9 never keeps a parent on its pod while a budget has stopped its subtree. A parent that waits for its children by ending its turn has no tool call in flight, and without this rule it would release its pod while they work and pay a restore for the next child event. When the whole tree is waiting or paused, each session becomes `dormant` on its own clock. A transferred session counts as a descendant of its root for this rule, and a released session counts for no session of its former tree ("SL-9"). Because `events.peek` adds no activity, an extension that polls with it does not keep a parent's pod: SLR-9 keeps the pod while a child runs, and a parent whose children are all waiting or paused becomes `dormant` on its own clock (0166 "Events on a tool connection" in "DP-4").

**Timers.** `maxSessionAge` and `maxClientIdleSeconds` pause in `dormant`, and `maxSuspendedSessionSeconds` expires a `dormant` session, as both apply to an idle-suspended session today. Budget keys carry no TTL under [`0170-budgets`, "BG-9"](../0170-budgets/change.md#bg-9-counters-failure-and-precision) (L36), so no state re-arms them. A session can therefore outlive `maxSessionAge` in wall-clock time, which the rollout drain of `0140-runtime-revisions` reads as a bound; that conflict is open decision SL-Q5.

### SL-7 Closing a session

The owner settled L9 on 2026-11-11, and L21 after the review of revision 1 **[design]**.

**What the terminal state is for.** Every close and every cancel stops the session at once, releases the pod, seals the workspace under the session's effective `checkpointPolicy` (no seal runs under `none`, IC-8), and applies the session's `onParentEnd` to its children ("SL-9", SLR-25), which closes them at once under the default `close_all`. The gateway does nothing differently afterwards for `completed` and `cancelled`, and the audit record names who closed the session. The state matters to readers outside the gateway:

- An A2A or MCP Tasks client branches on it. A `completed` task carries results the client uses, and a cancelled task, spelled `canceled` on A2A and `cancelled` on MCP Tasks under [MP-4](../0134-mcp-protocol-upgrade/change.md#mp-4-tasks), tells it the work was abandoned. Both protocols define a cancel operation whose outcome is that cancelled status.
- A person watching the tree, and a parent's `session.get` and `session.list`, read `completed` as "this session produced what it was asked for" and `cancelled` as "its work was cut off".
- Dashboards and experiment comparisons that count outcomes per runtime revision or variant read it.

A parent never needs the state of a child it closed itself.

**The close rule.** A close records:

- `completed` when no turn is open at the close, no call of the session to a tool its client offers is open, and `last_turn_end` is `message`. The result is that end-of-turn message.
- `cancelled` otherwise: a turn is open, a call to a tool the session's client offers is open (L21), the last turn was cut or ended with `error`, or the session never finished a turn. There is no result.

Under the blocking interface of [`0122-client-tools`](../0122-client-tools/change.md) (decision 23 of 0166), a call to a client's tool stays open inside the turn that made it, so an open call also means an open turn, and L21's rule that a close with calls outstanding records `cancelled` holds through both conditions. A call that the gateway withdrew after `clientToolHoldSeconds` is no longer open, and the close reads how the turn ended.

**Operations.**

| Who | Close | Cancel |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| A REST client | `POST /v1/sessions/{id}/close`, which replaces `POST /terminate` | `DELETE /v1/sessions/{id}`, unchanged |
| An MCP client, through the MCP adapter of [`0138-rest-only-client-api`](../0138-rest-only-client-api/change.md) | `close_session`, which replaces `terminate_session` | `cancel_session`, unchanged |
| A parent | `session.close`, which names the child and takes no other argument (0166 "DP-1") | A parent has no cancel operation. |
| A cascade | `onParentEnd` ("SL-9") | A cascade closes by the close rule and never cancels. |

A close of a `running` session cuts its turn at once, as `POST /terminate` does today, and the partial output stays in the transcript and outside the result. A parent that wants a child's turn to finish waits for its end-of-turn child event before it closes the child. A close in a setup state records `cancelled`, where `POST /terminate` records `completed` today.

**One tool.** A model mostly calls a tool with a flag without setting it **[unverified]**, and with two tools it picks between them inconsistently, so a state the model chose would record its guess. The gateway knows whether the work finished.

**Results.** `session.close` returns the child's terminal state and its `TaskResult`, and the parent receives no child event for a child it closed itself. `TaskResult.output` of a `completed` close is the last end-of-turn message, and of a completion by `complete: true` the completing message ("MD-22").

**Hand-off (decisions 21, 32, and 33 of 0166).** A root that releases one of its children with `session.release` and `handoff: true` hands its conversation to that child and ends. 0166 "Leaving the parent" in "DP-2" states the release, what a leave carries, and the rule that a root alone may set `handoff: true`. The end of the root is this section's:

1. The gateway commits the release first. A release that fails, or that its caveats refuse, ends nothing.
2. In the same write that ends the root, the gateway records `successorSessionId` on the root's row, naming the released session, and `GET /v1/sessions/{id}` returns it.
3. The root ends `completed` with no result. The close rule does not apply, because the root's runtime called the release inside an open turn, and that turn is cut. Everything else follows a close: the inbox closes, `onParentEnd` applies to the root's remaining children (SLR-25), its pod is released, and its workspace is sealed under its effective `checkpointPolicy`. The released session left the root before the end, so the cascade does not reach it.
4. The root's event stream ends with a `handoff` event naming the successor, and a webhook subscription receives the same event.
5. A message sent to the root afterwards, and a resume, are refused with `409 SESSION_HANDED_OFF`, which names the successor in its details.

[`0138-rest-only-client-api`, "RO-2"](../0138-rest-only-client-api/change.md#ro-2-rest-additions) owns `successorSessionId` on the session's representation, the `handoff` event and its webhook kind, and the `409 SESSION_HANDED_OFF` answer with its details. A client, or an MCP or A2A adapter, that reads `successorSessionId` or the `handoff` event sends its next message to the successor, which is a root of the same principal with the root's client as its client. The hand-off swaps no session ID (decision 21 of 0166).

### SL-8 Parents, children, and their states

**Child events by the parent's state.** The parent's `wakeOn`, which 0166 "DP-4" defines, decides which child events open a turn in the parent or wake it, and "MD-22" of 0135 states how the gateway delivers by it. An event in `wakeOn` is one whose class or kind the set names, and by default every class except `update` is in it. A child event outside `wakeOn` is never the cause of a turn or a wake: it reaches an open turn, or waits in the inbox for the parent's next turn.

| Parent state | Its children | Child events in `wakeOn` | Child events outside `wakeOn` |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `running` | They keep running. A runtime calls `session.create` during a turn, so a child starts while its parent is `running`. | They are written after the current tool result on a `steer` runtime, and at the turn's end on other runtimes, where each opens the next turn ("MD-22"). | They are written after the current tool result on a `steer` runtime, and on other runtimes they wait in the inbox for the parent's next turn. |
| `idle` | They keep running. | Each opens a turn. | They wait in the inbox for the parent's next turn. |
| `dormant` | They keep running. "SL-6" makes this rare, because a parent becomes `dormant` only when no descendant runs. | Each wakes the parent through resume admission and a restore, then opens a turn. A refused admission holds the event with `resume_refused` ("MD-22"). | They wait in the inbox and wake nothing. |
| `suspended`, `blocked` | They are paused with it. | They are held (SLR-5). | They are held (SLR-5). |
| `resume_pending`, `resuming` | They keep running. | They wait in the inbox, and "SL-12" states what the restore resends. | As for events in `wakeOn`. |
| `awaiting_client_action` | They keep running, and their terminal outcomes are archived in `session_tree_archive`, as today. | They are held until the parent resumes, and none expires while the parent lives (SLR-14). | As for events in `wakeOn`. |
| Terminal | `onParentEnd` applies ("SL-9"). | The inbox is closed, and a later child event goes only to the archive. | As for events in `wakeOn`. |

**Child events by the child's state.** The event names and their classes are those of "MD-22".

| Child state or change | What the parent receives |
| :-- | :-- |
| A mid-turn `message` | A `message` child event, of the class `update`, which the default `wakeOn` leaves out. |
| `running` to `idle` | An `end_of_turn` child event. |
| `running` to `dormant` by the idle clock | An `end_of_turn` child event with `error: "idle_timeout"` ("SL-6"). |
| `idle` to `dormant` | No event. A `session.send` wakes the child. |
| `running` stopped by a suspension | No `end_of_turn` event, also when the runtime acknowledges with an end-of-turn `message` ("SL-3"). |
| `blocked` | No event, because the parent is paused as well. |
| `suspended` directly by the root's client, and its resume | A `suspended` child event, then a `resumed` child event. |
| `resume_pending`, `resuming` | No event while the child recovers, and the parent's messages wait in the child's inbox. When a pod loss cut the child's turn, the parent receives an `end_of_turn` child event with `error: "turn_lost"` once the restore commits, and the child receives the resume notice (L16). A restore that commits into a state other than `running` answers a pending `events.wait` that covers the child ("SL-12"). |
| `awaiting_client_action` | An `awaiting_client_action` child event, marked recoverable. The parent closes the child or resumes it with `session.send` (L7). |
| A question the child raises | A `question` child event ("MD-22"), unless the parent created the child with `questions: forward`, under which the question passes one hop up without delivery to the parent (0166 "DP-4"). |
| A call to a tool the parent offers | A `tool_call` child event, under the blocking interface of [`0122-client-tools`](../0122-client-tools/change.md) and 0166 "DP-8". |
| A leave by `session.transfer` or `session.release` | A `left` child event naming the mode, as the last event from the session (0166 "Leaving the parent" in "DP-2"). |
| Any terminal state the parent did not cause | One `ended` child event with the state and the `TaskResult`. It covers `completed`, `failed`, `cancelled`, and `expired`, and replaces §8.10's `child_failed`. An `ended` event with `failed`, `cancelled`, or `expired` is of the class `status`, so a parent that leaves the kind `expired` out of its `wakeOn` receives an expiry at its next turn without being woken (0166 "DP-3", decisions 13 and 25). |

**No deadlock to detect.** The subtree deadlock detector of §8.8 looks for sessions blocked in `input_required`, which leaves. A session that waits for its client is `idle` and waits for no other session. `events.wait` returns at once for a covered child that is waiting or paused with nothing new to report, and as soon as a covered child ends for any reason ("MD-22"). An elicitation that travels the §9.2 chain is bounded by its per-hop forwarding timeout between parents that forward it, and by `maxElicitationWait` while it waits at a parent ("MD-22" of 0135, MDR-64). The detector, `deadlock_detected`, `maxDeadlockWaitSeconds`, and `DEADLOCK_TIMEOUT` therefore leave.

### SL-9 When a parent ends

The owner settled L4, L6, and L10 on 2026-11-11 **[design]**.

**Order.** At a parent's terminal transition, whatever its cause:

1. The parent's inbox closes. A child event that arrives later is recorded in `session_tree_archive` and opens no turn (SLR-10).
2. The gateway applies `onParentEnd` to the parent's non-terminal children.
3. Messages still in the inbox are drained to the DLQ and reported as `target_terminated`, as today (§7.2 "Inbox drain on terminal transition").

**`onParentEnd`.** It replaces `cascadeOnFailure` and keeps its default, `close_all`. It is a security caveat of the session grant, ordered `close_all` before `let_finish`, so a child's value is its parent's or `close_all`; 0166 "Ending a tree" in "DP-2" owns that ordering, and this section owns the cascade.

| Value | Effect on each non-terminal child |
| :-- | :-- |
| `close_all`, the default | The child is closed at once by the close rule, and so is each of its descendants. |
| `let_finish` | A `running` child keeps working, up to `cascadeTimeoutSeconds`, and is closed by the close rule when its turn ends. A child in a waiting state or in `awaiting_client_action` is closed at once, because no client is left to resume it. A child in `resume_pending` or `resuming` is handled by where its restore lands: a restore that writes the resume notice continues the cut turn as a `running` child, and any other landing closes the child at once. A paused child stays paused and ends at its own `maxSuspendedSessionSeconds` or at `cascadeTimeoutSeconds`, whichever comes first (L4). A child still `running` at `cascadeTimeoutSeconds` expires. |

**A `blocked` session that ends.** At the terminal transition of a `blocked` session, the gateway re-points every session whose `paused_ancestor_id` names it to the ended session's own `paused_ancestor_id`, so its `blocked` descendants stay under a paused ancestor that a resume reaches (SLR-28). As a safety net, backlog resume recomputes the nearest paused ancestor of a `blocked` session whose `paused_ancestor_id` names a terminal session that is not `ended_suspended`.

**A suspension root that ends.** A session that reaches a terminal state while `suspended` records `ended_suspended`. Under `let_finish`, the sessions `blocked` under it stay `blocked`, and nothing resumes them: backlog resume and the restore landing treat such a root as suspended (SLR-15).

`detach` leaves (L6). Its orphans had no client and no result reader, and under the waiting states a child that outlives its parent would wait for a client that no longer exists. The orphan cleanup job keeps its role for `let_finish` children and for a cascade a replica failure interrupted, and `maxOrphanTasksPerTenant` applies to `let_finish`, which falls back to `close_all` for a delegation that would exceed it, as it does for `detach` today.

**A child's result.** A child closed by a cascade has a `TaskResult` in `session_tree_archive` under the close rule, readable through `GET /v1/sessions/{id}/tree`. No parent receives it, because the parent is terminal.

**Force-terminate and tree terminate.** An operator's force-terminate of any session closes each of its descendants at once by the close rule, whatever `onParentEnd` holds, and so does a `tenant-admin`'s `POST /v1/admin/sessions/{rootId}/terminate-tree`, which 0166 "DP-2" adds for a tree of its own tenant (DPR-6 of 0166). The order above holds at each session: its inbox closes before its children are closed.

**Sessions that left their parent.** 0166 DPR-16 states which cascades reach a session that left its parent under "Leaving the parent" in "DP-2" (decisions 32 and 46), and the cascade applies it as follows:

- A transferred session is no longer its former parent's child, so its former parent's end, and the end of any session between the root and its former parent, does not reach it. It stays in its root's tree: the root's `onParentEnd` applies to it at the root's end as to a child of the root, and a force-terminate of the root and a tree terminate close it.
- A released session is a root. No cascade, force-terminate, or tree terminate of its former tree reaches it, and its own children stay under it, so its own end applies its `onParentEnd` to them.

### SL-10 Admission, caps, and timers

**Caps [design, SLR-12].** `CountsTowardConcurrencyCaps` and `countedStatePredicate` count every non-terminal state except `dormant`, `suspended`, and `blocked`. An `idle` session counts, as a `running` session with no open turn counts today, and a `dormant` session does not, as an idle-suspended session does not today. A paused session that keeps its pod under `sealOnly` or `none` is not counted under this rule, and whether it counts is open decision SL-Q3.

**A parent's parallelism [design].** A child counts toward its parent's `maxParallelChildren` in every state the caps count and in `blocked`, under 0166 "Parallelism" in "DP-3", which owns that count and its reason: a `blocked` child holds its place in its parent's work. The caps already count the setup states, `resume_pending`, `resuming`, and `awaiting_client_action`, so a restored parent cannot start new children while its old ones recover, and `blocked` is the one state in which the two counts differ. This list is the one statement of the states the caps count, and 0166 cites it.

**Resume admission [design].** §11.1's check runs at every move from `dormant`, `suspended`, or `blocked` toward a pod: `dormant → resume_pending`, and `suspended` or `blocked` to `idle` or `resume_pending`. A paused session that lost its pod while paused resumes through `resume_pending`, so its restore passes the check (SLR-16). A retry from `awaiting_client_action` runs the budget admission of 0170 "BG-6" alone, because the caps already count that state (L41). The check includes the budget admission of [`0170-budgets`, "BG-6"](../0170-budgets/change.md#bg-6-enforcement), which refuses a move toward a pod while a limit of the session's budget set that it would charge is exhausted, a limit on a storage meter included, and while a limit of the tree's session budget or a cap on the session's path whose `onExhausted.running` resolved to `suspend` has no room, whatever meter it counts (L41). A refused client message answers `429 QUOTA_EXCEEDED`, or `429 BUDGET_EXHAUSTED` when a budget refused it, as 0170 states. A refused child event or system message stays `pending` with `resume_refused`. A `blocked` descendant whose tree resume is refused stays `blocked`, with its own subtree `blocked` under it (L25), and backlog resume evaluates it again, as it evaluates a refused backlog resume today. §11.1 **Resume admission.** runs "the check that session creation runs", and session creation in the tree runs further gates besides the caps (`createAdmissionGates` in `pkg/gateway/core/session/sessionserver/create.go`: the store-availability check, the lifecycle fence of §12.8, the admission-rate limit, the policy chain, and environment admission, with the pool-drain gate that `0161` added to create-and-start); which of these a resume runs, and whether a tree resume admits its descendants one at a time rather than under §11.1 **Concurrent admissions.**, is open decision SL-Q12.

**Timers.**

| Timer | Runs in | Ends in |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| `maxSessionAge` | `running` and `idle`. It pauses in `dormant`, `suspended`, `blocked`, `resume_pending`, `resuming`, and `awaiting_client_action`, as §6.2 pauses it in today's counterparts, and `finalizing`, `ready`, and `starting` keep their dedicated watchdogs. | `expired` |
| `maxClientIdleSeconds` | `running` and `idle` ("SL-6") | `dormant`, with the turn cut from `running`, or `expired` for a session that cannot become `dormant` |

It does not run for a session whose effective `checkpointPolicy` is `sealOnly` or `none`, which keeps its pod (IC-8). | Pod release, which for a suspension that cannot take hold is forced, and the session resumes from its last good checkpoint (L24) |
| `budgets.suspendPodHoldSeconds` of 0170 | `suspended` with `suspension_cause: budget` or `external_limit`, and `blocked` under such a session, in place of `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds`, and a paused session that holds a pod from a compute tick a budget refuses (L37) | Pod release after a checkpoint, or `expired` with the reason `budget_exhausted` under `sealOnly` or `none` ("Budget suspension" in "SL-4") |

### SL-11 External mappings

> **Adapter-facing parts.** The A2A table, the MCP Tasks pointer, and decision L22 move to the A2A and MCP adapters under O8 of [`0138-rest-only-client-api`](../0138-rest-only-client-api/change.md), where that design's D4 replaces the session-level A2A mapping below with one task per message; they stay here unchanged until each adapter's repository takes them, and stage no edit to Lenny's specification. **`state_change`** and **Removed text** still govern Lenny.

**A2A [design].** §8.8's "Session-level state mapping (supplementary)" changes for A2A:

| Lenny state | A2A |
| :-- | :-- |
| `running` | `working` |
| `idle` | `input-required` |
| `dormant` | `input-required` with `metadata.dormant: true` |
| `suspended` | `working` with `metadata.paused: true` |
| `blocked` | `working` with `metadata.paused: true` |

Every turn end therefore reads as input required on A2A. That is accurate there: a session with no open turn waits for its client. A2A has no paused state, so the paused states read as `working` with a flag a client can check, and `metadata.paused` replaces today's `metadata.suspended` (L22). A2A keeps its spelling `canceled` for a cancelled session. A session in `awaiting_client_action` keeps the mapping `input-required`, and an A2A client, which has no resume call, resumes it with its next message ("SL-5").

**MCP Tasks.** [`0134-mcp-protocol-upgrade`, "MP-4"](../0134-mcp-protocol-upgrade/change.md#mp-4-tasks) owns the MCP projection and stages it in §8.8 in place of the "Protocol mapping", so this design stages no MCP row. Under MP-4 the projection uses the extension's statuses `working`, `input_required`, `completed`, `failed`, and `cancelled`, has no `submitted`, spells a cancelled session `cancelled`, and carries Lenny's metadata under `_meta` rather than in a `metadata` member. The projection makes each message a client sends one task, which completes at the end of the turn that consumes the message (L32). A session's states reach its tasks with the paused and dormant flags of the A2A table carried under `_meta`, and a task whose session is paused reads as `working`. An `idle`, `dormant`, or `awaiting_client_action` session has no task in `input_required`, a status the Tasks extension reserves for a task with `inputRequests` to answer, so it is left to elicitations. MP-4 states the projection, and the mapping moves with the MCP adapter out of Lenny under the owner's choice of 2026-11-13, which `0138-rest-only-client-api` governs.

**`state_change` [design].** The §15 `state_change` event carries the new states as top-level states and drops `subState`, whose values `input_required` and `suspended` leave with the sub-state model. A move to `suspended` carries `suspension_cause`, `client`, `budget`, or `external_limit`, and for `external_limit` the recorded detail, `status`, `message`, and `resetsAt`, which the gateway computes from a `retryAfter` when the signal names no `resetsAt`, with its `source`, `reporter` or `runtime` (L39, L43). §10.4's synthesized `status_change` on a coordinator handoff reads the new states.

**Removed text.** Every rule that names `input_required` is rewritten or removed in the sections "Current state" lists, §11.3's `maxRequestInputWait` among them, and "Specification changes" names each.

### SL-12 Recovery of child events

The owner's question L1 confirmed one inbox per session, and the owner settled L19 after the review of revision 1 **[design]**. Decision 15 of [`0166-delegation-primitives`](../0166-delegation-primitives/decisions.md#decisions) removes the bottom-up tree recovery of §8.10 and its timeouts and assigns this section the recovery rules that replace what that order implied ("DP-7" of 0166).

**No order across a tree (SLR-29).** Each session of a tree recovers under "SL-2" and §7.3 on its own, in parallel with the rest of its tree. No restore waits for another session's restore, and no timer bounds the recovery of a tree as a whole.

**A wait while a child recovers.** A child covered by a pending `events.wait` that commits out of `resume_pending` or `resuming` into any state other than `running` answers the wait with its state, so a wait does not outlast a restore that landed the child waiting, paused, or in `awaiting_client_action`. A covered child that is in `awaiting_client_action` when the wait is called counts as answered at once, because it cannot move until its client acts. "MD-22" of 0135 owns the wait, its modes, and its paging.

**Resent child events.** Every child event is an ordinary message in the parent's inbox. Under O4 of the message delivery design, when a session is restored from a checkpoint the gateway does not resend the messages its runtime consumed after that checkpoint, because a resend can repeat side effects. It reports each one as `consumed_unrecorded`, and the sender decides whether to send it again. A client can resend. A completed child cannot, and an idle child does not know it is expected to.

The gateway writes these child events from durable records, so it acts as their sender:

- For each `ended` child event the parent consumed after its checkpoint, the gateway writes the event again from `session_tree_archive` (SLR-11).
- For each `end_of_turn` child event the parent consumed after its checkpoint, the gateway writes the event again from the child's transcript, which records the child's end-of-turn `message` (L19).
- For each question still pending in its elicitation record whose hop is the parent, the gateway writes the `question` child event again with its original `questionId`. A restore resends a question only to the hop that holds it, under the hop record of "Questions and approvals" in "MD-22" of 0135, so a question the parent forwarded is resent to the session where it now waits and the parent does not receive it again.
- For each child still in `awaiting_client_action`, the gateway writes the `awaiting_client_action` child event again, so a restored parent learns which children wait for it.
- For each call that a child made to a tool the parent offers and that is still open, because the parent has not answered it with `tools.result` and the gateway has not withdrawn it, the gateway writes the `tool_call` child event again with its original `callId`. Delivery to a parent is at least once, and a handler keys its side effects on `callId` (0166 "Parent tools" in "DP-8").
- A child's mid-turn `message` events take the `consumed_unrecorded` notice of other messages, and their content stays readable in the child's transcript.

Each resent event carries its original message id, so the deduplication of "MD-3" drops it for a restored runtime whose own history already holds it. Its inbox entry does not move to `consumed_unrecorded`. Inside the gateway's restore step, under the parent's delivery lock, it moves from `consumed` back to `pending` and is delivered again with its original message id, as "MD-3" and MDR-15 of the message delivery design state. Each resent event follows the parent's `wakeOn` as any child event does ("SL-8"). The resend writes through the operation for child events into the parent's inbox that 0135.S5 adds to the gateway seam ("MD-22", **Recovery**), and 0146.S2 adds no second operation.

**Events a wait took.** Events that an `events.wait` took are `delivered` with the wait's call id. They become `consumed` when the adapter acknowledges the result, and they return to `pending` when a pod loss or a coordinator takeover finds no acknowledged result, so a parent that lost its pod while it read a wait's result receives those events again. "MD-3" of 0135 owns that custody. Once consumed, they fall under the resends above.

**Questions while a parent is away.** A question waiting at a parent outlives the parent's recovery: its timeout, its withdrawal on expiry, and the outcomes of `questions.answer` are those of "Questions and approvals" in "MD-22" of 0135 (MDR-64), and a restore resends no expired question.

**The children message.** At the restore's commit, after the resends, the gateway writes a system message to the restored parent's runtime when the parent has a non-terminal child or a pending question: `from.kind: system`, listing each non-terminal child with its state and its tags, and naming each pending question by its `questionId`. It carries no text that a child wrote. It takes the `append` intent of "MD-1", so it opens no turn of its own, and it is written before the resume notice of "SL-3", so a runtime that resumes its work reads it first. It replaces the `children_reattached` event, which leaves the §7.2 event list (decision 47 of 0166). A client reads the tree with `GET /v1/sessions/{id}/tree` after `session.resumed`.

**A child's own restore.** A child whose own pod loss cut its turn reports `turn_lost` to its parent when its restore commits ("SL-3").

**A parent that does not recover.** 0166 removes the tree recovery timeouts of §8.10, `maxLevelRecoverySeconds` and `maxTreeRecoverySeconds`, which moved a session still in `resume_pending` to `failed` when a level or the whole tree did not recover in time ("Limits" of 0166). §7.2's transition `resume_pending → failed` on those timeouts leaves with them. A parent that cannot be restored now moves to `awaiting_client_action` at `maxResumeWindowSeconds` and expires at `maxAwaitingClientActionSeconds`, and only then does `onParentEnd` apply to its children (SLR-25). Until then the parent counts against the caps and holds its budget, its children keep running with no session to answer their questions or their tool calls, and their tree's session budget and their caps bound what they spend. A client that does not want to wait closes the root, which closes the tree under `onParentEnd`.

With `messaging.durableInbox: false`, pending entries can still be lost when the coordinating replica crashes, as today, and `ended` and end-of-turn child events are recovered from the archive and the child's transcript in that case too.

## Worked example

Alice, of tenant `acme`, runs a `claude-code` session `sess_A` through REST. `claude-code` declares `interrupt` and `checkpoint`. Its model starts two children with the reference tool `start_session`, which calls `session.create` (0166 "DP-5"): `sess_B` to bisect a failing build and `sess_C` to update the changelog.

1. `sess_C` writes the changelog and ends its turn. It is `idle`, and `sess_A` receives an `end_of_turn` child event. `sess_B` keeps running.
2. `sess_A` ends its own turn while `sess_B` works. It is `idle`, and it keeps its pod past `maxClientIdleSeconds` because `sess_B` is `running`.
3. Alice suspends `sess_A` with `POST /v1/sessions/sess_A/suspend`. `sess_A` had no open turn and becomes `suspended`. `sess_B` becomes `blocked` with `resume_notice_due`, its runtime acknowledges the `interrupt_request` at a safe point, and it keeps its pod for the hold. `claude-code` had reported its task consumed with a `consumed` frame early in the turn, so the closing `message` returns nothing to `pending`, and `sess_B` keeps `resume_notice_due`. `sess_C` becomes `blocked`.
4. `sess_B` had written a mid-turn message shortly before the suspension stopped it. The `message` child event reaches `sess_A`'s inbox and is held, because `sess_A` is `suspended`.
5. Alice sends `sess_A` "Resume, and skip the changelog." The message resumes `sess_A`, then `sess_B` and `sess_C`, each through resume admission. `sess_B` receives the resume notice, because no message of its stopped turn returned to `pending`, and continues bisecting. `sess_A` receives no notice, because Alice's message lifted its suspension, and it receives the held child event and then Alice's message, which opens a turn.
6. `sess_A` closes `sess_C` with `session.close`. `sess_C`'s last turn ended with a message, so it ends `completed` with the changelog summary as its result.
7. Alice closes `sess_A` with `POST /v1/sessions/sess_A/close` while `sess_B` still runs. `sess_A`'s inbox closes, and under `close_all` `sess_B` is closed with its turn open, so it ends `cancelled`. `sess_A` had an open turn, so it ends `cancelled` too.

## Limits

- **A suspension without `interrupt` loses the runtime's in-memory state.** A runtime that does not declare `interrupt`, or one that does not acknowledge `interrupt_request`, loses its pod at once, and the restore at the resume starts from the checkpoint ("SL-3").
- **On a pool without slot attribution, a runtime keeps its egress until the release.** The egress halt of step 1 of "SL-3" needs slot attribution, so on such a pool a runtime that calls an LLM gateway, a provider ([LU-7](../0133-llm-upstreams-and-cost-budgets/change.md#lu-7-calls-outside-the-gateway)), or an operator service keeps calling it until the pod is released, which a failing checkpoint can delay up to `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds` (L24, CR-4).
- **A `netagent` restart briefly lifts a halt.** A `netagent` restart drops every halt, with egress closed while it restarts, and the halt lapses between its ready report and the adapter's reconnection, which re-sends `halt_slot` for each slot still halted (CR-4).
- **A session under `sealOnly` or `none` holds its pod.** It never becomes `dormant`, so an idle session keeps its pod until the idle timeout ends it, and a suspension that cannot take hold keeps the pod with the session's calls refused and its slot's egress halted, while on a pool without slot attribution a runtime that calls a provider outside the proxy can keep calling it until the session resumes or ends ("SL-3", "SL-6").
- **A forced release loses work.** When the checkpoint keeps failing until `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds`, the session resumes from its last good checkpoint, and the work after it is lost (L24).
- **A silent runtime loses its turn.** A runtime that works for `maxClientIdleSeconds` without writing a frame, reporting `busy`, or calling a tool has its turn cut ("SL-6").
- **A resumed tree repeats work.** A turn the suspension cut cannot continue where it stopped, and the resume notice starts a new turn, which can repeat steps the cut turn had taken.
- **A turn lost with a pod can run twice.** A client that resends its task after a `turn_lost` report, while the session continues from its resume notice, starts the work twice.
- **A tree resume can be partly refused.** Each `blocked` descendant passes resume admission on its own, so a tree at its tenant's cap resumes partly, and the rest resumes as backlog resume admits it.
- **A partial suspension lasts until the next sweep.** After a coordinator crash during a tree suspension, a descendant keeps working until the leader's backlog sweep blocks it ("SL-4").
- **The close rule cannot tell a satisfied close from an abandoned one.** A parent that closes an idle child whose answer it rejected records `completed`, because the child finished its turn.
- **A close in setup records `cancelled`.** A client that ends a session before its first turn sees `cancelled` where `POST /terminate` recorded `completed`.
- **Only the root's client lifts a suspension.** A parent whose child the root's client suspended cannot resume it, and its messages to the child wait.
- **A tree whose parent cannot recover waits.** With `maxTreeRecoverySeconds` removed (decision 15 of 0166), a parent that cannot be restored counts against the caps and holds its budget for `maxResumeWindowSeconds` plus `maxAwaitingClientActionSeconds` before `onParentEnd` closes its children, and meanwhile its children keep running with no session to answer them. Their tree's session budget and their caps bound what they spend ("SL-12").
- **A hand-off cuts the root's turn.** The root ends inside the turn that called the release, so output the root's runtime writes after the release does not reach its client, and the root records `completed` with no result ("SL-7").

## Alternatives rejected

- **One `suspended` state with a reason field.** It is today's model. What wakes a session and what a resume restores depend on the field, so every reader of the state also reads the field (L13).
- **The pod as a row property for every state.** The caps, resume admission, and a client reading the state would each combine the state with the property for a waiting session, where today the state alone tells them. A separate no-pod state for every paused state would double the paused states, and nothing reads the pod of a paused session except the path a resume takes (L14).
- **Suspending only the session the client named, with child events held as the only guard.** A client may not know that children run, and means everything when it suspends (L2).
- **Leaving a suspended turn open.** The harnesses cannot pause a turn and continue it, and a pod released during the suspension loses the turn in any case (L8).
- **Holding the pod of a runtime that did not stop.** The runtime would keep calling the model and its operator services and raising approvals for up to `maxSuspendedPodHoldSeconds` on every session of the tree, which defeats a suspension meant to stop the spending. Refusing those calls while the pod is held would leave the runtime inside a turn the gateway considers cut (L15).
- **Resuming without a notice.** Each resumed session would wait for a message, so the root's model would have to steer every child again.
- **Only a notice, or only a report, after a pod loss.** A notice alone leaves the client unaware when the runtime ignores it or cannot continue, and a report alone leaves the session idle until its client resends the task (L16).
- **Bounding a silent turn by `maxSessionAge` alone.** A turn whose end-of-turn frame was lost would hold its pod, and every ancestor's, for the session's whole age (L17).
- **Stopping a silent turn in `idle`.** The session would hold its pod for a second window after a whole window of silence (L17).
- **A `cancel` flag on the close tool, or two close tools.** The model would choose the state, and models leave a flag unset or pick between tools inconsistently. The gateway derives the state from facts (L9).
- **Inferring nothing, with every close recording `completed`.** External task protocols, the tree's readers, and outcome dashboards would read abandoned work as finished.
- **Keeping `detach`.** Its children had no client and no reader of their results, and a child that waits after a turn would wait until `cascadeTimeoutSeconds` (L6).
- **A parent's operation to suspend a child.** No use case in the review needed it, and the lifting rule of "SL-4" already accommodates one later (L12).
- **Recovering a tree from the bottom up.** §8.10 restored the children before the parent and failed the tree at `maxTreeRecoverySeconds`. The order made each restore wait on others and needed a re-await protocol, and the recovery rules of "SL-12" let each session recover on its own (decision 15 of 0166).
- **Ending a handed-off root by the close rule.** The root's runtime always calls the release inside an open turn, so the close rule would record every hand-off as `cancelled`, which reads as abandoned work to an A2A or MCP Tasks client ("SL-7").
- **Keeping the deadlock detector.** It detects waits on `input_required`, which leaves, and no remaining state waits on another session.
