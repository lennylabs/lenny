# Decisions: 0126 Delegation availability

## Open decisions

The M3 readiness review of 2026-11-19 raised O9 to O17, and revision 14 raised O18 and O19 from [`0166-delegation-primitives`](../0166-delegation-primitives/decisions.md#decisions). None has an owner answer yet. The numbering continues from O7; O2, O6, and O8 were the numbers revision 2 gave to D2, D4, and D5.

- **O9. Which step adds `capabilities.childSessions` and the delegation condition that reads it, and where the `inputPolicy` rule for `ask_question` lands.** 0135.S5 of [`0135-message-delivery`](../0135-message-delivery/steps.md#steps) depends on 0126.S1 and says it needs this change "for `capabilities.childSessions`", and 0135's §5.1 row also adds the declaration, while 0126.S1 placed the condition and the tool names "with or after" 0135.S5. The `ask_question` rule of "DA-4" reads `inputPolicy`, which R12 adds in 0138.S1 of [`0138-rest-only-client-api`](../0138-rest-only-client-api/steps.md#steps), and 0138.S1 follows 0126.S1 through 0135.S5, so no edge from 0126.S1 to either step can be added without a cycle. The consistency reports disagree: client-api-sessions-C9 recommends that 0126.S1 own the capability, and program-C9 recommends moving it into 0135.S5. Options: (a) 0126.S1 adds the Runtime field and the condition and lands whole before 0135.S5; 0135 removes the declaration from its §5.1 row and points "MD-22" to this change; the `inputPolicy` rule moves to 0138.S1, and until it lands the list applies the §9.2 suppression rule alone. (b) 0135.S5 adds the field and the condition, and its edge to 0126.S1 no longer names the capability; a second step of this change after 0135.S5 and 0138.S1 adds the `disabledBy: capability` check, the list rules that read the capability, and the `inputPolicy` rule. (c) 0135.S5 adds the field and the condition as program-C9 states, and the `inputPolicy` rule moves to 0138.S1. Recommendation: (a). "Non-specification changes" already places the field on the Runtime resource, 0135.S5 keeps the rendering rule, the conformance case, and the renames, and the `inputPolicy` rule then lands in the step that adds `inputPolicy`. Blocks: 0126.S1, 0135.S5, and 0138.S1; 0135 records the same conflict as O22 and 0138 as O23. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0126-OD1, 0126-F2, client-api-sessions-C9, program-C9)
- **O10. How the tenant default is written.** "DA-1" states a sub-resource, and "Validation owed" listed the question as open. Options: (a) the sub-resource `GET` and `PUT /v1/admin/tenants/{id}/delegation` with `If-Match`; (b) a field on `PUT /v1/admin/tenants/{id}`. Recommendation: (a). The tree serves `elicitation-content-integrity` and `rbac-config` as tenant sub-resources with ETags (`pkg/gateway/api/admin/routes.go`), and a sub-resource lets the route carry its own audit events. Under decision 26 of [`0166-delegation-primitives`](../0166-delegation-primitives/decisions.md#decisions), settled by the owner on 2026-11-23, the same route also carries the tenant's `policyRef` and `ceilings`, which 0166 "DP-1" reads as root grant inputs, so 0166's tenant delegation settings depend on this answer; under (b) the three members become fields of the tenant record. Blocks: 0126.S1, and the tenant inputs of 0166.S1. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0126-OD2, 0126-F10; 0166 dependency noted in revision 14)
- **O11. Where the tenant runtime entry's `allowDelegation: false` is stored.** "DA-1" places it on the per-tenant capability override record, and "Validation owed" listed the question as open. Under "Per-tenant capability override" of [`0140-runtime-revisions`](../0140-runtime-revisions/change.md#rv-1-revisions) the record "may only narrow `capabilities.interaction` and `capabilities.injection`" and applies over the chosen revision at the post-chain point, while this change reads `allowDelegation` at each call and each list. Options: (a) on the existing capability override record, with a sentence in "DA-1" that the record's capability axes apply to the chosen revision at session creation and `allowDelegation` is read at each call and each list, and 0140 admits the field; (b) a sibling record with the same key. Recommendation: (a). One record per tenant and runtime keeps one admin route, and the read time differs by field rather than by record. Blocks: 0126.S1; 0140 takes the matching edit. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0126-OD3, 0126-F10, 0126-F9)
- **O12. Which change closes F-8.3.14 and the second half of F-8.4.4.** F-8.3.14 (High, open in `BUILD-GAPS.md`) records that `start_child_session` does not apply the calling session's `DelegationPolicy` rules, and "Notes on the steps" says it "takes its own proposal". The second half of F-8.4.4, that no path stores `deny` on a lease, stays open after 0126.S1 removes the argument, and once `DELEGATION_DENIED` leaves no refusal code remains for a lease `approvalMode: deny`. No step of any member change lists either finding under `closes`. Options: (a) a new member change of the v1 program; (b) a second step of this change; (c) a part of 0149.S3, which reworks the delegation gate. Recommendation: (a). The fix has decisions of its own, such as how the policy's rules apply at delegation time and what a lease `deny` answers and whether it enters "may delegate", and the owner asked for its own proposal. [`0166-delegation-primitives`](../0166-delegation-primitives/change.md#dp-6-disposition-of-each-setting), which joined the program on 2026-11-23, is such a change: its "DP-6" checks the grant's policy cap at the post-chain point, "which also closes F-8.3.14", and drops `approvalMode` from the lease, so no path needs to store `deny`. Revision 14 therefore recommends that 0166.S1 list F-8.3.14 under `closes` and that 0166.S4 close the rest of F-8.4.4 with the lease field. Blocks: no step of this change; the exit of the delegation area's milestone. Whichever change owns F-8.3.14 evaluates the target rules at the post-chain point on the final runtime and pool, as the delegation-gates row of the post-chain table in [`0141-runtime-routing`](../0141-runtime-routing/change.md) states (requested by change 0141). (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0126-OD4, 0126-F11)
- **O13. The trigger and the audit chain of `delegation.availability_narrowed` and `delegation.availability_widened`.** "DA-1" writes an event when a write "changes the resolved availability of any running or future session", which a `platform-admin` write on a platform runtime can only evaluate by resolving every tenant, and the text names no audit chain for that write and no rule for a write under an active forbid. Options: (a) the direction follows the field transition alone: a write that sets `false` narrows, and a write that clears `false` or sets `true` widens, whatever the other levels hold; a write on a platform runtime is recorded on the platform's chain, and a write at a tenant level on the tenant's chain; (b) the handler resolves the availability of every affected tenant and writes an event on each affected tenant's chain under SO-4a only where the resolved value changes. Recommendation: (a). It is computable from the write alone, and a SIEM rule that alerts on widening still sees every write that can widen. Blocks: 0126.S1. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0126-F5)
- **O14. What `details.effectiveSettings` holds, and whether the pod receives it.** "DA-3" returns the values each level held, including the platform flags, the tenant default, and the active security record version, on a tool result that the runtime and its model read. Options: (a) list the members and return them to the pod, as §8.2's cycle refusal returns `effectiveSettings`; (b) return `disabledBy` and the name of the runtime or entry involved to the pod, and record the full settings in the delegation rejection audit only. Recommendation: (b). The model inside the pod is not a reader of the tenant's security posture, and an operator reads the full settings from the audit. Blocks: 0126.S1. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0126-F13)
- **O15. Whether a derive carries a source's explicit stored `maxChildrenTotal: 0`.** "Derive (O5)" under "DA-2" recomputes `maxDepth` from the source and does not state whether the explicit 0 that a parent's `lease_slice` stored on the source carries to the derived root. Options: (a) the derive carries it, so a session derived from a child that its parent created unable to delegate cannot delegate either; (b) the derive starts a new root and does not carry it. Recommendation: (a). DAR-3 states that a parent's `lease_slice.maxChildrenTotal: 0` is never lifted by a later hop or by lease extension, and a derive would otherwise lift it. Blocks: the derive narrowing of 0126.S1. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0126-F19)
- **O16. Whether an operator needs a per-tenant forbid that the tenant cannot lift.** A `platform-admin` can set the tenant default, which a `tenant-admin` can change, or write the delegation guardrail with a justification, which the tenant's `security-admin` can remove after the weakening delay. The only setting a tenant cannot lift is the platform forbid, which applies to every tenant. Options: (a) no per-tenant operator forbid; (b) a per-tenant forbid in the tenant security settings of [`0150-tenant-security-settings`](../0150-tenant-security-settings/change.md). Recommendation: (a). No use case for it has been named, and the owner's request named platform and tenant defaults. Blocks: no step. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0126-F21)
- **O17. Which step of 0142 the guardrail edge of 0126.S1 names.** 0126.S1 holds `0142.S2 (part)` for the guardrail row of the resolution. Open decision 41 of [`0142-security-oversight`](../0142-security-oversight/decisions.md) asks whether 0142.S2 splits into the record step, a step for the guardrail check, suspension, and exemptions, and a posture step. Options: (a) if 0142 splits, the edge of 0126.S1 moves to the new guardrail step, which carries the `"delegation": "forbid"` check that the resolution reads; (b) if 0142.S2 stays one step, the edge stays `0142.S2 (part)`. Recommendation: (a), following 0142's recommendation, because the edge then names the step that lands what 0126.S1 reads. The answer follows decision 41 of 0142, and this change makes no separate choice. Blocks: the depends-on of 0126.S1. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; requested by change 0142; mirrors decision 41 of 0142)

## Decisions

Settled by the owner on 2026-11-23, in answer to the open decisions that revision 14 raised:

| ID | Question | Owner's answer and what it settles |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| O18 | Whether a level that widens availability reaches a session created while delegation was unavailable. | "(a)". A session receives a grant at creation only when it may delegate then. A narrowing reaches every session at its next call, and a widening reaches only sessions created afterwards and sessions that already hold a grant. This follows 0166 decision 39 and departs from O4's live reads in the widening direction ("DA-2", "Limits"). |
| O19 | Whether the parent that gave a child an explicit `maxChildrenTotal: 0` can raise it later. | "(a)". The explicit 0 is a resource value, and the parent that set it may raise it with `session.set_limits`. A later hop and lease extension never raise it. The permanent switch is depth: a parent that passes `maxDepth: 0` for a child in `session.create` gives it a remaining depth of 0, a security caveat that nothing raises ("DA-6", DAR-3). |

Settled by an earlier owner decision or by a project rule:

| ID | Decision |
| :-- | :-- |
| S1 | A client disables delegation for one session and its tree with `delegationLease.maxDepth: 0` at creation. The owner settled it on 2026-11-03 as decision 26 of [`0149-tenant-scoping-identity`](../0149-tenant-scoping-identity/change.md#client-delegation-limits-decision-26), in the words "yes, add it with maxDepth: 0". O5 settles what decision 26 and D3 left open. |
| S2 | The refusal is the enforcement. Leaving an unavailable tool out of the platform tool list keeps its description out of the agent's context on every model call and keeps the agent from choosing a tool that will be refused. TS-3 states the first half in its words "Hiding is a convenience, and the refusal is the control", which this design keeps as the history of the rule. The owner stated the stronger reason on 2026-11-10: "It's more than a convenience. Tools cost context tokens and can confuse the agent." This design applies the rule at every level and to the inapplicable tools of "DA-4". |
| S3 | `allowDelegation` on the Runtime is a live field under [RV-1](../0140-runtime-revisions/change.md#rv-1-revisions). The ground is who writes it. A `platform-admin` writes it on a platform runtime and a `tenant-admin` on a tenant-derived runtime, as the authority over where and for whom the runtime runs, and the gateway enforces it, which is RV-1's definition of a live field. The image the runtime author builds does not carry the value, so a change to it describes no change to the binary and needs no revision. A binary that relies on delegation keeps delegating only while its admin leaves delegation enabled, and meets the refusal of "DA-3" otherwise. |
| S4 | No compatibility mode. Nothing is deployed, so the new fields carry no migration path, and the meaning of an explicit `maxChildrenTotal: 0` changes in place (DA-6). |

Settled by the owner on 2026-11-10. These decisions were open in revision 3 and keep their IDs, which the rest of this document cites:

| ID | Question | Owner's answer and what it settles |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| O1 | How the levels combine. (A) Restrict-only at every level, so any `false` wins and a platform "default" of off can never be turned on below it. (B) Defaults resolved by specificity, a runtime overriding the tenant default and the tenant default overriding the platform default, with forbids that nothing below lifts, and restrict-only between a runtime, its base, and a tenant runtime entry. (C) As (B) without forbids. | "ok" to (B). The more specific level wins among the defaults, and the platform forbid in Helm and the tenant forbid, which is the delegation guardrail (D5), override every other level, as the resolution table of "DA-2" states. The tenant runtime entry (D1) is the tenant's narrowing of a single runtime. The owner asked for a platform and a tenant *default*, which a more specific level must be able to override, and (A) gives none. Without a forbid, a tenant that wants no delegation cannot stop a platform runtime that sets `allowDelegation: true`, so (C) leaves the tenant without a hard switch. (B) follows the layering §8.6 uses for lease extension: defaults by specificity, capped by ceilings. |
| O3 | The platform default. | "ok" to `gateway.delegation.allowByDefault: true`. §8.1 describes delegation as a platform primitive that every agent runtime can use, a deployment that sets nothing behaves as today, and the depth, fan-out, budget, and tree-size limits of §8.3 still bound every tree. A deployer who wants delegation off by default sets the value to `false`. |
| O4 | How a change reaches a running session. Revision 3 offered (A) live reads in both directions with a notification to the runtime, (B) a snapshot at creation, and (C) live for a change that disables and a snapshot for one that enables, and recommended (A). | "ok" to option (D), a new option. The owner asked for "whichever means the smallest blast radius from an implementation point of view". The comparison rests on the tree: the adapter caches nothing today, and each `tools/list` is forwarded live to the gateway; `ListPlatformTools` returns one catalog for every session; and no list-change notification exists in the tree ("Current state"). Under (D) the `lenny/delegate_task` handler reads the current settings at each call, and `ListPlatformTools` filters the list per session from the current settings at each list. The design keeps no adapter cache, no notification RPC, no fan-out, no `listChanged` declaration, and no `subscriptions/listen`. A session that listed before a change keeps its list until its runtime lists again, and the refusal covers a stale entry. Most harnesses list once per connection. The notification machinery can be added later without changing (D) ("DA-5"). The session's own choice (S1) stays stored, because it is the client's and nothing lifts it. "Alternatives rejected" records revision 3's (A), (B), and (C). **Reopened and settled again.** The owner reopened O4 on 2026-11-11 in [`0139-runtime-protocol`](../0139-runtime-protocol/decisions.md#decisions) decision D4, answering "yes" to reopening it so that a runtime learns of changes to what its session may use, and settled the mechanism on 2026-11-12 with "go with your recommendations" (RP O6 (e) and O8 (b)). The reads of (D) stay. The notice is added as "DA-5" foresaw: the coordinating replica recomputes `delegation` and `questions` when it opens or records a turn and sends `availability_changed` when either differs from what it last sent, with no fan-out from any write path and no copy of the list in the adapter ("RP-4"). |
| O5 | How derive and resume treat the session level. D3 settles that a session cannot change it mid-session. | "ok". A derive request accepts `delegationLease.maxDepth`, which can only lower the value TS-3 carries over from the source. A resume keeps the stored value ("Session level" under DA-2). |
| O7 | Whether any other platform tool needs a per-runtime switch. | "ok", with an extension. No new per-runtime switch is added: "DA-7" finds an existing control for each tool, some tools leave the surface under other designs, and a deployer who wants a tool held for approval uses an SO-5 rule. The extension: the per-session list filter of "DA-4" also leaves out the inapplicable tools, those whose call the session's existing settings guarantee to fail or to have no effect, so their descriptions cost no context. The refusals remain the enforcement. The extension adds one runtime declaration, `capabilities.harnessApprovalRequests`, which "MD-19" of [`0135-message-delivery`](../0135-message-delivery/change.md#md-19-harness-approvals) states. E3 withdrew that declaration and its list rule on 2026-11-10, and the rest of O7 stands (noted by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19). |
