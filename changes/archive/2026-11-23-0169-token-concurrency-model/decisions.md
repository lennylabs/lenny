# Decisions: 0169 A concurrency model of the token rules

## Open decisions

None. The owner settled open decisions 10 to 14 on 2026-11-23 (decisions 10 to 14).

## Decisions

1. **Build the TLA+ model now, as its own change (owner, 2026-11-23).** The owner decided on 2026-11-23 to build the TLA+ model of the concurrent orderings of the token rules now, as this change. This amends decision 14 of change 0165 (a formal model of the token rules), under which the model stayed a candidate until step 0165.S2 was done.
2. **The model checks 0168's locking design before its code lane (owner, 2026-11-23).** The owner decided on 2026-11-23 that the model checks the locking design of change 0168 (token rule fixes from the property tests), as its decisions 1, 2, 5, and 6 and its sections TF-1 to TF-5 state it, before the code lane of 0168.S1 is written. The specification lane of 0168.S1 may proceed in parallel. The counterexamples the model finds in that design are reported to the owner before the code lane starts.

The owner answered FM-19 to FM-25 on the M3 readiness decision page on 2026-11-23, each with option (a). The answers settle open decisions 3 to 9 of revision 1.

3. **PlusCal for the request processes (FM-19).** The request processes are written in PlusCal, with one label per statement or lock step, and Postgres, the deadlock detector, and the event bus are TLA+ operators and actions in the same module. The translator ships in `tla2tools.jar` (`pcal.trans`), the translation is committed in the module between the translator's markers, and the runner checks that it is current. Owner's answer to FM-19 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), the whole model in plain TLA+ as one next-state relation.
4. **TLC `v1.7.4` is pinned (FM-20).** The runner uses `tla2tools.jar` of the tlaplus release `v1.7.4`, downloaded from `https://github.com/tlaplus/tlaplus/releases/download/v1.7.4/tla2tools.jar`, with SHA-256 `936a262061c914694dfd669a543be24573c45d5aa0ff20a8b96b23d01e050e88`, and stops when the jar's SHA-256 differs. Owner's answer to FM-20 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), the pre-release `v1.8.0`.
5. **Directed configurations and one free configuration (FM-21).** The directed configurations run at 2 tenants, 2 users per tenant, 2 replicas, up to 3 request processes, up to 6 tokens, 1 abort per behavior, and the 3 cascade attempts the code makes, one configuration per ordering of "Scope" in `change.md`. One free configuration runs at 2 request processes and 5 tokens, in which each process chooses any operation. A configuration that does not finish within 12 hours is stopped, and the bound it reached is recorded. Owner's answer to FM-21 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The options not chosen were (b), free configurations only, and (c), directed configurations at 3 tenants as well.
6. **No tier of `lenny-test` runs the model (FM-22).** The model is a design check run by hand with `tests/formal/tokenconcurrency/run.sh`, which runs TLC in an `eclipse-temurin` container with `--network none`, the model and the jar mounted read-only, and the jar's SHA-256 checked, as `tests/formal/tokens/run.sh` runs the Alloy model. The change that edits a rule the model describes reruns it. Owner's answer to FM-22 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The options not chosen were (b), the static tier running the translator and the parser only, and (c), the release gate running the directed configurations.
7. **The model checks the `I13Lock` variant (FM-23).** Step 0169.S2 adds the variant `I13Lock`, which is `TF` with the per-tenant audit advisory lock taken by the cascade and by `RevokeBySubject` before the statement that reads their snapshot, as TOKX-I13 suggests, and checks C1, C5, C7, and C8 under it. Owner's answer to FM-23 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), checking `Main` and `TF` only.
8. **Steps 0169.S1 and 0169.S2 share one branch and one digest (FM-24).** Owner's answer to FM-24 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), separate branches with 0169.S1 merged first.
9. **No specification text (FM-25).** The properties stay in `change.md` and in the model's README, and each change that fixes a counterexample states in §13.3 the ordering it settles. Owner's answer to FM-25 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), a specification lane on 0169.S2's branch stating the confirmed properties in §13.3.

The owner answered FM-26 to FM-30 on the M3 readiness decision page on 2026-11-23, and answered a follow-up question on FM-30 in chat on the same day. The answers settle open decisions 10 to 14 of revision 3. 10. **The impersonation step's target-tenant updates run in the outer transaction (FM-26, C8).** The target-tenant updates of 0168's impersonation step run in the outer transaction rather than in nested transactions. The step sets the target tenant's context with `SET LOCAL app.current_tenant` before each update and the request's tenant after it, and retries on `40P01` as `RevokeLineage` retries the cascade. Every wait of R5 is then a lock wait, the deadlock detector aborts one of the two requests, and its retry finds its caller revoked by the other request and is refused. A scratch copy of the model with this ordering held C5, C8, and C9 in `nested_step` (93,083 distinct states). TF-2 of 0168 and the text of §4.2 that 0168's specification lane edits for nested transactions change with it. Owner's answer to FM-26 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The options not chosen were (b), keeping the nested transactions with a `lock_timeout` on each, and (c), keeping them and stating the wait in rule 7.
11. **The cascade and the full revoke take the per-tenant audit advisory lock, in change 0168 (FM-27, C1 and C7).** `revokeLineageOnce` and `RevokeBySubject` take the per-tenant audit advisory lock of §11.7 before the statement that reads their snapshot, as the `I13Lock` variant does, which closes TOKX-I13. The owner decided the bound of TOKX-I10 with it, in chat on 2026-11-23: an exchange whose subject token is already 32 links deep is refused, which needs a depth column on `issued_tokens` set at issuance to the parent's depth plus one; and the cascade and the full revoke's walk run under a 5-second statement timeout, matching the 5-second lock acquisition timeout of §11.7, so a cascade that reaches it revokes nothing and answers `503` with a retryable error. Both values are operator-tunable defaults. The option named the fix's home as change 0168 or a change of its own, and the owner left the packaging open; the orchestrator of the 2026-11-23 session placed it in change 0168, because 0168's code lane has not started and the model checked `I13Lock` on top of 0168's design. Owner's answer to FM-27 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23, and to the TOKX-I10 question in chat, 2026-11-23. The options not chosen were (b), keeping the window and stating it in §13.3 and §11.4, and (c), extending TF-4's user-row read to the playground mint; for TOKX-I10, a timeout only and a depth cap only.
12. **A bearer row without a start record is accepted and stated (FM-28, C6).** Rule 1 (c) of **Platform-admin impersonation.** states that a failed first transaction can leave a bearer row that no client holds, that the impersonation step does not reach, and that the `GET /v1/admin/impersonation` listing shows until its `exp`, a `DELETE`, or rule 6 ends it. Owner's answer to FM-28 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The options not chosen were (b), committing the start record before the bearer row in a transaction of its own, and (c), ending the bearer row with `EndImpersonation` on such a failure.
13. **The impersonation start reads the caller bearer's row under a share lock (FM-29, C6, gap G3).** The first transaction of rule 1 (c) reads the caller bearer's `issued_tokens` row under a share lock, which conflicts with the cascade's update of that row, so an impersonation either commits before the cascade's update reaches the bearer or reads the bearer revoked, and rule 7's window ends when the cascade commits. The change that carries it, 0168, reruns the model's `impersonation` and `nested_start` configurations. Owner's answer to FM-29 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (a), stating in rule 7 that the window includes an impersonation whose caller check precedes the cascade's commit and whose record commits after it.
14. **Each replica rehydrates its revocation cache when its event-bus subscription reconnects (FM-30, C4, gap G1).** On FM-30 the owner asked whether the code could be fixed with a small edit, to be filed as an issue, or needed a redesign, to be discussed. In chat on 2026-11-23 the orchestrator explained that §13.3 is inconsistent, because **Authoritative durability for revocation.** says every validation can fall back to `issued_tokens.revoked_at` while **Token rotation and revocation — no grace period.** describes the read only on a cache miss for a recently issued token, a case the 503 staleness gate already covers at startup, and that the gap is a lost `token.revoked` event. The owner chose an option that neither (a) nor (b) offered: each replica rehydrates its revocation cache immediately when its event-bus subscription reconnects, or when the bus reports a dropped message, and §13.3 states the bound, under which a lost event is reconciled by the next rehydration within 30 seconds and a replica refuses validation after 90 seconds without a successful rehydration. The per-request Postgres read is removed from the text, and no validation reads the database. The decision lands as change 0171 ([revocation cache rehydration on event-bus reconnect](../../0171-revocation-rehydrate-on-reconnect/change.md)) rather than as an issue. Owner's answer to FM-30 on the M3 readiness decision page and in chat, 2026-11-23. The options not chosen were (a), an issue under TOKX for the missing read, (b), a specification edit stating the guarantee the code gives, and a per-token Postgres read behind a short TTL cache (default 5 seconds).
