# Decisions: 0143 Service account credentials

## Open decisions

The owner settled D1 to D3 and O1 to O7 on 2026-11-15, and "Decisions" records them. The questions below are open.

- **O8. The Kubernetes service account path under SA-6.** `pkg/auth/authmw` admits a projected Kubernetes ServiceAccount token through `ServiceIdentity.ResolveService` in `pkg/gateway/api/serviceidentity` and builds a principal with `caller_type: service` whose subject is the username the TokenReview returns, such as `system:serviceaccount:lenny-system:lenny-ops-sa`, with no `~svc~` prefix. SA-6 item 1 names only the verifier registry's resolver and the deployment issuer's path, so SAP-10 does not hold for this principal: a deployment-source person whose `sub` equals that username holds the same Lenny subject as the platform service account. Options: (a) the resolver forms `~svc~system:serviceaccount:<namespace>:<name>`, SA-6 item 1 names `pkg/gateway/api/serviceidentity`, and its tests cover the prefix; (b) the path carries a distinct marker, a `caller_type` or a token `typ` of its own, that never owns a credential and sits outside SA-6, and SA-6 states that. Recommendation: (a), because it keeps one namespace rule for every `service` principal and needs no new caller type. Blocks 0143.S1. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0143-F2)
- **O9. The owner of SA-6, and the order of the `~svc~` validations in other changes.** 0143.S1 says it lands SA-6, and [`0149-tenant-scoping-identity`](../0149-tenant-scoping-identity/change.md), "Caller-type namespaces.", specifies and tests the same resolver refusal with `SUBJECT_RESERVED` in its §10.2 edit list. The writes that check the namespace live in other changes' steps: [`0152-user-git-identity`](../0152-user-git-identity/change.md) 0152.S2 (`serviceIdentities`), [`0142-security-oversight`](../0142-security-oversight/change.md) 0142.S2 (`lifecycleSubjects`), 0149.S8 (the SCIM refusal), and [`0124-connector-removal`](../0124-connector-removal/change.md) CR-2 (the subject without its prefix), and none of those steps is ordered against 0143.S1. A validation that lands first refuses every entry while no principal carries the prefix. Options: (a) 0143.S1 owns the resolver and token-issuance part of SA-6, 0149.S6 keeps the reservation of the source identifiers `svc` and `agt`, 0152.S2 and 0142.S2 depend on 0143.S1 (part), and 0149.S8 and the 0124 step that lands CR-2's subject either depend on 0143.S1 (part) or carry "whichever lands second adds the case"; (b) 0149.S6 owns the resolver part of SA-6, 0143.S1 depends on it as today and keeps SA-1 and the subject-naming writes, and the same edges follow from 0149.S6. Recommendation: (a), because SA-6 exists for service account credentials and 0143.S1 already lands the writes that name a subject. The other changes record the same question. Blocks 0143.S1. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0143-F3, credentials-git-C5)
- **O10. Who triggers an emergency rotation of the exchange key.** SA-3 **Rotation.** says that "An emergency rotation through the admin API drops the previous key at once and signs with the new key at once", and no route, permission, or caller is defined for it. The rotation can refuse every `issuerExchange` exchange of the tenant for up to `prePublishSeconds`, so whoever can call it can deny the tenant's provider access for about a day. [`0127-egress-and-git`](../0127-egress-and-git/change.md) has the same sentence for the federation issuer. Options: (a) `POST /v1/admin/tenants/{id}/exchange-issuer/rotate`, held by the tenant's `security-admin` and by a `platform-admin` with a justification mirrored to the tenant's chain under SO-4a, audited as `token_service.exchange_key_activated` with the reason `emergency`, with a `security` tier test of its authorization, and the federation issuer's emergency rotation aligned in 0127; (b) the same route held by a `platform-admin` alone, mirrored under SO-4a, so the operator decides on a key compromise; (c) the same route held by the tenant's `tenant-admin`. Recommendation: (a), because a key compromise concerns the tenant's security role and the operator who holds the key store, and the mirror keeps the operator's call visible to the tenant. Blocks 0143.S3. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0143-F4)
- **O11. The entry kinds `lifecycleSubjects` accepts.** SA-6 item 2 states that the tenant security record's `lifecycleSubjects` subject entries must begin with `~svc~`, as [`0142-security-oversight`](../0142-security-oversight/change.md) states for the record and [`0149-tenant-scoping-identity`](../0149-tenant-scoping-identity/change.md) states under "Caller-type namespaces.". 0149's TS-8 refuses a SCIM deprovisioning of a security principal unless `scim:<source_id>` is in `lifecycleSubjects`, and 0142.S2 and its IdP removal residual assume that SCIM works, so a `security-admin` cannot write the entry that TS-8 needs. Options: (a) `lifecycleSubjects` accepts a `~svc~` subject or `scim:<source_id>` naming a source of the tenant, a `scim:` entry for a retired source stops matching, and SA-6 item 2, 0142's row, and 0149's sentence say so, with validator and SCIM tests; (b) SCIM never deprovisions a security principal, and 0149 and 0142 drop that rule and its residual. Recommendation: (a). Blocks 0143.S1, which lands the prefix check of item 2, and item 2's prefix check follows the owner's answer. The same question is open decision 36 of [`0142-security-oversight`](../0142-security-oversight/decisions.md#open-decisions) and decision 33 of [`0149-tenant-scoping-identity`](../0149-tenant-scoping-identity/decisions.md), and the answer applies to all three changes. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: tenancy-security-C1)
- **O12. Details of SA-1 to SA-4 that the design leaves undefined.** Each item has its own recommendation. Options: (a) adopt each recommendation below; (b) settle the items one by one. Recommendation: (a). Items 1, 2, 3, and 5 block 0143.S2, item 4 blocks 0143.S1, and item 6 blocks 0143.S3. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; sources: 0143-F9, 0143-F11, 0143-F13, 0143-F16, 0143-F18)
   1. Key generation (0143-F9). "One pending key exists at a time" leaves a second `POST .../keys` undefined, and nothing states what the first `POST .../keys` does on a service account with no row, or the outcome of an `alg` that differs from `assertionAlg` or a `clientId` that differs from the row's. Recommendation: refuse a second pending key with `409`, rather than replacing it; create the row in a `pending` state that releases nothing; and refuse an `alg` or `clientId` mismatch with `422 VALIDATION_ERROR`.
   2. Short provider lifetimes (0143-F11). `cacheUntil` is zero or negative for an `expires_in` at or below 60 seconds, and every release then calls the provider. Recommendation: treat an `expires_in` below 120 seconds as `invalid_response`, rather than clamping `cacheUntil` to a floor and stating the call rate.
   3. A rate bound on `verify`, `activate`, and `retry` (0143-F13). Each makes one provider call, and the row lease covers refresh alone. Recommendation: run them under the row lease and the admin route rate limit, rather than a per-row cooldown of their own.
   4. The service account route and Article 18 (0143-F16). The person route of 0151 refuses a subject under a GDPR Article 18 restriction, the service account route does not, and no route lists the service accounts that hold rows. Recommendation: add the Article 18 refusal, and add `GET /v1/admin/service-accounts`, listing the subjects with rows, rather than leaving the list to posture.
   5. The row lease keys (0143-F18). "Configuration by scope" names no Helm key for the row lease of 30 seconds and its wait of 5 seconds. Recommendation: `tokenService.providerCalls.rowLeaseSeconds` and `tokenService.providerCalls.rowLeaseWaitSeconds`, rather than a key shared with UC-2's refresh lease, which [`0151-user-credential-delivery`](../0151-user-credential-delivery/change.md) does not name either.
   6. Publication of the exchange issuer's discovery (0143-F18). The unauthenticated `/exchange/<tenant-id>/` routes answer for every tenant, as `/oidc/` and `/identity/` do, which lets anyone test whether a tenant identifier exists. Recommendation: serve them only for a tenant that holds an approved `issuerExchange` type, and answer `404` otherwise, rather than serving every tenant.
- **O13. The serving path of the `/exchange/` issuer routes.** SA-3 **Discovery and key set routes.** has the Token Service serve the exchange issuer's discovery document and key set unauthenticated under `/exchange/<tenant-id>/` on the gateway ingress, and providers must reach them. Today the Token Service admits only gateway pods on its mTLS port, and SA-3 states no listener, port, or `lenny-system` ingress row. Options: (a) a dedicated read-only HTTP listener on the Token Service that serves only the discovery documents and key sets, with its own connection budget, a `lenny-system` ingress row that admits the ingress controller on that port alone, and no route to the mint path or the pod credential API, with a `security` test that the listener answers nothing but the issuer routes; (b) the gateway serves the documents from key material that the Token Service publishes. Recommendation: (a), because it keeps signing keys and key-set assembly in one component and adds one narrow NetworkPolicy row. Blocks 0143.S3 (the `/exchange/` routes, their ingress row, and their `security` case). The same question is open decision 79 of [`0127-egress-and-git`](../0127-egress-and-git/decisions.md#open-decisions) for `/oidc/`, `/identity/`, and `/exchange/` together, and O26 of [`0124-connector-removal`](../0124-connector-removal/decisions.md#open-decisions) for `/identity/`, and the owner's answer applies to all three changes. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; requested by change 0127; mirrors 0127 decision 79)
- **O14. The edges of 0143 on 0142.S2 if 0142.S2 splits.** 0143.S3 depends on 0142.S2 for the tenant security record's `tokenTrust.allowedRecords`, the suspension of an `issuerExchange` type whose record is not listed, and the posture report of `issuerExchange` types with their approval state. Open decision 41 of [`0142-security-oversight`](../0142-security-oversight/decisions.md#open-decisions) asks whether 0142.S2 splits into the record step, a guardrail and suspension step, and a posture step. Options: (a) if 0142 decision 41 is settled as its option (a), 0143.S3 keeps 0142.S2 for the record and the weakening delay, depends on the guardrail and suspension step for the `tokenTrust.allowedRecords` suspension, and depends on the posture step in part for the posture report, which lands with whichever of the two steps lands second; (b) if 0142 decision 41 is settled as its option (b), the edge stays on 0142.S2 unchanged. Recommendation: follow the owner's answer to 0142 decision 41, and apply (a) or (b) to match it. Blocks the `depends-on` of 0143.S3. (raised by the M3 readiness review, 2026-11-19; requested by change 0142; mirrors 0142 decision 41)

## Decisions

| ID | Decision |
| :-- | :-- |
| D1 | Service accounts own per-principal credentials under the `userCredential` machinery. The owner asked on 2026-11-15, "what about service accounts? A user may be a legitimate service account but have no human behind it", and adopted option (b): a tenant admin or backend (`registeredBy: [service]`) writes a token for a service account under a `source: registered` type, the Token Service releases it only to sessions whose row names that service account, and UCP-1 becomes the token of the principal on the session row for `human` and `service` callers. One token per principal, the rotation, revocation, and request rules, UC-3's refusals on reused pods and on pods without slot attribution, and the impersonation refusal are reused. `oauth2Link` stays human-only, `agent` callers stay refused (O4), and long-lived bearer `registered` tokens stay supported, as the owner confirmed (SA-1). |
| D2 | `source: clientCredentials`. The owner answered on 2026-11-15: "b + client credentials ... go ahead". The Token Service runs the client credentials grant of RFC 6749 section 4.4 with a service account's client identifier and client secret or private key, which no pod reads, caches the access token, requests a new one before expiry, and `netagent` injects it (SA-2, SA-4). |
| D3 | Provider trust of Lenny's issuer. The owner answered on 2026-11-15: "I think we should also support 'A provider can be set up to trust Lenny's own issuer directly'". The Token Service signs a JWT for the session's principal and exchanges it at the provider through the JWT bearer grant of RFC 7523 or the token exchange of RFC 8693, with cloud workload identity federation as the common case, and `netagent` injects the result, so Lenny stores no secret (SA-3). |
| Review | The owner answered the summary of the security review of revision 1 and its updated recommendations on 2026-11-15: "O1-O7: go with your recommendations". Revision 2 applies every finding of [`evidence/security-review.md`](evidence/security-review.md) and settles O1 to O7 below as recommended. |
| O1 | Exchange JWTs are signed under a separate exchange issuer, `https://<lenny>/exchange/<tenant-id>`, with its own RS256 key set, separate from the federation issuer and from the identity issuer of CR-2 (SA-3). This reverses revision 1's recommendation of the federation issuer, because review finding 3 showed that AWS and Entra cannot condition on a custom claim and AWS uses one audience for both kinds of JWT, so a trust that does not pin an exact subject admitted an agent-readable federation token as a service account. The cost is another public route, another key set to rotate, and another registration at each provider. |
| O2 | `delivery: file` of an access token from a `clientCredentials` or `issuerExchange` row is admitted under the `userCredentialDelivery` grant, a type ceiling of `file`, and the binding's opt-in, with the file holding the access token alone (SA-5). The agent can read an access token that lives up to the provider's lifetime, often one hour. |
| O3 | `issuerExchange` serves service accounts alone. A type naming `human` in `principalTypes` is refused (SA-3). Review finding 4 showed that `entraFederated` returns an application token with tenant-wide permissions rather than acting as the person, and that a tenant admin writing a person's row would attach that person to any provider identity. A person reaches such a provider with `registered` or `oauth2Link`. |
| O4 | `agent` callers stay refused for every source. §25.1's `agent` callers are operability service accounts holding `platform-admin` or `tenant-admin`, the roles that write credentials, so a credential owned by one would be written and used by the same automation. A deployer whose agent needs a provider credential runs it under a `service` principal. An `agent` caller's identity assertion carries `principal_type: agent` and the subject `lenny:<tenant>:agent:<id>` ([`0124-connector-removal`, "CR-2 Identity assertion"](../0124-connector-removal/change.md#cr-2-identity-assertion)), and its Lenny subject carries `~agt~` (SA-6). |
| O5 | Mutual TLS client authentication of RFC 8705 is left out of the first version. `private_key_jwt` gives the same property, no shared secret, through the mint path as it stands. A provider that offers only `tls_client_auth` or `self_signed_tls_client_auth` cannot be used. |
| O6 | The `awsWebIdentity` profile is admitted, and its credentials reach a pod under `delivery: metadata`, behind the new grant `userCredentialMetadataDelivery`, with `DurationSeconds` fixed at 900, exposure records, `lockEgressToHosts`, `leakScan`, and per-slot attribution (SA-5). The cost is AWS signing credentials within the agent's reach for 900 seconds, usable from anywhere unless the role conditions on the source address, with role chaining possible. |
| O7 | The default of `principalTypes` on a `registered` type is `[human]`. A tenant opts a type into service accounts by writing `[service]` or `[human, service]`, so every existing type is unchanged. This reverses revision 1's default of `[human, service]`. |
| SD1 | The source name `issuerExchange`, chosen by this design under the owner's instruction to choose a clear name. |
| SD2 | A dedicated admin route for a service account's rows, beside the person route, so a write states which principal type it means. |
| SD3 | The identifiers `userCredential`, `user_credential`, `USER_CREDENTIAL_*`, and `manage_user_credentials` keep their names for both principal types, because renaming them touches every design, schema, and error code for no change in behaviour. The documentation states that "user" in these names covers a service account. The grant `userCredentialMetadataDelivery` follows the same stem. |
