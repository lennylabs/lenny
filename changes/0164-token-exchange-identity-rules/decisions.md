# Decisions: 0164 Token exchange narrowing and identity rules

## Open decisions

None.

## Decisions

1. **How an exchange narrows a subject token without a `scope` claim (SM-5, TOKX-I3).** Settled by the owner on 2026-11-20 on the M3 readiness decision page, choosing option (a): the subset check reads an absent subject claim as unrestricted, as §25.1 **Matching.** states. A requested set is accepted when every value parses as a canonical scope and the subject's parsed set matches it, which also honours `tools:*` and `tools:<domain>:*` subjects. The `authorized_tools` allowlist of §13.3 rule (e) is kept when the issued scope is absent. The operator guide example that shows narrowing is corrected. The option not chosen was (b), stating that an unscoped subject cannot be narrowed through the exchange.
2. **A playground bearer as `subject_token` or `actor_token` (SM-6, TOKX-I4).** Settled by the owner on 2026-11-20, choosing option (a): the Token Service refuses a `subject_token` or `actor_token` whose `origin` claim is `playground` with `invalid_grant`. The option not chosen was (b), copying the subject's `origin` claim onto the issued token.
3. **The caller-identity rule and the lineage of derived tokens (SM-7, TOKX-I5).** Settled by the owner on 2026-11-20, choosing option (a): the Token Service enforces the rule §13.3 **Client authentication on `/v1/oauth/token`.** states. When the caller's `sub` or `caller_type` differs from the subject token's, the request must name a scope strictly narrower than the subject's, or the exchange is refused. Delegation child minting with an `actor_token` keeps its own rules and is unaffected. Every exchange records the derived token's parent, the subject token's `jti`, and not only `actor_token` child mints, so the recursive revocation of a token reaches the tokens derived from it by narrowing or by an exchange from another caller. §13.3 states the rule.
4. **The scope claim of a platform-admin impersonation bearer (SM-8, TOKX-I6).** Settled by the owner on 2026-11-20, choosing option (a): the impersonation bearer carries the caller's `scope` claim when the caller's claim is present, as §13.3 states. The option not chosen was refusing a caller whose claim is present and is not `tools:*`.
5. **The scope fields of the `token.exchanged` row (TOKX-I7).** No decision was needed: the row carries `requested_scope` and `granted_scope` as §16.7 names them.
6. **Placement.** The owner's request of 2026-11-20 places the change outside program v1, as a small change before M3 in the manner of 0161, 0162, and 0163. The change plans one step, because the lineage of decision 3 is one function and one specification sentence, and the other rules share the validator and its tests.
7. **Whether a rotation records the token it replaces as its parent (O1, SM-9).** Raised by the security review of 0164.S1 on 2026-11-20 (finding 1), because a holder of a leaked token that its owner rotated away from could revoke the owner's current token through the rotation edge. Settled by the owner on 2026-11-20 on the M3 readiness decision page, choosing option (a): a rotation starts a new lineage. The token the self-rotation grant issues records no parent, so revoking a token that a rotation replaced, such as a leaked one, does not revoke the owner's current tokens. A token derived from the replaced token before the rotation still records it as parent. Narrowing exchanges, lease issuance, exchanges by another caller, and child mints keep recording their parent. The options not chosen were (b), keeping the rotation edge and relying on the revocation-request rules of decision 9, and (c), keeping the step as built.
8. **Whether a child mint whose subject is another user's token must narrow (O2, SM-10).** Raised by the security review of 0164.S1 on 2026-11-20 (finding 2), because a caller holding another user's token could present it as `subject_token` with its own token as `actor_token` and receive a child with the other user's `sub` and full scope, outside the reach of the revocation of the other user's token. Settled by the owner on 2026-11-20, choosing option (a): an exchange with an `actor_token` requires the actor token's `sub` to equal the caller's `sub`, so a caller cannot present someone else's actor token, and when the subject token's `sub` differs from the actor token's, the request must name a scope strictly narrower than the subject's. A parent session minting a child for its own user is unaffected. The options not chosen were (b), recording a second lineage edge from the subject, and (c), keeping child minting as built.
9. **Who may send a recursive-revocation request, and whether its root may be revoked (SM-11, [TOKX-I9](../../issues/closed/TOKX/TOKX-I9-revocation-request-accepts-any-caller-and-a-revoked-root.md)).** Settled by the owner on 2026-11-20, choosing option (a): the Token Service accepts a revocation request, including its cascade, only from the token's own user (the caller's `sub` equals the subject token's) or from a caller holding an administrator role for the tenant, and refuses it when the caller's own token is revoked. Revoking a token that is already revoked still cascades to its descendants, so an owner finishes a cleanup with another live token. The administrator roles are `tenant-admin` and `platform-admin`, the roles §10.2 names, read from the caller token's `roles` claim as the other role checks read it.
