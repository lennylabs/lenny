# Decisions: 0169 A concurrency model of the token rules

## Open decisions

The model's results of 2026-11-23 raise these decisions. "Model results" in `change.md` states each counterexample, and `tests/formal/tokenconcurrency/README.md` gives the configuration that reaches it. Decisions 10 and 12 concern the locking design of change 0168, whose code lane waits on them (decision 2).

10. **An undetected wait in 0168's impersonation step (C8).** Under `TF`, two recursive-revocation requests in acme and globex whose callers are impersonation bearers started from tokens of each other's subtrees wait on each other with no end. alice in acme holds the `platform-admin` bearer A, and carol in globex holds G; carol impersonated alice in acme with the bearer Ia started from G, and alice impersonated carol in globex with the bearer Ig started from A. alice revokes A with Ia as caller while carol revokes G with Ig as caller. alice's outer transaction holds the share lock on Ia, and its nested transaction in globex waits for the update lock on Ig; carol's outer transaction holds the share lock on Ig, and its nested transaction in acme waits for the update lock on Ia. Each outer transaction waits for its nested one in the application, so Postgres sees two lock waits and no cycle, and neither request ends until its client gives up (gap G2 in the README). `I13Lock` has the same wait. `Main` has none, because its step's update runs in a separate transaction after the share lock is released, which is the ordering of TOKX-I20.
    - (a) Run the target-tenant updates of the step in the outer transaction, setting the target tenant's context with `SET LOCAL app.current_tenant` before each update and the request's tenant after it, and retry the step on `40P01` as `RevokeLineage` retries the cascade. Every wait is then a lock wait, the deadlock detector aborts one of the two requests, and its retry finds its caller revoked by the other request and is refused. A scratch copy of the model with this change holds C5, C8, and C9 in `nested_step` (93,083 distinct states). TF-2's statement that each target tenant runs in a nested transaction "as the second transaction of rule 1 (c) does", and the text of §4.2 that 0168's specification lane edits for nested transactions, change with it.
    - (b) Keep the nested transactions and set `lock_timeout` on each one. The request whose nested transaction times out answers `500` and revokes nothing in its tenant. Both requests can time out together, and the clients' retries can meet in the same wait again.
    - (c) Keep the nested transactions and accept the wait, stating in rule 7 that two such requests wait until their clients give up.
    - Recommendation: (a). It keeps the share lock across the step, which closes TOKX-I20, and makes the one cycle the design adds visible to the detector that already resolves the cascade's mutual revocations.
11. **TOKX-I13 under 0168's design (C1 and C7).** A derivation that commits while a revocation's statement runs escapes the revocation under `Main` and under `TF`. For a recursive revocation, a rotation, a narrowing, a child mint, or a playground mint from a token of the subtree reads its sources before the cascade's update reaches them and commits after the cascade's snapshot (the worked example in `change.md`, and the narrowing in `cascade_issuance`). For a full revoke of alice, TF-4's user-row read closes the window only for a mint on `/v1/oauth/token` that presents a token carrying alice's `sub`: a playground mint from alice's A, and bob's narrowing of K, a child of A that carries bob's `sub`, each commit after step 5 and stay live. `I13Lock` closes C1 and C7 in every configuration and adds no wait other than the one of decision 10.
    - (a) Take the per-tenant audit advisory lock in `revokeLineageOnce` and `RevokeBySubject` before the statement that reads their snapshot, as TOKX-I13 suggests, in change 0168 or in a change of its own, and decide the bound of TOKX-I10 with it, because the cascade then blocks the tenant's issuances for its duration and an issuance waits for the lock under the 5-second acquisition timeout of §11.7.
    - (b) Keep the window, and state in §13.3 and §11.4 that a derivation committed while a revocation runs escapes it, with the outcomes above.
    - (c) Extend TF-4's user-row read to the playground mint. This closes the full revoke's window for a token carrying the user's `sub`, and leaves the recursive revocation's window and the descendants that carry another user's `sub` open.
    - Recommendation: (a). It is the only option the model shows closing both properties, and the owner decides TOKX-I13 with TOKX-I10 as that issue states.
12. **A bearer row without a start record (C6).** Under `TF`, when the first transaction of rule 1 (c) fails at `COMMIT` after its nested transaction committed the bearer row, the impersonation start answers with an error and returns no bearer, the bearer row stays live, and its start record never commits. alice starts an impersonation of carol in globex from her bearer A, the bearer row N2 commits in globex, and the commit of alice's transaction in acme fails; alice then revokes A with her token D, and the step finds no start record naming globex, so N2 stays live after the cascade commits. Under `Main` the step's update under `__all__` follows the impersonator link and ends N2. No client holds N2, because the signed bearer is discarded, but the `GET /v1/admin/impersonation` listing shows it as an active session until its `exp`, a `DELETE`, or rule 6 ends it.
    - (a) Accept, and state in rule 1 (c) that a failed first transaction can leave a bearer row that no client holds, that the impersonation step does not reach, and that the listing shows until it ends.
    - (b) Commit the start record before the bearer row, in a transaction of its own in the administrator's tenant before the first transaction of rule 1 (c), so every committed bearer row has a committed start record; a start record whose bearer row never commits makes the step update no row.
    - (c) On a failure of the first transaction after the nested one committed, end the bearer row with `EndImpersonation`, which can itself fail.
    - Recommendation: (a). The row grants nothing to anyone, and (b) moves the start record outside the user-row fence of rule 1 (c), which the model has not checked.
13. **The rule 7 window and the unlocked caller check (C6, gap G3).** `checkCaller` reads the caller's bearer without a lock, so an impersonation whose caller check runs before a cascade commits and whose record commits after it is linked to a revoked bearer and stays live, under both `Main` and `TF`. Rule 7 says that "an impersonation started from a token of the subtree after the impersonation step and before the cascade commits" escapes the request, which does not say whether an impersonation that commits after the cascade is in that window. The model reads "started" as the caller check.
    - (a) State in rule 7 that the window includes an impersonation whose caller check precedes the cascade's commit and whose record commits after it.
    - (b) Read the caller bearer's row under a share lock in the first transaction of rule 1 (c), which conflicts with the cascade's update of that row, so an impersonation either commits before the cascade's update reaches the bearer or reads it revoked; the window then ends when the cascade commits.
    - Recommendation: (b). It ends the window at the cascade's commit, as the text reads. The model has not checked it, and the change that carries it reruns the `impersonation` and `nested_start` configurations.
14. **The validation fallback read (C4, gap G1).** §13.3 **Authoritative durability for revocation.** says that correctness survives a lost event "because every validation can fall back to `issued_tokens.revoked_at`", and **Token rotation and revocation — no grace period.** describes a read of `revoked_at` on a cache miss for a recently issued token. The gateway's auth middleware reads only the in-memory cache and its 90-second staleness gate, so a replica accepts a revoked token until the event arrives or the next 30-second rehydration runs. The model describes the code, and C4 holds for it.
    - (a) File an issue under TOKX for the missing read, and leave its design to a later change.
    - (b) Change §13.3 to state the guarantee the code gives: the cache, the 30-second rehydration, and the 90-second staleness gate.
    - Recommendation: (a). The text states the read as the reason correctness survives a lost event, so the code is the side that differs.

## Decisions

1. **Build the TLA+ model now, as its own change (owner, 2026-11-23).** The owner decided on 2026-11-23 to build the TLA+ model of the concurrent orderings of the token rules now, as this change. This amends decision 14 of change 0165 (a formal model of the token rules), under which the model stayed a candidate until step 0165.S2 was done.
2. **The model checks 0168's locking design before its code lane (owner, 2026-11-23).** The owner decided on 2026-11-23 that the model checks the locking design of change 0168 (token rule fixes from the property tests), as its decisions 1, 2, 5, and 6 and its sections TF-1 to TF-5 state it, before the code lane of 0168.S1 is written. The specification lane of 0168.S1 may proceed in parallel. The counterexamples the model finds in that design are reported to the owner before the code lane starts.

The owner answered FM-19 to FM-25 on the M3 readiness decision page on 2026-11-23, each with option (a). The answers settle open decisions 3 to 9 of revision 1.

3. **PlusCal for the request processes (FM-19).** The request processes are written in PlusCal, with one label per statement or lock step, and Postgres, the deadlock detector, and the event bus are TLA+ operators and actions in the same module. The translator ships in `tla2tools.jar` (`pcal.trans`), the translation is committed in the module between the translator's markers, and the runner checks that it is current. Owner's answer to FM-19 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), the whole model in plain TLA+ as one next-state relation.
4. **TLC `v1.7.4` is pinned (FM-20).** The runner uses `tla2tools.jar` of the tlaplus release `v1.7.4`, downloaded from `https://github.com/tlaplus/tlaplus/releases/download/v1.7.4/tla2tools.jar`, with SHA-256 `936a262061c914694dfd669a543be24573c45d5aa0ff20a8b96b23d01e050e88`, and stops when the jar's SHA-256 differs. Owner's answer to FM-20 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), the pre-release `v1.8.0`.
5. **Directed configurations and one free configuration (FM-21).** The directed configurations run at 2 tenants, 2 users per tenant, 2 replicas, up to 3 request processes, up to 6 tokens, 1 abort per behavior, and the 3 cascade attempts the code makes, one configuration per ordering of "Scope" in `change.md`. One free configuration runs at 2 request processes and 5 tokens, in which each process chooses any operation. A configuration that does not finish within 12 hours is stopped, and the bound it reached is recorded. Owner's answer to FM-21 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The options not chosen were (b), free configurations only, and (c), directed configurations at 3 tenants as well.
6. **No tier of `lenny-test` runs the model (FM-22).** The model is a design check run by hand with `tests/formal/tokenconcurrency/run.sh`, which runs TLC in an `eclipse-temurin` container with `--network none`, the model and the jar mounted read-only, and the jar's SHA-256 checked, as `tests/formal/tokens/run.sh` runs the Alloy model. The change that edits a rule the model describes reruns it. Owner's answer to FM-22 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The options not chosen were (b), the static tier running the translator and the parser only, and (c), the release gate running the directed configurations.
7. **The model checks the `I13Lock` variant (FM-23).** Step 0169.S2 adds the variant `I13Lock`, which is `TF` with the per-tenant audit advisory lock taken by the cascade and by `RevokeBySubject` before the statement that reads their snapshot, as TOKX-I13 suggests, and checks C1, C5, C7, and C8 under it. Owner's answer to FM-23 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), checking `Main` and `TF` only.
8. **Steps 0169.S1 and 0169.S2 share one branch and one digest (FM-24).** Owner's answer to FM-24 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), separate branches with 0169.S1 merged first.
9. **No specification text (FM-25).** The properties stay in `change.md` and in the model's README, and each change that fixes a counterexample states in §13.3 the ordering it settles. Owner's answer to FM-25 on the M3 readiness decision page, 2026-11-23. The option not chosen was (b), a specification lane on 0169.S2's branch stating the confirmed properties in §13.3.
